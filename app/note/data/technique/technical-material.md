# Technical Material

> **Technical Material** is all intentionally transformed matter: every substance a purposeful
> agent has worked — smelted, fired, sawn, pulped, refined, synthesized — into feedstocks,
> objects, and products. The counterpart of
> [Natural Material](note.html?n=nature/natural-material.md) (matter as found); the two meet
> at feedstocks, where the formation is natural and the object technical. Children:
> [Technical Biogenic Material](note.html?n=technique/technical-biogenic-material.md) (from
> living feedstocks) and
> [Technical Abiogenic Material](note.html?n=technique/technical-abiogenic-material.md) (from
> mineral feedstocks). Worked per
> [How to decompose any technical instance?](note.html?n=meta/philosophia-artium-technicarum-et-operis.md).

## Formulation

### What technical element type does this technical instance belong to?

**Technical Material belongs to the `Technical Resource` technical element type — inputs
required and consumed by technical activity (feedstocks, substrates, consumables).** The two
children scope genuine instance families under the same type (same-type scoping per the
No-repetition exception); full expansion lives in the child notes, not here.

### What is this technical instance?

> Matter under intention: natural substances taken up by technique — lumber from forest,
> steel from ore, polymer from petroleum — and transformed into resources that enable action
> and further production. The cut includes the worked substance, its feedstock lineage, the
> mechanisms that transformed it, and the properties, standards, and constraints that govern
> its use; it excludes the untransformed formation (natural reading, one tree per reading)
> and the firms and markets priced around it (social reading).

## QA

### Why do petroleum-derived polymers classify at Technical Material?

> Because forcing them into either origin family is false precision. Their feedstock is
> paleo-living petroleum — a transformed [Natural Biogenic](note.html?n=nature/biogenic-material.md)
> substance — which reads biogenic; yet refining, cracking, and polymerization erase every
> origin character the biogenic family is individuated by, which reads abiogenic. When
> origin is ambiguous it does not make sense to classify either way, so the instance rises
> to the most abstract classification — Technical Material — with `readable as biogenic …`
> and `readable as abiogenic …` prose, in the spirit of the multi-root forest rule
> ([ambiguity-resolution](note.html?n=meta/ambiguity-resolution.md)).
