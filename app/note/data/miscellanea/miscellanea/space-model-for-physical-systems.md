# Space Model for Physical Systems

Good question! The physics model I use generally assumes a **pseudo-Riemannian manifold** as the underlying mathematical structure of spacetime, specifically a **four-dimensional Lorentzian manifold** as described by General Relativity (GR).

However, the choice of the space model depends on the physical theory being used:

1. **Newtonian Mechanics:**
    - Assumes a **three-dimensional Euclidean space** () with an independent absolute time ().

        $\mathbb{R}^3$

        $\mathbb{R}$

    - Space is flat and homogeneous, with a fixed Cartesian coordinate system.
2. **Special Relativity (SR):**
    - Uses **Minkowski spacetime** (), a four-dimensional flat spacetime with a metric signature (+,−,−,−).

        R1,3\mathbb{R}^{1,3}

    - There is no absolute time, and space and time are linked through the Lorentz transformations.
3. **General Relativity (GR):**
    - Uses a **four-dimensional curved Lorentzian manifold** with a metric  that evolves dynamically.

        gμνg_{\mu\nu}

    - The curvature of space is dictated by the presence of mass-energy via Einstein's field equations.
4. **Quantum Mechanics (QM):**
    - The configuration space of a quantum system can be very abstract.
    - For a single particle, it's often **R3\mathbb{R}^3** (position space) or **Hilbert space** () in more general cases.

        H\mathcal{H}

    - Quantum fields live in **Fock space**, and quantum gravity candidates (like loop quantum gravity or string theory) have different underlying spaces.
5. **Cosmology (FRW Models):**
    - Uses a **Friedmann-Lemaître-Robertson-Walker (FLRW) metric**, which describes an expanding or contracting universe.
    - Space can have **positive curvature (spherical), zero curvature (flat), or negative curvature (hyperbolic)** depending on the density of the universe.
