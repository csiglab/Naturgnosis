# Financial Sector

> Financial Sector models the organized provision of money, credit, risk transfer, and settlement — banks, markets, insurers, infrastructures, and their auxiliaries — as a segment of reality; intervention on it is not typed here as a technical practice.

## Formulation

### What epistemic target does this note model?

The financial sector as a reality segment: the population of chartered, licensed, and market-recognized entities that take deposits, extend credit, underwrite risk, operate markets and infrastructures, manage assets, move payments, and supply the information those activities run on — parsed here into twelve sectors (banking, credit & finance, capital markets, securities services, asset & wealth management, insurance, pensions & retirement, payments, financial market infrastructure, financial information & support, financial technology, monetary & public finance institutions), each cut into categories and subsectors, in the pattern of an epistemic domain such as `Human Social Network` — a portion of social reality that epistemic practice targets and models. The instrumental side (how positions are taken, risks priced, and obligations settled by intervention) lives in the companion technical note.

### What claims and models does financial-sector modeling make about it?

A sector taxonomy as classification model: twelve mutually intelligible sectors, each with numbered categories (e.g. `1.1` Deposit-Taking Banking, `3.3` Derivatives Markets) and leaf subsectors (e.g. Retail Banking, Swaps, Reinsurance). Each claim carries its scope conditions: jurisdiction and charter regime (what counts as a bank differs by supervisor), boundary cases (fintech lenders that are and are not deposit-takers, digital-asset venues that are and are not exchanges), and time (the taxonomy is a snapshot — categories such as fintech payments did not exist a generation ago).

### How is validity evaluated?

Through fit against external registries and perimeters (statistical classifications, supervisory entity lists, market membership rolls), through straddle tests (entities whose activity spans sectors force a boundary ruling, not a taxonomy failure), and through revision pressure (new instruments and charters must land somewhere without breaking the numbering). A claim is accepted only with its boundary: which entities, under which regime, at which date.
