# Materials Genome Initiative

> The Materials Genome Initiative (MGI) is the bounded scope of coordinated American activity that reorders how advanced materials are discovered, developed, and deployed: federal agencies funding platforms, national laboratories and universities running them, industry and professional societies supplying participants and legitimacy, and a shared warrant that materials can be found twice as fast at a fraction of the cost.

> It is a scope rather than a body. The Initiative owns no laboratories, employs no staff, and has no membership; it is the composite in which those units appear as a program portfolio, held together by an appropriated mandate, a coordinating apparatus, and an institutional data-governance regime.

> This note decomposes the coordination, not the artifacts, per the guide at [Philosophia Socialium et Operis](note.html?n=meta/philosophia-socialium-et-operis.md). The engineering of the platforms and the materials themselves belong to [Physical Material Technology](note.html?n=technique/systems/physical-material-technology.md); the descriptor vocabularies and reference databases belong to the epistemic decomposition.

## Formulation

### What social element type does this social instance belong to?

**The Materials Genome Initiative belongs to the `Social Compound` social element type — the generic composite that the `(root) := <<Social Element>>` binding embodies for collective scopes.**

```text
Social Compound
└── Materials Genome Initiative (materials-discovery coordination scope)
```

It is a compound, not an `Institution`. An institution is a stabilized configuration of roles and rules that persists by itself; the Initiative has no such persistence — it is a portfolio of separately chartered programs, each with its own host, budget line, and lifecycle, and the Initiative exists only as their coordination. What is institutional here is nested one level down: the data-governance regime that binds depositors, curators, and standards bodies into a rule of metadata and deposit. That regime is decomposed below, typed as an `Institution` beneath the bound root; the Initiative itself is the scope that contains it.

The Initiative is also readable as a `Process / Event` — the funded trajectory from the 2011 announcement through the 2012 appropriations, the 2014 Strategic Plan, and the 2016 five-year review. That reading is kept as prose: a trajectory is not what the Initiative is, only how it ran. No row here carries two types.

### What is this social instance?

> The Materials Genome Initiative is a materials-discovery coordination scope: agencies, laboratories, universities, and firms jointly funding and running open computational and experimental platforms, under a data-governance regime of shared metadata, standards, and deposit obligation, to halve the time from a material's discovery to its deployment.

**Layer.** The apparatus is **Ontic** — appropriations, agencies, platforms, and participants exist extra-mentally. The databases and descriptor vocabularies they operate are **Noetic** instruments. The Initiative is *bound* by neither alone but by a **Synontic** stratum carried by its representation and its warrant: the genome metaphor is the group's image to itself, and the twice-as-fast claim is a shared anticipation that coordinates behavior toward it. An Initiative whose apparatus vanished but whose warrant survived would still recruit participants — that is the layer doing the work, and it is why the Initiative is decomposed here at all rather than left as a budget line in a technicarum note.

**Facet.** Assigned by dominant causal mechanism, not by location: MGI's work is reorganized by the *institutional-technical* mechanism of mandated shared data infrastructure, which is what makes the coordination norm-governed rather than market-coordinated. Firms are not a facet here; they are participants.

**Boundary.** This note covers the coordination: mandate, agencies, programs, participants, the governance regime, resources, and the run of the initiative. It does not decompose the platforms' engineering, the data schemas, or the materials themselves; those are referenced as instances, not expanded.

Lineage: the 2009–2011 computational-materials argument (density-functional theory reaching predictive accuracy at scale) → the June 2011 presidential announcement and the National Science and Technology Council white paper → FY2012 appropriations across four agencies → the 2014 Strategic Plan and its four challenges → the 2016 five-year review, past which the Initiative persists as standing programs rather than a named program.

### What is the recursive instance decomposition of this social instance?

> Boundary: a middle-depth decomposition of one coordination scope — eight branches, each worked to instance leaves. The root plus its direct constituents is not the claim here; the claim is that the coordination is legible as mandate, units, programs, a governance regime, resources, a run, and relations, each with instances underneath.
>
> Typing reads from the path. Rows typed `Interaction Unit` sit on the head of the agentic spine; the curator row carries the full spine `Interaction Unit -> Agency -> Social Action`, with no level skipped. The governance branch enters at `Institution` and follows the normative spine `Institution -> Social Role -> Norm / Regulation -> Right / Obligation` in order. Every other branch type is a flat facet type landing directly under the bound root. Bare type rows group and never terminate a branch; every leaf is a `**bold**` social instance.
>
> Stopping rule: a row is terminal when it names a real agency, program, unit, role, rule, resource, event, relation, or representation. No exemplars are carried — every row is an instance, not a sample.
