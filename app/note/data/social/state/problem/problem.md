# Problem

> A **problem** is the normative gap between the current state of affairs and a desired
> state, as apprehended by an agent capable of acting on it. A **policy problem** is a
> problem taken up as warranting a policy course: it names what the authorized doing
> shall head toward, and decomposes into mandate, instrument, goal, target, agency,
> rule, and review.

> Problematization formulates the problem; [Policy](note.html?n=social/state/action/guidance/policy.md)
> answers it with a course; the [Dimension](note.html?n=social/state/problem/dimension/domain.md)
> index scopes in which domain of social reality it sits. See
> [Problematization](note.html?n=epistemica/problematization.md) for the process,
> [Problem](note.html?n=social/problem.md) for the general element (problem as intentional
> structure), and
> [The Collective Action Problem](note.html?n=general/collective-action-problem.md) for
> the coordination failure that keeps recognized problems unanswered.

## What is a policy problem?

> A policy problem is a condition claimed to require collective doing under authority:
> it states a gap (what is versus what ought to be), attributes it to alterable
> arrangements rather than fate, and addresses an agency mandated to close it. Without
> the mandate there is grievance or analysis, not yet a policy problem.

## How do we know we have a policy problem?

> Four tests, all must hold: (1) a gap is stated between conditions and a norm;
> (2) the gap is attributed to arrangements agents can alter; (3) an agency exists, or
> can be constituted, with mandate over those arrangements; (4) instruments exist, or
> can be built, that plausibly close the gap. Failing (2) leaves fate; failing (3)
> leaves advocacy; failing (4) leaves research.

## When does a problem become policy-actionable?

> Through problematization to agenda to mandate: conditions are apprehended, the gap
> is formulated and evidenced, the formulation wins collective attention (agenda),
> an authority adopts it as its course (mandate), and instruments are chosen and
> tasked. Actionability is therefore not a property of conditions but of the
> chain linking them to a mandated agency with instruments.

## Dimensions

> Every policy problem sits in a domain, at a scale, with a tractability and a
> mandate-holder: which [Domain](note.html?n=social/state/problem/dimension/domain.md)
> of social reality it belongs to (production, infrastructure, human capital, monetary,
> governance, research, international, urban, cognition, welfare, the state); how
> severe and wide it runs; whether instruments exist that can close it; and whose
> mandate binds the response. Scope all four before prescribing the course.
