# India Five-Year Plan

> The India Five-Year Plan is the strategic planning instrument by which the Republic of India arranged its mixed-economy development: the Planning Commission drafted, the National Development Council endorsed, Parliament debated and adopted, and states mirrored — public-sector outlays, monitorable targets, and mid-term appraisals composed into one five-year arrangement, renewed from 1951 until the twelfth round closed the cycle in 2017.

> It is an instrument rather than a document. No single text governed; the arrangement existed only as the composed sequence of drafts, endorsements, outlays, execution, and appraisal, each round inheriting the apparatus of the last. What persisted across rounds was the planning machinery, not the targets.

> This note decomposes the instrument, not the economics, per the guide at [Philosophia Socialium et Operis](note.html?n=meta/philosophia-socialium-et-operis.md). The targets' substance — outlays, growth rates, sectoral shares — belongs to the policy notes; the Harrod-Domar and input-output models warranting them belong to the epistemic decomposition.

## Formulation

### What social element type does this social instance belong to?

**The India Five-Year Plan belongs to the `Strategic Planning Instrument` social element type (Action category, `Action Guidance` branch via `Framework → Planning Framework`, Normative layer)** — a state-scale instrument binding commissions, cycles, and targets into one standing arrangement. It is the mixed-economy form of the instrument: outlays directing the public sector while signaling the private, backed by persuasion and finance rather than by command quotas.

```text
Strategic Planning Instrument
└── India Five-Year Plan (mixed-economy arrangement machinery, 1951–2017)
```

The instrument is also readable as a `Policy` — each round authorized courses that oriented subsequent decisions across the economy. That reading is kept as prose: authorization is what the arrangement carried, not what it is. No row here carries two types.

### What is this social instance?

> The India Five-Year Plan is a strategic planning instrument: a five-year composition of public-sector outlays and monitorable targets, drafted by the planning commission, endorsed by the federal council, adopted by the legislature, executed through mirrored state plans, and judged at mid-term appraisal — the machinery by which one polity arranged its development from independence to the commission's replacement in 2015.

**Layer.** The apparatus is **Ontic** — commissions, councils, documents, and outlays exist extra-mentally. The self-reliance the cycle moved is a **Noetic** instrument. The arrangement is *bound* by a **Synontic** stratum carried by its anticipation: the shared expectation that the next five years are already composed toward self-reliance, which coordinated ministries, states, and firms toward the composed future before any outlay applied.

**Facet.** Assigned by dominant causal mechanism: the cycle's work is reorganized by the *planning-federal* mechanism of composed outlays allocated through Gadgil-formula transfers and licenses, which is what makes the arrangement plan-guided rather than market-coordinated or command-allocated.

**Boundary.** This note covers the instrument: mandate, commission apparatus, endorsement, outlays, mirrored execution, appraisal, and the twelve composed rounds with their two plan holidays. It does not decompose the outlays' economic substance or the models warranting them; those are referenced, not expanded.

Lineage: the Bombay Plan (1944) → the Planning Commission founded (1950) → the First Plan (1951–1956) → the Mahalanobis heavy-industry turn (Second Plan) → wars, drought, and the 1966–1969 plan holiday → Garibi Hatao (Fourth Plan) → the 1990–1992 holiday and liberalization → the rights-based 11th round → the 12th round (2012–2017), past which NITI Aayog replaces the Commission (2015) and the cycle closes.
