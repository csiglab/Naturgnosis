# Soviet Five-Year Plan

> The Soviet Five-Year Plan is the strategic planning instrument by which the Union of Soviet Socialist Republics arranged its command economy: the Party Congress issued control figures, Gosplan balanced them into material allocations, the Supreme Soviet ratified the five-year law, and ministries and enterprises executed under fulfillment accounting — binding output quotas, counter-plan pledges, and annual breakdowns composed into one five-year arrangement, renewed from 1928 until the Union dissolved mid-cycle in 1991.

> It is an instrument rather than a document. No single text governed; the arrangement existed only as the composed sequence of directives, balances, laws, execution, and fulfillment accounting, each round inheriting the apparatus of the last. What persisted across rounds was the machinery of allocation, not the quotas.

> This note decomposes the instrument, not the economics, per the guide at [Philosophia Socialium et Operis](note.html?n=meta/philosophia-socialium-et-operis.md). The quotas' substance — steel, coal, machinery, grain — belongs to the policy notes; the input-output models warranting the balances belong to the epistemic decomposition.

## Formulation

### What social element type does this social instance belong to?

**The Soviet Five-Year Plan belongs to the `Strategic Planning Instrument` social element type (Action category, `Action Guidance` branch via `Framework → Planning Framework`, Normative layer)** — a state-scale instrument binding commissions, cycles, and targets into one standing arrangement. It is the command form of the instrument: quotas backed by the Party's monopoly rather than by market signals.

```text
Strategic Planning Instrument
└── Soviet Five-Year Plan (command allocation machinery, 1928–1991)
```

The instrument is also readable as a `Policy` — each round authorized courses that oriented every subsequent decision in the economy. That reading is kept as prose: authorization is what the arrangement carried, not what it is. No row here carries two types.

### What is this social instance?

> The Soviet Five-Year Plan is a strategic planning instrument: a five-year composition of binding output quotas and above-plan pledges, drafted by the state planning committee, directed by the Party, ratified as law, executed through ministerial hierarchies, and judged in fulfillment accounting — the machinery by which one polity allocated its economy without markets for over sixty years.

**Layer.** The apparatus is **Ontic** — committees, ministries, enterprises, balances, and quotas exist extra-mentally. The pledges the cycle moved are **Noetic** instruments. The arrangement is *bound* by a **Synontic** stratum carried by its anticipation: the shared expectation that fulfillment equals legitimacy, which coordinated managers, workers, and planners toward the composed quotas before any sanction applied.

**Facet.** Assigned by dominant causal mechanism: the cycle's work is reorganized by the *command-administrative* mechanism of material balances allocated down the ministerial hierarchy, which is what makes the arrangement plan-commanded rather than market-coordinated.

**Boundary.** This note covers the instrument: mandate, Gosplan apparatus, ratification, quotas, ministerial execution, fulfillment review, and the composed rounds. It does not decompose the quotas' economic substance or the balancing models; those are referenced, not expanded.

Lineage: GOELRO electrification plan (1920) → Gosplan founded (1921) → the First Five-Year Plan (1928–1932) → the war rupture → postwar restoration → the sovnarkhoz decentralization (1957) and its reversal → the Kosygin reform attempt (1965) → perestroika acceleration (1986) → the uncompleted 13th round and dissolution (1991).
