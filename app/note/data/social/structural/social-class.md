# Social Class

> A **Social Class** is an aggregate collective defined by shared position in relations of production and access to resources: owners and workers, rentiers and debtors, managers and managed — a plurality bound not by charter or kinship but by occupying the same side of an economic divide.

## Formulation

### What is this social instance?

> A social class fixes *who stands where in the economy*: the shared position in production and property that makes strangers into counterparts — allies in bargaining, rivals in distribution, and, when mobilized, actors in history. Position first, consciousness after, organization last.

> **Note:** Concepts can be constructed through definitions that identify particular cuts of reality, potentially generating an indefinite number of possible concepts. However, definability does not entail analytical value: a concept is useful only insofar as it identifies meaningful structures, distinctions, or regularities that help us understand or explain reality. The concept of `social class`, in particular, risks being too coarse-grained to provide much analytical value on its own, requiring extensive additional description of economic and social reality to become useful. This creates opportunities for conceptual confusion, arbitrary classifications, and overinference through the application of deductive rules without adequately examining the empirical conditions under which their conclusions hold. It also encourages the reification of analytical abstractions, treating them as if they were concrete structures of reality rather than conceptual constructions whose validity and explanatory power depend on how well they correspond to the phenomena being analysed.

### What type of social element does this instance belong to?

**Social Class belongs to `Social Compound` as its primary kind** — an aggregate collective defined by shared structural position; see [Collective](note.html?n=social/collective/collective.md). Its layer is **Ontic** (positions in production exist extra-mentally) with **Synontic** aspects (class consciousness and identity, where recognition constitutes the class *for itself*). It is typed under [Actor](note.html?n=social/actor/actor.md), the root hub of the actor typology.

Readable secondarily as an **Interaction Unit** where mobilized (strikes, parties, struggle — the class acting in history) and as carrying **Status** (class honor, prestige, lifestyle deference). Those readings are prose, not second trees; the tree below is typed once.

### Is the type of this `social element instance` unambiguous?

> Yes, as a compound kind: the positional definition (shared place in production and property) is unambiguous, while the mobilized-agency and status-honor aspects are secondary readings of the same aggregate, not rival roots — no second tree is required.

### Which facet does this element belong to, if applicable?

> Economic–structural first (positional differentiation by access to resources); political where mobilized around struggle, parties, and programs.

## QA

### Is class primarily economic?

> As a kind, yes: position in production and property defines it. But no class stays merely economic — mobilization makes it political (parties, programs, struggle), and recognition makes it status (honor, prestige, lifestyle). The economic definition anchors; the political and status aspects complete.

### Class versus stratum?

> A stratum is a gradational layer — higher/lower on some scale. A class is a relational position — defined by its place *against* other positions in production. Strata rank; classes oppose. Type gradational layers as strata, relational positions as classes, and never merge them into one row.
