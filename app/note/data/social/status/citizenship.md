# Citizenship

> **Citizenship** is membership standing in a polity: a relationship between an individual and a state to which the individual owes allegiance and in turn is entitled to its protection (T. H. Marshall) — a Status realized as a bundle of rights and obligations and occupied through the Citizen member role.

## Formulation

### What is this social instance?

> Citizenship fixes *who belongs to the polity and on what terms*: the conferring collective, the criterion of admission (birth, descent, naturalization), the rights extended, the duties exacted, and the documents and registers in which the standing is inscribed. A passport is paper; citizenship is what the paper counts as.

### What type of social element does this instance belong to?

**Citizenship belongs to `Status` as its primary kind** — an attributive standing recognized by a collective, here the polity; see [Status](note.html?n=social/status/status.md). Its layer is **Synontic** (conferred standing, existing through collective recognition) with **Ontic** anchors (registers, documents, territory). It is typed under [Actor](note.html?n=social/actor/actor.md), the root hub of the actor typology.

Readable secondarily as **Normative** (the rights–obligations bundle: vote, protection, duties, allegiance) and as a **Social Role** (the Citizen member role through which the standing is occupied). Those readings are prose, not second trees; the tree below is typed once.

### Is the type of this `social element instance` unambiguous?

> Yes. Citizenship is unambiguously a Status kind (membership standing in a polity); the rights-bundle and Citizen-role aspects are secondary readings of the same standing, not rival roots — no second tree is required.

### Which facet does this element belong to, if applicable?

> Legal–political: codified rights plus authority membership.

## QA

### Citizenship versus nationality?

> Citizenship is the legal standing — rights, duties, documents. Nationality-as-identity is belonging felt and recognized — language, memory, attachment. The two coincide often and diverge often: one can hold the passport without the belonging, and the belonging without the passport. Type the first as Status, the second as Collective Identity, and never merge them into one row.

### Can citizenship be lost?

> Yes, and the ways map the constituents: renunciation dissolves the tie by declaration, denaturalization revokes it by authority, and state succession strands it when the conferring polity itself ceases. Loss without replacement is statelessness — standing with no collective to recognize it.
