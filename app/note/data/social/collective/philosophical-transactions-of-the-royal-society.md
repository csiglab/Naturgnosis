# Philosophical Transactions of the Royal Society

> The first scientific journal: Henry Oldenburg's periodical for the Royal Society, first number published in London in 1665 — the compound of editors, contributors, readers, and publication rules through which priority-of-publication and the scientific paper were made into an institution.

## Formulation

### What is this social instance?

> A scholarly journal fixes *what hangs together so that a finding can be told first, and told as settled*: the editorial office that solicits and selects, the contributors who submit, the readers for whom circulation counts, the rules that make printing an announcement, and the serial run that makes the announcement periodic. *Philosophical Transactions* is the founding instance of that arrangement — Oldenburg editing, Fellows contributing, the Royal Society sponsoring, and a number appearing every few weeks where before there was correspondence and priority dispute.

### What type of social element does this instance belong to?

**Philosophical Transactions belongs to the `Social Compound` social element type (Ontic category), as an instance of the `Scholarly Journal` kind** — a serial, refereed compound constituted of contributors, editors, and readers held together by a publication regime. Its layer is **Ontic**: the office, the serial run, and the rules in force exist extra-mentally. Its facet is **Scientific**: the dominant causal mechanism is knowledge production and peer evaluation, not market exchange or political authority.

It is not an `Interaction Unit`: the journal has no decision architecture of its own — it intends and selects only through its editor, contributors, and readers, so the actors are decomposed, never the masthead. It is not an `Institution` as such: the priority rule it instituted is a *component* of the compound (and is stabilized by it), not the whole. And it is not a `Social Action`: a single number is a bounded occurrence, while the serial run is the compound's structure.

### Is the type of this `social element instance` ambiguous?

Yes, in three readings — grown one tree per reading, never two types on one row:

- **`Social Compound` (the tree below)** — the journal as the organized compound of units, rules, and a serial run. Typed once here.
- **`Synontic`** — the masthead and title as a shared representation: what the republic of letters collectively recognizes as conferring priority and standing. Prose reading, not a second root in this note.
- **`Ontic` → `Activity`** — the publication cycle as the coarse, recurring *doing* soliciting, refereeing, editing, printing, and distributing. Prose reading; recurrence is dynamics (a cycle), not a type.

The compound reading is primary because it is the one the other two presuppose: recognition attaches to a standing compound, and the cycle is the compound's course of action.

### Which facet does this element belong to, if applicable?

**Scientific** — knowledge production, research, peer evaluation, formalized inquiry. Secondary touch: **Normative** (the priority rule it institutes) and **Literature** (the serial as textual transmission), neither of which is the dominant mechanism.

### What is a critical conceptual evaluation of this `social element instance`?

Two load-bearing caveats.

**The reference table has a gap here.** The Activity reading and the guide's own QA both hold that *every Activity decomposes into the granular actions it bundles*, yet the Permitted Link Forms table licenses no link from an `Activity` to the `Social Action`s it bundles — only `Social Compound (→ Part/Component/Association)` and `Interaction Unit (→ Participation) Activity`. The tree therefore stops at `Publication Cycle Activity` rather than inventing a permitted link; the gap is recorded for table extension, per the rule on unlicensed relationships.

**Do not anachronize the institution.** The 1665 number instituted priority of publication and serial regularity; formal peer refereeing arrived later. Naming the component "Priority-and-Refereeing" describes the modern compound — a reader must not attribute modern referee practice to Oldenburg's first numbers. Note also what the tree deliberately excludes: the printed issues and the papers are technical and epistemic artifacts handled by technique/production and Research Space respectively, reachable by association only — a social decomposition is about the coordination, not the artifacts that carry it.
