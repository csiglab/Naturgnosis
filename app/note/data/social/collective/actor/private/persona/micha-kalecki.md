# Michał Kalecki


> 

## Main Ideas

| **Main Idea** | **Description** |
| --- | --- |
| Effective Demand | Kalecki emphasized the role of effective demand in determining output and employment levels, similar to Keynes. |
| Income Distribution | He highlighted the impact of income distribution between capitalists and workers on consumption, investment, and economic dynamics. |
| Business Cycle Theory | Kalecki developed a theory of business cycles based on the interaction between investment, profits, and changes in income distribution. |
| Degree of Monopoly | Kalecki introduced the concept of the degree of monopoly, describing how firms with market power influence prices and distribution. |
| Fiscal Policy | He argued that government fiscal policy, including public investment and consumption, plays a crucial role in stabilizing the economy. |
| Investment Decision-Making | Kalecki analyzed the determinants of investment, emphasizing the role of profits and expectations in investment decisions. |
| Political Business Cycle | He proposed that political factors, such as elections and government policies, can influence economic cycles and business conditions. |
| Class Conflict | Kalecki integrated class conflict into his analysis, recognizing the differing interests of capitalists and workers in economic outcomes. |
| Critique of Say's Law | He challenged Say's Law by arguing that supply does not automatically create its own demand, highlighting the importance of aggregate demand. |
| Imperfect Competition | Kalecki's work incorporated the reality of imperfect competition in markets, contrasting with the traditional assumption of perfect competition. |

## 🏗️ I. **Macroeconomic Dynamics & Business Cycle Theory**

### 🔁 **Business Cycle Model**

- Core idea: **Investment drives the cycle**, not autonomous shocks.
- Key components:
    - **Investment is volatile** due to expectations.
    - **Profits = Investment + Capitalists' Consumption**.
    - Lagged adjustments in output and profits lead to **cyclical fluctuations**.
    - Incorporates **multiplier-accelerator dynamics**.
- Technical structure: nonlinear difference equations with delayed investment response.

### 📉 **Crisis Theory**

- Crises are not anomalies—they are **structurally embedded**.
- Causes include:
    - Declining investment.
    - Falling profit expectations.
    - Insufficient effective demand.
- Recurrent crises are tied to **capitalist control over investment** and underconsumption tendencies.

## 📊 II. **Distribution & Income Theory**

### 💸 **Profit Equation**

One of his most famous insights:

> Profits = Investment + Capitalists’ Consumption + Government Deficit + Net Exports − Workers’ Saving
> 
- Workers tend to **spend all their wages** (MPC ≈ 1).
- Capitalists save, so **only their spending drives profits**.
- Emphasizes a **circular flow of income** in a class-divided economy.

### 📐 **Markup Pricing and Income Distribution**

- In oligopolistic industries, firms **set prices via markup** over costs:
    
    P=(1+μ)⋅Unit Labor CostP = (1 + \mu) \cdot \text{Unit Labor Cost}
    
- Distribution between wages and profits depends on:
    - The **markup (μ)**.
    - The **degree of monopoly**.
    - **Class bargaining power**.
- This forms the basis for his theory of **income distribution**.

## 🧰 III. **Growth Theory**

### 📈 **Theory of Economic Dynamics (1954)**

- Integrates short-term cycles and long-term growth.
- Growth rate depends on:
    - **Investment propensity** (out of profits).
    - **Government policy** (exogenous demand stimulation).
    - **Structural features** like income distribution.
- Distinct from Harrod-Domar by being **class-based** and dynamic differently (not just knife-edge instability).

## 🏛️ IV. **Political Economy**

### 🧠 **Political Aspects of Full Employment (1943)**

- Anticipates modern political economy of macro policy:
    - Even if **full employment is possible**, capitalists may **resist** it.
    - Reasons:
        - **Loss of social control** over workers.
        - **Weakened discipline** in the workplace.
        - Threat to **ideological dominance**.
- Insightful for understanding **why capital resists Keynesianism** in practice.

## 🛠️ V. **Public Finance and Policy**

### 💵 **Functional Finance Perspective**

- Fiscal deficits are **necessary and good** under unemployment.
- Deficits should **stimulate demand** and reduce idle capacity.
- Anticipates **Lerner’s Functional Finance**, but from a **class-conflict** perspective.

### 🛑 **Limits of Monetary Policy**

- Kalecki was skeptical of **monetary policy** as a stabilizer.
    - Interest rates affect investment *weakly* and *unpredictably*.
    - Emphasis on **fiscal policy and public investment** instead.

## 🌍 VI. **Open Economy and Imperialism**

### 🌐 **External Sector: Net Exports**

- Net exports boost profits:
    
    Profits=I+Cc+G+(X−M)−Sw\text{Profits} = I + C_c + G + (X - M) - S_w
    
- Trade surpluses benefit capitalist profits, much like investment and deficit spending.

### 🛢️ **Colonialism and Unequal Exchange**

- Early writings touched on how **imperialism supports capitalist accumulation** in core countries.
- Profits can be **sustained by extracting surplus** from the periphery.

## 📘 VII. **Key Concepts and Theoretical Innovations**

| Concept | Description |
| --- | --- |
| **Effective Demand** | Output is demand-constrained, not supply-constrained. |
| **Investment-led Profits** | "Capitalists earn what they spend." |
| **Class-Based Macroeconomics** | Divides agents into classes (workers/capitalists) instead of representative agents. |
| **Markup Pricing** | Prices set by firms over costs, not by marginal productivity. |
| **Political Constraints** | Macroeconomic policy is limited by class conflict, not technical feasibility. |
