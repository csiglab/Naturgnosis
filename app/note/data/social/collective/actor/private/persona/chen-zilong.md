# Chen Zilong

> Chen Zilong (陳子龍; 1608 – 1647) was a Chinese poet, essayist, and official active during the late Ming dynasty and early Qing dynasty. He is remembered as an editor of Xu Guangqi's agricultural treatise and as a loyalist poet of the Ming collapse.

| **Contribution** | **Description** |
| --- | --- |
| **Editing the *Nongzheng Quanshu*** | Introduced to Xu Guangqi in Beijing in 1630, Chen received the final draft of Xu's agricultural treatise from Xu's grandson in 1635; he assembled Songjiang scholars, added about twenty per cent new material, titled it *Nongzheng Quanshu*, and presented it to the Chongzhen Emperor in 1641. Frederic Wakeman called it the "first compilation of statecraft writing" in China. |
| **Poetry** | An accomplished poet who exchanged verses with the courtesan Liu Rushi; one of the "Three Men of Yun Jian" with Li Wen and Song Zhengyu. He began with romantic *ci* and turned to emotionally intense loyalist poetry, arguing that medieval "archaic and abstruse" forms were superior to the "bold and straightforward" style of rivals such as Qian Qianyi. |
| **Statecraft and loyalism** | A member of the reformist Restoration Society (復社), modelled on the Song loyalist Wen Tianxiang. He served as a prefectural judge, opposed renewed mining and a strong coin currency, and after the Ming fall was captured by the Qing; facing interrogation, he drowned himself in 1647. The Qianlong Emperor gave him the posthumous name Zhongyu (忠裕). |
