# Joe Henrich


> 

Joseph Henrich has proposed several models and theoretical frameworks throughout his academic work, largely focusing on evolutionary approaches to psychology, decision-making, and culture. His research encompasses a wide array of topics, including cultural learning, cultural evolution, culture-gene coevolution, human sociality, prestige, leadership, large-scale cooperation, religion, and the emergence of complex human institutions.

Some key concepts that Henrich has explored in his published works include:

1. **Cultural Evolution and Group Selection**: He has discussed how **cultural group selection** plays a role in the evolution of cooperation and the development of complex societies.
2. **Cultural Brain Hypothesis**: Henrich has collaborated on work proposing the Cultural Brain Hypothesis, which explores how culture drives brain expansion, sociality, and life history.
3. **Cumulative Cultural Evolution**: He has explored how human beings accumulate cultural knowledge over generations and how this process can lead to complex cultural practices and technologies.
4. **Prestige and Social Learning**: His work often references how individuals in societies use prestige as a cue for whom to learn from, thereby affecting cultural transmission and social hierarchies.
5. **Culture-gene Coevolution**: Henrich has written about how cultural practices can influence genetic evolution, and vice versa, leading to a unique pathway of human development.
6. **WEIRD Societies**: He has written about Western, Educated, Industrialized, Rich, and Democratic (WEIRD) societies and how they differ psychologically from other societies around the world.

Henrich's models are often backed by empirical research, integrating ethnographic tools from anthropology with experimental techniques from psychology and economics. His areas of interest span several geographical locations, including Amazonia, Chile, and Fiji, where he has conducted fieldwork to support his theories.
