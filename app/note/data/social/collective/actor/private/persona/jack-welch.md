# Jack Welch


> **Jack Welch** (1935–2020) was an American business executive best known for his role as **Chairman and CEO of General Electric (GE)** from 1981 to 2001. Under his leadership, GE became one of the most valuable and admired corporations in the world.
> 

> Jack Welch represents the **high-efficiency, shareholder-focused capitalism** of the late 20th century — admired for results, criticized for human cost. His tenure at GE is often seen as both a **high watermark** and a **cautionary tale**.
> 

## 📈 **Career Highlights**

- **Joined GE in 1960** as a chemical engineer.
- Became **CEO in 1981**, succeeding Reginald H. Jones.
- Led GE until his **retirement in 2001**.

## 🔧 **Management Style and Reforms**

1. **“Fix, sell, or close” philosophy**:
    - Welch insisted that every GE business be **#1 or #2 in its market**, or it would be fixed, sold, or shut down.
    - This led to **streamlining** and **downsizing**, often aggressively.
2. **Rank and Yank**:
    - Introduced a controversial performance management system:
        - Top 20% of employees were rewarded.
        - Middle 70% were encouraged to improve.
        - Bottom 10% were let go.
    - Criticized for creating a **cutthroat culture**.
3. **Delayering**:
    - Reduced layers of management to speed up decision-making and increase efficiency.
    - Promoted a **"boundaryless organization"**, breaking down silos.
4. **Globalization & Acquisitions**:
    - Pushed GE into **financial services**, **media**, and **global markets**.
    - Expanded GE Capital and acquired companies like RCA (which owned NBC).

## 💰 **Results**

- GE's market capitalization rose from **$12 billion to over $400 billion** during his tenure.
- Became a **management icon** and was named “Manager of the Century” by *Fortune* magazine in 1999.

## 📚 **Books**

- *Jack: Straight from the Gut* (2001) – Autobiography
- *Winning* (2005, with his wife Suzy Welch) – A business strategy book still widely read.

## 🧭 **Legacy and Criticism**

**Positive**:

- Revolutionized corporate efficiency.
- Inspired a generation of CEOs with a clear performance-driven model.
- Built a leadership pipeline (many GE alumni became CEOs elsewhere).

**Negative**:

- Criticized for **short-termism** and financial engineering.
- GE’s overreliance on GE Capital became problematic, contributing to the company’s later decline.
- “Rank and yank” has been widely debated and mostly abandoned in modern HR practice.
