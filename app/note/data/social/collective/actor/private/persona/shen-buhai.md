# Shen Buhai

> Shen Buhai (申不害; c. 400 – c. 337 BC) was a Chinese statesman, reformer, and diplomat, and Chancellor of the Han state for about fifteen years until his natural death in office. He is remembered as the foremost early theorist of administrative method — the control and performance-checking of a bureaucracy — variously grouped with the Legalists and, by Sima Qian, said to be rooted in Huang-Lao.

| **Contribution** | **Description** |
| --- | --- |
| **Administrative technique (*shu*)** | Reformed Han's administrative practices, military defenses, and (to a lesser extent) laws, emphasizing supervision and accountability: bestowing office by concrete responsibility, matching ministerial achievement to stated proposal (form to name), and grasping the handles of reward and punishment. |
| **Meritocratic selection** | Held that the methods for controlling a bureaucracy could not be mixed with feudal government or staffed merely by "a group of good men", but required men qualified for their posts — emphasizing able selection as much as Confucius did, but with constant vigilance over performance. |
| **Wu wei as method** | Advocated an "acting inactive" ruler who conceals himself and lets others reveal themselves — "like a mirror, reflecting light, doing nothing, and yet beauty and ugliness present themselves" — delegating routine management while holding the levers of power. |
| **Minimal punishment** | In contrast to Shang Yang, appears to have opposed punishment, hoping that a strict and efficient administration would abolish the need for it in the bureaucracy. |
| ***Shenzi*** | Author of the *Shenzi* (申子), a six-chapter work on governmental administration, now lost but partly reassembled from quotations by the sinologist Herrlee Creel. |

> Though the term *shu* is Han Fei's and likely posthumous, Shen Buhai seemed to influence Han-dynasty reformers and, arguably, the imperial examination — leading some scholars to count him a founder in world bureaucracy and perhaps the first political scientist. His given name Buhai (不害) means "does not harm".
