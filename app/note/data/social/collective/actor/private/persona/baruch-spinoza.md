# Baruch Spinoza

> **Baruch Spinoza** (Benedictus de Spinoza; 1632–1677) was a Dutch philosopher of the early Enlightenment. Raised in Amsterdam's Portuguese-Jewish community, he was expelled by its *herem* in 1656, earned his living grinding optical lenses, and developed a rationalist metaphysics of one infinite substance — God or Nature (*Deus sive Natura*) — set out deductively in the *Ethics*. His *Tractatus Theologico-Politicus* defended freedom of thought and the separation of philosophy from theology.

### What is a critical conceptual evaluation of this `social element instance`?

> Evaluating the person-concept applied to Spinoza: it identifies and locates him cleanly (embodied individual, societal actor, no state mandate), and the tree's rows discriminate sharply among constitution, agency, activity, association, and attribute. The concept strains where the person and his *works* are conflated — the books are epistemic artifacts received and validated by an observer, so reading them as parts of the person would type an epistemic object as a social constituent and invite the hagiographic fusion of man and corpus. The disciplined cut is to decompose the actor here and cite the artifacts by reference.

## Works

> Spinoza's philosophical, scientific, and linguistic works. These are the documents — the vehicles — recorded here for reference; the epistemic constructions they carry (the theories, concepts, and explanations, which may span several works) are decomposed in [Epistemic Practice](#epistemic-practice).

| Work | Description | Year |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Korte Verhandeling van God, de Mensch en deszelvs Welstand** (*Short Treatise on God, Man, and His Well-Being*) | Earliest philosophical work, in Dutch; an early sketch of the monist metaphysics and ethics later developed in the *Ethics*. | c. 1660 (published 1862) |
| **Renati Descartes principiorum philosophiae** (*Principles of Cartesian Philosophy*) | A geometrical exposition of Descartes's philosophy, the only work published under his own name in his lifetime. | 1663 |
| **Cogitata Metaphysica** (*Metaphysical Thoughts*) | Appendix to the *Principles*; sets out Spinoza's own positions on being, essence, and God. | 1663 |
| **Tractatus Theologico-Politicus** (*Theologico-Political Treatise*) | Anonymous defence of freedom of thought and the separation of philosophy from theology; argues for a historical-critical reading of Scripture. | 1670 |
| **Ethica, Ordine Geometrico Demonstrata** (*Ethics*) | Magnum opus; a deductive system from definitions and axioms deriving substance monism, the theory of the affects, and human freedom as the intellectual love of God. | 1677 (posthumous) |
| **Tractatus Politicus** (*Political Treatise*) | Analysis of the foundations of the state and the best constitution; unfinished at his death. | 1677 (posthumous) |
| **Tractatus de Intellectus Emendatione** (*Treatise on the Emendation of the Intellect*) | Unfinished methodological work on the correct method for attaining true knowledge. | 1677 (posthumous) |
| **Epistolae** (*Correspondence*) | Letters to and from Spinoza on metaphysics, politics, and the sciences. | 1677 (posthumous) |
| **Compendium Grammatices Linguae Hebraeae** (*Hebrew Grammar*) | A grammar of biblical Hebrew. | 1677 (posthumous) |
| **Stelkonstige Reeckening van den Regenboog** / **Reeckening van Kansén** (*On the Rainbow* / *On the Calculation of Chances*) | Scientific essays on optics and on probability. | 1687 |

## Epistemic Practice

> Spinoza is also a profound epistemic agent, and his social note carries an epistemic reading beside its social one. His practice is the organized work of an `Epistemic Agent` binding together an `Epistemic Orientation` (the pursuit of blessedness through adequate knowledge), an `Epistemic Operation` (deduction *more geometrico* and historical-critical exegesis), an `Epistemic Representation` (the geometrical order and the Hebrew grammar), a body of `Epistemic Construction`s (the substance-monism metaphysics, the theory of the affects, the political theory, and the rest), an `Epistemic Evaluation` (adequacy and geometrical certainty), an `Epistemic Access` regime (sense-experience, reason, intuitive knowledge, and the Scriptural text), an `Epistemic Communication` circuit (manuscript circulation, anonymous and posthumous publication, the correspondence), the `Epistemic Context` of *herem* and censorship, and the `Epistemic Infrastructure` of his workshop, library, and circles.
