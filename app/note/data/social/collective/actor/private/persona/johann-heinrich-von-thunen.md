# Johann Heinrich von Thünen


**Johann Heinrich von Thünen** was a 19th-century German economist and agriculturalist known for his pioneering work on agricultural economics and spatial economics. Here are the main ideas associated with his work:

### 1. **The Isolated State Model**

Thünen's most famous contribution is his model of the "Isolated State," presented in his book "Der isolierte Staat" (The Isolated State). This model analyzes the optimal use of land surrounding a central city, considering transportation costs and the perishability of agricultural products. It was one of the first **spatial economic models** and laid the foundation for **location theory**.

### 2. **Location Theory**

Thünen's model introduced the concept of location theory in economics. He proposed that different types of farming would be located at various distances from a central market based on transportation costs and the value of the land. For example:

- **Dairying and intensive farming** are located closest to the city because of high transportation costs and perishability.
- **Forestry** follows next because timber is bulky and costly to transport.
- **Crop farming** and **grain production** are farther away since these products are less perishable and cheaper to transport.
- **Ranching and livestock raising** are located farthest from the city due to lower animal transportation costs.

### 3. **Economic Rent**

Thünen introduced the concept of economic rent in his model. Economic rent refers to the surplus income a farmer earns due to the advantageous location of their land relative to the market. The closer the land is to the market, the higher its economic rent due to reduced transportation costs.

### 4. **Marginal Productivity Theory**

Thünen also contributed to the development of marginal productivity theory. He was among the first to describe the relationship between labor input and agricultural output. He suggested that labor productivity diminishes as more labor is added, a principle later formalized in neoclassical economics.

### 5. **Intensive vs. Extensive Agriculture**

Thünen differentiated between intensive and extensive agriculture based on their proximity to the market and land use intensity. Intensive agriculture, requiring more labor and capital per unit of land, is practiced closer to the market. In contrast, extensive agriculture, which uses larger land areas with less labor and capital, is practiced further away.

### 6. **The Thünen Rings**

In his model, Thünen visualized the land use around the central market city as a series of concentric rings, each dedicated to a specific type of agriculture. This spatial arrangement is driven by transportation costs and the perishability of goods, providing a theoretical explanation for land use patterns.
