# Ideational

> **Ideational** designates the synontic family of **social philosophies**: systems of shared ideas that orient the **conduct of life** — personal and collective — by saying what is to be done and not done, for the individual and for the collective.

> The **Ideational** facet — shared ideas, worldviews, ideologies, and cognitive schemas that influence perception, meaning-making, and social goals — is defined in [Facet](note.html?n=social/framework/facet.md). This note is the home of the family of elements scoped by that facet: the philosophies of conduct through which agents and collectives make life intelligible and decide how to live it.

> Morality appears here only insofar as shared moral schemas, norms, and traditions are transmitted, recognized, negotiated, and revised. The normative machinery — justification, duty, imputability, judgment, and repair — belongs to [Philosophia Moralis et Operis](note.html?n=meta/philosophia-moralis-et-operis.md).

> The shared moral schemas named here are elaborated in [Morality](note.html?n=social/synontic/ideational/morality/morality.md).

## Formulation

### What is an Ideational Element?

> An **Ideational Element** is a synontic element that functions as a *social philosophy*: a relatively stable system of shared ideas, worldviews, or doctrines about the conduct of life — personal and collective — specifying what to do and not to do, what to value, how to interpret situations, and toward what ends to live.

> It is a philosophy of conduct in the broad sense: comprehensive worldviews (religions, civilizations) and focused doctrines (political ideologies, economic schools, ethical systems) alike. Its unifying function is orientation — it tells the individual and the collective how to act or refrain from acting.

### What type of social element does an Ideational Element belong to?

**An Ideational Element belongs to `Synontic Element` as its primary kind** — a recognition-constituted, relational element whose significance is fixed by shared interpretation rather than by material substrate; see [Synontic](note.html?n=social/synontic/synontic.md).

> Its type path is nested: `Synontic Element (→ Kind) Ideational Element`. It is the family of synontic elements scoped by the Ideational facet.

Its layer is **Synontic**: an ideational element exists through shared recognition and interpretation — a doctrine has no extra-mental body, though it may be carried by texts, rituals, institutions, and material symbols. Its facet is **Ideational** (see [Facet](note.html?n=social/framework/facet.md)).

### Which are the categories of the ideational family?

> The family is grouped by the domain of conduct a philosophy primarily orients. Categories are communicative handles, not exclusive boxes: a single doctrine may sit in more than one.

| **Category** | **Domain of conduct** | **Orients** |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Political ideology | Power, authority, and collective decision | How a polity should be ordered and who should rule |
| Economic doctrine | Production, exchange, and distribution | How provisioning should be organized and wealth distributed |
| Religious / spiritual system | Sacred order, ritual, and ultimate meaning | How to live in relation to the sacred and the community of believers |
| Ethical / philosophical school | Character, virtue, and the good life | How the individual should live and judge conduct |
| Civilizational worldview | Collective identity and historical order | How a civilization understands itself and its trajectory |
| Meta-concept | Ideas about ideas | The general structures (belief, ideology, morality) through which philosophies of conduct are built |

## QA

### Is Ideational a facet or an element?

> Both, at different registers. As a **facet**, Ideational is an analytical dimension that scopes inquiry by dominant causal mechanism (meaning-making) and owns no nodes — see [Facet](note.html?n=social/framework/facet.md). As a **family of elements**, the Ideational Elements are the synontic elements scoped by that facet: the concrete philosophies of conduct. This note holds the family; the facet table holds the lens.

### Why is Ideational not decomposed as one social element?

> Because it is not a single compound but a **family** — a stratum of distinct social philosophies, each an element of its own. Forcing one decomposition tree would type the whole family as one entity and misplace each doctrine as a part of it. Decompose each ideal element (a liberalism, a religion, a school) on its own note; this hub only types the family and catalogs its members.

### Where do the texts, rituals, and institutions carrying an ideational element belong?

> By reference, to their own decompositions. A sacred text is a literary work, a rite a symbolic practice, a church an institution — these carry or express a philosophy of conduct without being it. Decompose the carrier where its grammar lives and link it from the ideational element that it transmits.
