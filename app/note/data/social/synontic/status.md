# Status

> A **Status** is the relative standing recognized by a collective: the position from which an actor is evaluated, deferred to, or looked up to — prestige earned, rank assigned, seniority accrued, membership certified.

## Formulation

### What is this social instance?

> A status fixes *where one stands and in whose eyes*: the collective that recognizes, the criteria it applies, and the deference, precedence, or entitlement that follows. Standing without recognition is vanity; recognition without criteria is rumor. Every status kind below is one stabilized way a collective answers "who counts, and for what."

### What type of social element does this instance belong to?

**Status belongs to the attributive reading of standing** — it attaches to an actor rather than standing alone: `Social Actor (→ Attribute)` in the guide's own link grammar. Its layer is **Synontic** where it exists through collective recognition (honor, prestige, reputation) with **Ontic** anchors where it is inscribed (badges, titles, offices, registers). It is typed under [Actor](note.html?n=social/collective/actor/actor.md), the root hub of the actor typology.

Readable secondarily as **Normative** where rank orders are backed by enforceable rules, and as a **Social Role** where a standing is occupied as an expectation-tag (office, degree, rank). Those readings are prose, not second trees; the tree below is typed once.

### Is the type of this `social element instance` unambiguous?

> Yes. Status is unambiguously an attributive standing: the Normative rank-order and Social Role occupancy aspects are secondary readings of the same standing, not rival roots — no second tree is required.

### Which facet does this element belong to, if applicable?

> Social-general: standing orders every association before any economic or political scoping.

## QA

### What distinguishes Status from a Social Role?

> A status is standing *attributed* — where one ranks in others' eyes. A role is conduct *expected* — what one must do from a position. The two meet constantly: offices confer rank, and rank licenses office. Type the standing as Status, the expectations as Social Role, and the binding between them as the occupying relation — never merge them into one row.

### What is status honor?

> Standing recognized as a way of life rather than a position: the code of conduct, consumption, and association through which a group marks who belongs to it. See Weber on status groups below.
