# Facet

> **Facet** is the analytical concept denoting a distinct dimension or space of social interaction: a coherent set of patterns, processes, or mechanisms, defined by its dominant causal mechanism, that can be analyzed independently while remaining interdependent with the others. Facets are analytical tools for organizing the representation of social reality, not reified entities; they scope inquiry, never owning nodes.

> A facet is defined by dominant causal mechanisms, not by location or actors. Assign a facet by asking which mechanism does the explanatory work here — market coordination, rule enforcement, meaning-making, technical transformation — not where the instance sits or who participates.

## Is there a cultural facet?

> No. Culture is not a single domain of social life and therefore does not form a facet of its own; it permeates all facets. Every facet is, in effect, constituted by cultural elements — shared symbols, meanings, norms, and habitual certainties — making culture a common component across the entirety of social reality rather than one corner of it.

## Why facets and not "systems"?

> The traditional **notion** of a system — a cohesive whole with fixed boundaries — is a poor organizer for social reality. More specialized concepts, such as **social regions**, **ecosystems**, or other **relational frameworks**, capture what matters instead: the multiplicity of interacting structures, emergent dynamics, fluid and porous boundaries, and complex interdependencies of the social sphere. Facets cut across these ensembles as analytical dimensions; no facet draws a boundary around a society.

| **Social** | The general facet, encompassing all other specialized facets: overall association, structure, relationships, and interactions. |
| **Economic** | Patterned interactions of material provisioning: production, exchange, distribution, and consumption. |
| **Normative** | Rules, regulations, moral expectations, and social obligations that guide behavior and resolve conflicts. |

## Facets and layers

> Facets and layers are two orthogonal axes. Layers (Ontic, Synontic, Noetic, Multi) state in which stratum an element exists — extra-mentally, through shared interpretation, as a descriptive instrument, or across strata; facets state by which dominant causal mechanism it is scoped. Every social element is read against both: an element's layer is part of its type, while its facet scopes the inquiry. See "Why do we need layers and facets?" in [Philosophia Socialium et Operis](note.html?n=meta/philosophia-socialium-et-operis.md).

## Facet vs. field

> Do not confuse a facet with the `Social Field` social element type. A **facet** is an analytical dimension that scopes inquiry and owns nothing. A **field** is a structured arena of social relations, competition, and influence — an instance with positions, resources, and logics of its own — decomposable as a social element. Facets are lenses; fields are loci.

## QA

### Why do we need the idea of a facet?

> Because one configuration of social reality answers to many mechanisms at once, and naming none of them invites mono-causal explanation. Facets keep inquiry scoped — economic coordination is explained economically, normative binding normatively — without carving reality into separate containers or reifying each dimension into a substance of its own.

### Why integrate the idea of a facet into the instance decomposition?

> Because every associative move presupposes a parsing of who relates how *under which mechanism*. Typing each decomposition row from its grouping segments (per the well-formedness rule) and reading the whole tree against one dominant facet keeps the decomposition from mixing causal grammars mid-branch: the same port city decomposes as labor market under the economic facet, as jurisdiction under the legal one, and as hiring culture under the symbolic one — three readings, each internally coherent.
