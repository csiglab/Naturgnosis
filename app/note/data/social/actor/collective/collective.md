# Collective

> A **Collective** is the most abstract element on the plural side of social ontology: a social entity constituted by a plurality of social agents connected through one or more constitutive relations — the genus of which populations, communities, organizations, groups, and polities are kinds.

## Formulation

### What social element type does this social instance belong to?

**Collective belongs to the `Social Compound` social element type (Structural category)** — the generic bounded collective scoping a decomposition: a plurality of agents bound by constitutive relations into an identifiable social entity.

It is not `Collective / Organization` as such (that is its organized subtype — the deliberate actor with identity, membership, and rules; see [Organization](note.html?n=social/actor/societal/organization.md)); the genus also covers unorganized aggregates (Population), recognition-constituted communities, and compounds. Its layer is **Ontic** where the collective exists extra-mentally (populations, organizations, polities) and **Synontic** where it exists through shared recognition (nations-as-peoples, imagined communities). Its facet is social-general: the dominant causal mechanism is plural association as such, prior to economic, political, or ideational scoping. It is typed under [Actor](note.html?n=social/actor/actor.md), the root hub of the actor typology.

Readable secondarily as an `Interaction Unit` (a bounded entity capable of interaction), as an `Institution` (where the collective just is its stabilized rules), and as a `Polity` (where bound by authority). Those readings are prose, not second trees; the tree below is typed once.

On terms: **Collective** is the genus and instance term. **Organization** names the organized subtype. **Institution** names the stabilized rules a collective hosts, never the actor itself. **Group** names an interacting aggregate with unity but without membership or rules (see [Social Group](note.html?n=social/actor/collective/group/group.md)). **Association**, **Union**, **Cooperative**, **Party** name organization subtypes.

### What is this social instance?

> A collective fixes *who hangs together and how*: the plurality of agents, the constitutive relations binding them — membership, governance, coordination, territory, recognition — and the entity-ness that makes the plurality one social thing rather than an arbitrary set. Every collective kind below, organized or not, is one way plurality becomes entity: through charter and roles, through joint action, through shared identity and symbols, through co-location and jurisdiction, or through binding authority.

## QA

### What is the identification mechanism?

> A collective is identified by the tie that counts one as belonging: a charter or register for membership organizations, mutual recognition for communities, jurisdiction or census for populations and polities. The mechanism is always a constitutive relation — membership, recognition, or authority — never mere co-location. See [Membership Tie](note.html?n=social/actor/actor.md) as typed in the actor hub.

### What grounds a social collective?

> A collective is grounded by whatever makes its plurality one entity rather than an arbitrary set. The grounding varies by kind:

| Collective kind | Description | Grounding mechanism |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Kinship-based collective | Family and descent groups | Descent and alliance |
| Membership-based collective | Associations and organizations | Charter, roles, and admission rules |
| Activity-based collective | Social groups and followings | Joint action and recurrent interaction |
| Territorial collective | Populations and territorial polities | Co-location and jurisdiction |
| Recognition-based collective | Communities and nations-as-peoples | Shared identity, symbols, and mutual recognition |
| Authority-based collective | Political collectives and colonies | Mandate and sovereignty |
| Provisioning-based collective | Cooperatives, firms, unions, corporations | Exchange, employment, and ownership |

### What makes a collection a collective?

> A collection of randomly selected people is not necessarily a collective in the social-ontological sense. A collection becomes a collective only where a constitutive relation binds the plurality into an entity: shared membership, joint action, common identity, co-location under jurisdiction, or binding authority. Without such a relation there is multiplicity but no oneness — and nothing to decompose.

### Should the term grounding be used in this context?

> Yes, in the constitutive sense: grounding names what makes the plurality the entity it is, not a causal foundation beneath it and not a moral justification above it. Each kind above states its own grounding mechanism; there is no single ground of collectivity as such.
