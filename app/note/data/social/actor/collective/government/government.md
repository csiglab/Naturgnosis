# Government

> A **Government** is the set of institutions and authorities constituted to exercise public power within a state — the office-holding collective that directs policy, executes laws, and answers for binding collective decisions under a constitutional order.

> **Note:** This note can be used to study government at any level, as well as different conceptions of government within a state. However, its primary focus is the central government, particularly the executive branch and its top-level organization.

## Formulation

### What is this social instance?

> A government fixes *who governs and how*: the mandate that warrants rule, the office-holders and organizations that compose it, the decision arrangements through which it acts as one, and the jurisdiction — persons, places, matters — over which it claims authority. Canonical instances: a national government (cabinet and ministries), a regional government of a federal unit or autonomous region, a local or municipal government.

### What type of social element does this instance belong to?

**Government belongs to `Social Compound` as its primary kind** — an organized political collective (`Collective (→ Kind) Political Collective`); see [Collective](note.html?n=social/actor/collective/collective.md). It is typed under [Actor](note.html?n=social/actor/actor.md), the root hub of the actor typology, and specialized organizationally under [Organization](note.html?n=social/actor/societal/organization.md); its operating units are [Public Organization](note.html?n=social/actor/societal/public-organization.md) instances.

Its layer is **Ontic** where the government exists extra-mentally (office-holders, ministries, premises, budgets) with **Synontic** aspects (authority, legitimacy, and jurisdiction exist through shared recognition and constitutional warrant).

It is not the `State` (the political organization that persists across successive governments), not the `Regime` (the rules by which power is acquired, exercised, and transferred), not the `Political System` (the broader configuration of institutions, actors, and processes), not `Public Administration` (the machinery through which it operates), and not the governing leadership (the persons — decompose the office, where the mandate lives, not the person).

### Is the type of this `social element instance` unambiguous?

> Yes, as a compound kind: the mandate-bound governing collective is unambiguous, while the state-actor reading (an `Interaction Unit` wielding delegated sovereign power) and the office/role readings (minister, head of government as `Social Role`s) are secondary aspects of the same instance, not rival roots. They are recorded here as prose, not second trees; the tree below is typed once. A second tree is not grown because the state-actor grammar (`Social Actor` / `State Actor`) is not established as a subtype branch in [Actor](note.html?n=social/actor/actor.md), so it would use unlicensed types.

### Which facet does this element belong to, if applicable?

> Political first (authoritative rule-execution is the dominant causal mechanism); administrative where it operates through agencies and the civil service; legal-constitutional where its mandate, competencies, and limits are defined. See [Facet](note.html?n=social/facet.md).

## Government Model

> A **Government Model** is an abstract conceptual representation of the constitutive elements, institutional configuration, organizational relationships, and operating arrangements through which a government is constituted, organized, and exercised.

> The model supports the systematic characterization and comparison of governments by specifying their composition, organizational structure, allocation of competencies, authority relationships, decision arrangements, territorial organization, government-wide administrative system, particular organizations adminsitrative systems, capabilities, resources, and operations.

| Governmental and Administrative Models          | Administrative Representation        | Administrative Process Model                             | A conceptual representation of the activities, decisions, actors, information flows, and controls through which an administrative process operates.                                                              | A process model represents a selected workflow and should not be confused with a complete model of the organization or administrative system.                                              |
| Governmental and Administrative Models          | Analytical Representation            | Government Typology                                      | A classification of governments according to specified political, constitutional, institutional, organizational, or administrative characteristics.                                                              | Typologies depend on explicit criteria; a government may combine characteristics associated with several ideal types.                                                                      |
| Governmental and Administrative Models          | Analytical Representation            | Government Classification Framework                      | A structured set of dimensions, criteria, and categories for classifying and comparing governments.                                                                                                              | The framework should distinguish observable characteristics from evaluative judgments and avoid categories that overlap without a defined rationale.                                       |
| Governmental and Administrative Models          | Analytical Representation            | Government Characterization Framework                    | A structured framework for describing the political, constitutional, institutional, organizational, administrative, fiscal, and performance characteristics of a country or government.                          | A characterization framework organizes evidence; it is not necessarily a causal model or a complete representation of government.                                                          |

### Is the term Public Administrative System conceptually useful?

> If we adopt a canonical definition of a **Public Administrative System** as the totality of **government administration** functions, we encounter a conceptual problem. The totality comprises both administrative arrangements shared across public organizations and the particular arrangements of individual organizations. 

> What do we gain by conceptualizing shared **administrative arrangements and organization-specific** particularities collectively as a *system*? The term may be useful as a broad research category for studying administrative phenomena across public organizations. However, for analytical comparison and characterization, more concrete and scope-specific concepts—such as *Government-Wide Administrative System* and *Organization-Specific Administrative System*—are likely to be more productive.
