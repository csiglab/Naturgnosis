# Government

> A **Government** is the set of institutions and authorities constituted to exercise public power within a state — the office-holding collective that directs policy, executes laws, and answers for binding collective decisions under a constitutional order.

## Formulation

### What is this social instance?

> A government fixes *who governs and how*: the mandate that warrants rule, the office-holders and organizations that compose it, the decision arrangements through which it acts as one, and the jurisdiction — persons, places, matters — over which it claims authority. Canonical instances: a national government (cabinet and ministries), a regional government of a federal unit or autonomous region, a local or municipal government.

### What type of social element does this instance belong to?

**Government belongs to `Social Compound` as its primary kind** — an organized political collective (`Collective (→ Kind) Political Collective`); see [Collective](note.html?n=social/actor/collective/collective.md). It is typed under [Actor](note.html?n=social/actor/actor.md), the root hub of the actor typology, and specialized organizationally under [Organization](note.html?n=social/actor/societal/organization.md); its operating units are [Public Organization](note.html?n=social/actor/societal/public-organization.md) instances.

Its layer is **Ontic** where the government exists extra-mentally (office-holders, ministries, premises, budgets) with **Synontic** aspects (authority, legitimacy, and jurisdiction exist through shared recognition and constitutional warrant).

It is not the `State` (the political organization that persists across successive governments), not the `Regime` (the rules by which power is acquired, exercised, and transferred), not the `Political System` (the broader configuration of institutions, actors, and processes), not `Public Administration` (the machinery through which it operates), and not the governing leadership (the persons — decompose the office, where the mandate lives, not the person).

### Is the type of this `social element instance` unambiguous?

> Yes, as a compound kind: the mandate-bound governing collective is unambiguous, while the state-actor reading (an `Interaction Unit` wielding delegated sovereign power) and the office/role readings (minister, head of government as `Social Role`s) are secondary aspects of the same instance, not rival roots. They are recorded here as prose, not second trees; the tree below is typed once. A second tree is not grown because the state-actor grammar (`Social Actor` / `State Actor`) is not established as a subtype branch in [Actor](note.html?n=social/actor/actor.md), so it would use unlicensed types.

### Which facet does this element belong to, if applicable?

> Political first (authoritative rule-execution is the dominant causal mechanism); administrative where it operates through agencies and the civil service; legal-constitutional where its mandate, competencies, and limits are defined. See [Facet](note.html?n=social/facet.md).

## Government Model

> A **Government Model** is an abstract conceptual representation of the institutional architecture, constitutive elements, structural relationships, and operational mechanisms through which public authority is constituted, organized, exercised, and reproduced within a political community.

> **Note:** Frankly, the analytical gain from distinguishing a *Government Organization Model* from a *Government Administration Model* may be limited. The distinction becomes more meaningful if the former captures the **fundamental principles and abstract design choices that configure government**, while the latter describes their institutional and operational realization. Under this interpretation, the Government Organization Model specifies the most abstract constitutive and configurational elements of government, whereas the Government Administration Model specifies how those principles are embodied in the structures, systems, and mechanisms through which government operates.

### How is the concept of a `Government Organization Model` operationalized?

> The model is operationalized by identifying and characterizing the **institutional configuration of government**: which institutions exist, what responsibilities they hold, how authority is distributed, and how they relate to one another.

> **Operational output:** A structured representation of governmental institutions, their organizational units, competencies, and relationships, suitable for describing and comparing institutional arrangements across countries.

> The model should distinguish **formal institutional arrangements from their actual operation**. Constitutional provisions and organizational charts describe formally established structures; they do not, by themselves, establish how authority is exercised in practice.

Its principal analytical dimensions are:

| Dimension                         | Description
| --------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| **Institutional Composition**     | The governmental institutions that exist, including the executive, legislature, judiciary, and other constitutionally established institutions within the model's scope. |
| **Institutional Typology**        | The types of institutions, classified by mandate, authority, function, organizational form, and institutional status.                                                    |
| **Functional Differentiation**    | How governmental responsibilities are differentiated into functions such as legislation, execution, adjudication, regulation, and oversight.                             |
| **Organizational Structure**      | The organizational units, offices, and positions composing each institution and their internal arrangements.                                                             |
| **Competency Allocation**         | The formal powers, responsibilities, and decision-making competencies assigned to each institution and organizational unit.                                              |
| **Authority Relationships**       | The hierarchical, supervisory, delegated, and independent relationships among institutions and organizational units.                                                     |
| **Coordination Arrangements**     | The formal mechanisms through which institutions coordinate decisions and activities across organizational boundaries.                                                   |
| **Accountability Arrangements**   | The relationships and mechanisms through which institutions answer for their decisions and exercise of authority.                                                        |
| **Territorial Organization**      | The distribution of governmental institutions and competencies across national, regional, and local levels.                                                              |
| **Institutional Interdependence** | The dependencies, shared competencies, checks, and constraints connecting governmental institutions.                                                                     |

### How is the concept of a `Government Administration Model` operationalized?

> The model is operationalized by identifying and characterizing the **administrative institutions, organizational units, personnel, capabilities, and operating arrangements through which public administration conducts public affairs**.

> **Operational output:** A structured representation of public administration's institutions, organizational structure, personnel system, capabilities, resources, processes, and control arrangements, suitable for describing and comparing administrative systems across countries.

> The model should distinguish **formal administrative arrangements from actual administrative capacity and performance**. The existence of an agency, an established procedure, or a legally defined competency does not establish that the agency functions effectively or that the procedure is implemented as intended.

Its principal analytical dimensions are:

| Dimension                                     | Description characterize                                                                                                                                                                      |
| --------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| **Administrative Institutional Composition**  | The administrative institutions composing public administration, including ministries, departments, agencies, and other administrative bodies.                                                         |
| **Administrative Institutional Typology**     | The types of administrative bodies, classified by mandate, function, legal status, organizational form, and degree of autonomy.                                                                        |
| **Administrative Structure**                  | The organizational units, offices, and positions composing administrative institutions and their internal arrangements.                                                                                |
| **Functional Allocation**                     | How administrative responsibilities are distributed across institutions and organizational units, including policy implementation, regulation, revenue collection, enforcement, and service provision. |
| **Administrative Competencies**               | The legally assigned responsibilities, decision-making powers, and operational authority of administrative institutions and units.                                                                     |
| **Administrative Authority Relationships**    | The hierarchical, supervisory, delegated, and autonomous relationships governing administrative organizations.                                                                                         |
| **Coordination Arrangements**                 | The mechanisms through which administrative institutions coordinate policies, activities, information, and resources.                                                                                  |
| **Civil Service System**                      | The institutional and legal arrangements governing public personnel, including eligibility, recruitment, selection, appointment, career progression, compensation, performance, and separation.        |
| **Administrative Capabilities**               | The human, organizational, informational, technological, and material capacities available to perform administrative functions.                                                                        |
| **Administrative Resources**                  | The financial, human, material, and informational resources allocated to administrative institutions and their activities.                                                                             |
| **Administrative Processes and Operations**   | The procedures, workflows, decision processes, and operational arrangements through which administrative responsibilities are performed.                                                               |
| **Administrative Accountability and Control** | The mechanisms for supervision, internal control, audit, compliance, performance monitoring, and accountability.                                                                                       |
| **Administrative Performance**                | The extent to which administrative institutions perform their assigned responsibilities, considering effectiveness, efficiency, reliability, responsiveness, and other relevant criteria.              |

### What Are the Differences Between the Two Models?

The models are complementary but should not be conflated.

| Analytical question             | Government Organization Model                                                                                               | Government Administration Model                                                             |
| ------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Primary concern**             | How government is institutionally organized.                                                                                | How public administration is organized and operates.                                        |
| **Object of characterization**  | Governmental institutions and their organizational relationships.                                                           | Administrative institutions, personnel, capabilities, and operations.                       |
| **Institutional scope**         | The governmental institutional configuration, including political and constitutional institutions within the defined scope. | The administrative machinery through which public affairs are conducted.                    |
| **Structural focus**            | Institutional composition, functional differentiation, competency allocation, and authority relationships.                  | Administrative agencies, internal structures, staffing, and operational arrangements.       |
| **Personnel focus**             | The distribution of governmental offices, positions, and responsibilities.                                                  | The civil service system and the management of administrative personnel.                    |
| **Operational focus**           | The institutional arrangements through which governmental responsibilities and authority are distributed and coordinated.   | The processes and capabilities through which administrative responsibilities are performed. |
| **Performance focus**           | Institutional functioning, coordination, and the effective distribution and exercise of authority.                          | Administrative capacity, operational effectiveness, efficiency, and service performance.    |
| **Central analytical question** | How is government institutionally constituted and organized?                                                                | How is public administration constituted, equipped, and operated?                           |

## QA

### Government versus state, regime, political system, administration, and governing leadership?

| Element | What it is | Why it is not the government |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **State** | A political organization exercising public authority over a population within a territory. | It persists across successive governments; the government is its transient office-holding collective. |
| **Regime** | The rules determining how power is acquired, exercised, and transferred. | Regime type constrains but does not describe the government's organization or capacity. |
| **Political System** | The broader configuration of institutions, actors, processes, and relationships making binding decisions. | Broader than government; its boundaries vary across analytical traditions. |
| **Public Administration** | The administrative institutions and organized activities through which public affairs are conducted. | It is the machinery the government operates through, including regulation, revenue, and enforcement beyond any single government. |
| **Governing leadership** | The persons holding office at a given moment. | Persons are societal actors even in office; the mandate lives in the office, not the person. |
| **Legitimacy** | The recognized justification of an authority's right to rule. | Legality and sociological legitimacy are not identical; a lawful government may lack either. |

### Is the separation of powers a description of government?

> No — it is one principle of functional differentiation and institutional allocation among others. Separation may be partial; institutional independence and practical checks vary. Formal organizational charts, including branch diagrams, do not necessarily reflect actual distributions of power. Type the formal allocation as `Functional Differentiation` and the effective distribution as observed conduct; never read one off the other.

| Governmental Organization     | Governmental Functional Differentiation | The differentiation of governmental responsibilities into distinct functions, such as legislation, execution, adjudication, regulation, and oversight.      | Functional differentiation does not necessarily correspond to a strict separation of institutions or powers.             |
| Governmental Organization     | Governmental Institutional Structure    | The configuration of governmental institutions, their competencies, and their formal relationships.                                                         | Formal institutional arrangements may differ from the actual distribution and exercise of power.                         |
| Governmental Organization     | Administrative Structure                | The arrangement of administrative institutions, agencies, departments, and organizational units, including their hierarchical and functional relationships. | Administrative structure concerns the organization of administration rather than the entire governmental system.         |
| Governmental Organization     | Institutional Typology                  | A classification of institutions according to characteristics such as mandate, authority, function, organizational form, or institutional status.           | Classifications depend on the criteria selected; institutions may belong to multiple types.                              |

Its scope must also distinguish governmental institutions from the broader political system and state. |

### Which concepts should guide the structure of a country characterization?

```bash
Country Model
│
├── Political System Model
│
├── State Model
│
├── Government Model
│   ├── Government Organization Model
│   └── Government Administration Model
│
├── Public Finance Model
│
├── Economic Model
│
└── Social Structure Model
```

| Concept | Analytical Role |
| --- | --- |
| **Country Model** | Provides the overarching framework for characterizing a country. |
| **Political System Model** | Represents political institutions, actors, processes, and relationships through which political power is exercised. |
| **State Model** | Represents the state's territorial, institutional, legal, and authoritative dimensions. |
| **Government Model** | Represents the institutions, functions, structures, and arrangements through which government exercises public authority. A descriptive model of an existing government must be distinguished from a normative model of a proposed one, and governmental institutions from the broader political system and state. |
| **Government Organization Model** | Represents the configuration of governmental institutions, organizational units, competencies, and relationships. A descriptive model of an existing arrangement must be distinguished from a normative model of a proposed one. |
| **Government Administration Model** | Represents the organization, personnel, capabilities, and operations of public administration. The organization of administration must be distinguished from the broader organization of government, including its political and constitutional institutions. |
