# Roberto Mangabeira Unger


> Roberto Mangabeira Unger is a Brazilian philosopher, legal theorist, and politician whose **social theory** is one of the most ambitious and original in contemporary thought. His work spans law, politics, economics, and philosophy, and it resists conventional ideological categorization. Unger’s core contribution is the idea that **social reality is plastic, mutable, and subject to radical innovation**—a view he calls **"formative context" theory** and **"radical institutionalism."**
> 

## Ideas

| **Main Idea/Concept** | **Description** |
| --- | --- |
| **Empowered Democracy** | Advocates for deepening democracy by increasing citizen participation and decentralizing power. |
| **Plasticity of Social Institutions** | Emphasizes that social structures and institutions are malleable and can be reshaped by human action. |
| **Radical Reformism** | Proposes comprehensive and transformative reforms to address systemic social and economic issues. |
| **Imagination and Innovation** | Stresses the importance of creativity and innovation in driving social and economic progress. |
| **Critique of Neoliberalism** | Criticizes neoliberal policies for exacerbating inequalities and limiting democratic control. |
| **Alternative Globalization** | Advocates for a form of globalization that prioritizes social justice, environmental sustainability, and equitable development. |

| **Social Solidarity** | Emphasizes the need for social cohesion and collective action to address common challenges. |

## Social Theory

### **Formative Contexts**

> Unger argues that every society is shaped by **formative contexts**—deeply embedded institutional and imaginative frameworks that define the limits of social, economic, and political life.
> 

> Unlike structuralist or Marxist thinkers, Unger believes these contexts are **not fixed** but **radically revisable**. Human societies are not constrained by immutable laws but are **open to deep innovation**.
> 

 These include:

- Legal and institutional structures (e.g., property, contract, government)
- Social hierarchies and norms
- Prevailing ideologies and narratives

### **Plasticity of the Social**

> “There is no natural or necessary form of social life.”
> 

> This means that **human institutions and practices are inherently malleable**, and we can **rebuild society in alternative ways** through imaginative thought and political action.
> 

### **False Necessity and Institutional Alternatives**

> In his seminal book *False Necessity: Anti-Necessitarian Social Theory in the Service of Radical Democracy* (1987), Unger argues that:
> 
- Many social structures are **presented as necessary or inevitable** (e.g., capitalism, nation-states, legal frameworks)
- These necessities are **"false"**, sustained by ideology and inertia rather than logic or nature
- We must explore **alternative institutional forms**—especially those that **deepen democratic participation**, **flatten hierarchies**, and **promote self-realization**

### **Empowered Democracy**

Unger calls for an "empowered democracy," which:

- Goes beyond liberal democracy
- Involves **radical experimentation** with new forms of political, economic, and social organization
- Requires **institutional innovations** like:
    - Rotating leadership
    - Decentralized decision-making
    - Flexible property regimes
    - Educational systems fostering creative and critical capabilities

### **Non-ideal Theory with a Utopian Aim**

Unlike most analytic political philosophers who work within "ideal theory" (e.g., Rawls), Unger:

- Works in a **"non-ideal" but aspirational mode**
- Tries to **imagine and design real alternatives**, not just describe justice in theory
- Believes **political imagination is a central intellectual tool**—we must design new futures, not just critique the present

### **Integration with Law, Politics, and the Economy**

Unger was a key figure in the **Critical Legal Studies movement**, and much of his early work challenges the **neutrality and objectivity** of legal reasoning. He integrates his social theory with:

- **Law**: Law is both a tool of reproduction and a site of creative disruption.
- **Politics**: Calls for **"high-energy politics"**—where citizens are engaged in continuous institutional redesign.
- **Economy**: Advocates for **democratizing the market** by introducing **cooperative, decentralized, and innovative economic models**.
