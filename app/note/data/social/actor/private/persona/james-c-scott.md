# James C. Scott


> ..
> 

| **Idea** | **Description** |
| --- | --- |
| **High Modernism** | Scott critiques "high modernism," a belief in the ability to scientifically manage a society that often leads to authoritarian state projects that disregard local knowledge and customs. |
| **Legibility and State Control** | He argues that states attempt to make society "legible" by standardizing and simplifying complex social practices, which can lead to inefficiencies and resistance from local communities. |
| **Anarchism and Resistance** | Scott explores how marginalized and oppressed groups resist state control and oppression through everyday resistance, such as foot-dragging, sabotage, and non-compliance. |
| **Peasant Studies** | In his work on peasant societies, Scott emphasizes the importance of understanding peasants' economic and social practices, who often resist external control and exploitation. |
| **The Moral Economy** | Scott introduces the concept of a "moral economy," where peasant communities prioritize social norms and subsistence ethics over market-based profit motives, challenging capitalist exploitation. |
| **Critique of State-led Development** | He critiques state-led development projects that ignore the local context and impose top-down solutions, often resulting in failures and harm to the people they intend to help. |
| **Zomia and Stateless Societies** | Scott studies the highland regions of Southeast Asia, known as Zomia, where communities have historically evaded state control, maintaining stateless societies with their forms of governance. |
