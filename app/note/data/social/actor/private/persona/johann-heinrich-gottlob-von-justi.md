# Johann Heinrich Gottlob von Justi

**Johann Heinrich Gottlob von Justi** (1717–1771) was the most systematic cameralist of the 18th century — chair of cameral sciences at the Theresianum in Vienna (1750), police director in Göttingen (1755), Danish service in Copenhagen (1757), and Prussian mining administrator under Frederick II until his fall and death in custody at Küstrin. Living from his pen, he published over fifty works.

He was the first to organize cameralism into a systematic state science pivoting on *gemeinschaftliche Glückseligkeit* (common happiness): statecraft, police, commerce/manufactures, household management, and finance. His manufacturing-centered, innovation-driven industrial policy has been called the eighteenth-century entrepreneurial state.

> Justi is the hinge of German cameralism: the bridge from its 17th-century founders to Sonnenfels — and the textbook source for Halle until 1819.
