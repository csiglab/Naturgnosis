# Xu Guangqi

> Xu Guangqi (徐光啓; April 24, 1562 – November 8, 1633), also known by his baptismal name Paul (Paul Siu), was a Chinese agronomist, astronomer, mathematician, scholar-official, and writer of the late Ming dynasty. He rose to Minister of Rites and Deputy Senior Grand Secretary, led the calendar reform that produced the Chongzhen calendar, and is counted among the Three Pillars of Chinese Catholicism.

| **Contribution** | **Description** |
| --- | --- |
| **Euclid in Chinese** | In 1607, with the Jesuit Matteo Ricci, translated the first parts of Euclid's *Elements* into Chinese — a landmark in introducing European mathematics and logic to China. |
| **Calendar reform** | Appointed in 1629 by the Chongzhen Emperor to lead the *Shixian* calendar reform, the first major scientific collaboration between Europe and the Far East; completed after his death as the Chongzhen calendar, which introduced Copernican, Galilean, and Tychonic astronomy. |
| **Agronomy** | Wrote the *Nongzheng Quanshu* (*Complete Treatise on Agriculture*, c. 700,000 characters), covering irrigation, fertilizers, famine relief, economic crops, sericulture, and Western-style irrigation. It was edited and published posthumously by Chen Zilong in 1639. |
| **Military science** | Wrote *Mr Xu's Amateur Observations* on military technique and advocated recruiting Western artillery, moving the court in 1629 to bring Portuguese cannon and gunners to the capital. |
| **Statecraft** | Advocated the "Rich Country and Strong Army" (富國強兵) program — a phrase later adopted by Meiji Japan as *fukoku kyōhei*. |
