# Śreṇi

> **Śreṇi** refers not necessarily to a prescribed organizational form, but to a **mode of economic aggregation**: the organization of multiple independent economic actors, productive capacities, or interests into a collective arrangement capable of coordinating activities that would otherwise be difficult or costly to perform individually.

## Formulation

### What social element type does this social instance belong to?

**Śreṇi belongs to the `Collective / Organization` social element type — a compound Interaction Unit with identity, membership, and rules, carrying its own agencies, roles, and practices.**

What individuates it is the modal character above: members enter as **independent** economic actors and remain so — the collective exists to coordinate high-cost activities (quality assurance, dispute settlement, pooled finance, collective bargaining), not to subsume its members. Its layer is **Ontic**: śreṇis existed extra-mentally, with seals, halls, treasuries, and account books. Its facet is economic.

Readable secondarily as a coordination `Activity`/`Mechanism` (aggregation-for-costly-coordination): that reading is prose here, not a second root; the tree below is typed once.

### What is this social instance?

> The canonical instance is the ancient Indian śreṇi (Sanskrit śreṇi, Prakrit seni): an association of traders, merchants, or artisans of one vocation, attested from the 6th century BCE (Pāṇini, Jātakas) to the 12th century CE, at its height under the Mauryan and Gupta empires. Mostly self-governing under a head (jeṭṭhaka, pamukha, pradhāna — removable by the membership for misconduct), assisted by a council of seniors and secretaries; its customary code (śreṇi-dharma) carried legal weight — the Manusmṛti counsels the king to respect it. Economically it procured raw materials, organized production, controlled quality, fixed wages and prices, and secured markets; with surplus it received deposits, lent money, and custodied religious endowments (the Vidisha ivory-carvers' śreṇi financed Sanchi's southern gateway). Scholars caution against equating it with the medieval European guild.
