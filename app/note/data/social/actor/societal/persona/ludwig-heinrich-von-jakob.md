# Ludwig Heinrich von Jakob

<img src="https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/Special:FilePath/Jacob.png"
     alt="Ludwig Heinrich von Jakob"
     width="200"
     align="left"
     style="margin: 0 25px 15px 0;">

**Ludwig Heinrich von Jakob** (1759–1827; ennobled 1816) was a German philosopher, political economist, and cameralist — professor of philosophy at Halle (1791), professor of the sciences of the state at Kharkov (1807), finance commissioner in St Petersburg (1809), and professor of Staatswissenschaften at Halle again from 1816.

He turned from Kantian philosophy to cameralist studies after 1798 and became one of the earliest German propagators of Adam Smith: his *Grundsätze der National-Oekonomie* (1805) made `Nationalökonomie` a standard term, and he systematized police science and public finance while insisting, in cameralist tradition, on the necessity of state economic and social action.

> His writings therefore show how German thinkers around 1800 received English liberal economics — translating Hume, Thornton, and Say — while recasting it as a doctrine of **organized productive capability under the state**.
