# Zhao Shukai

> **Zhao Shukai** (赵树凯; b. 1959) is a **Chinese official of rural policy and governance**. From 1982 to 1989 he worked at the Rural Policy Research Office of the CPC Central Committee Secretariat; from 1990 at the Development Research Centre of the State Council. A veteran drafter of China's rural policy documents and a chronicler of the reform-era policy process.

## Career

Zhao joined the Rural Policy Research Office of the CPC Central Committee Secretariat in 1982 and moved to the Development Research Centre of the State Council in 1990, where he held posts including Director General of the Rural Department's Organisation Research Office and Director General of the Information Centre. He held short visiting positions at ANU, CUHK, Duke, Harvard, Tübingen, and completed executive programmes at Harvard Kennedy School and Cambridge Judge. In 2006 he was the main presenter at a Politburo group study session.

## Rural reform and the hired-labour debate

In *Deng Xiaoping and Hired Labour* (WeChat, 26 Aug 2024), Zhao reconstructs how China accommodated private employment: rural experiments (e.g. Guangdong fishpond contracting), the Marx "eight-worker" threshold treated as a capitalist cutoff, divided senior leaders (Deng Xiaoping, Chen Yun, Wan Li, Hu Qiaomu), and the policy researchers—notably Du Runsheng—who kept contentious wording out of central documents. Restrictive instructions were interpreted flexibly and prohibitions left unenforced; the 1989 ban on Party members hiring labour was never formally repealed nor enforced.

## Official document writing

In *Notes on Official Document Writing* (2020 talk, published 2025), Zhao reflects on four decades drafting for leaders: writing "for public purposes, in someone else's name"; the craft as "dancing in shackles" (innovation within institutional constraint); platform support for researchers; learning from classical memorials and Western speeches; and persuading leaders so ideas become their own.
