# Norwegian Institute of International Affairs (NUPI)

> **NUPI** is a state research body that studies international affairs for Norwegian foreign policy: security and war, economy and development, and diplomacy and global governance. The Norwegian Parliament created it in 1959, explicitly on the model of Chatham House, and it has run on that logic ever since — a body inside the state whose professional work is kept independent of the government's position.

> That is the institute's whole design. It is answerable to the Ministry of Education and Research, its board is appointed by that ministry, and its findings are published whether or not they suit the government that funds it. Framed per [Philosophia Socialium et Operis](note.html?n=meta/philosophia-socialium-et-operis.md).

## Formulation

### What social element type does this social instance belong to?

**NUPI belongs to the `Collective / Organization` social element type — a compound unit with identity, membership, and rules.**

It is a body of about a hundred people in five research groups and two supporting departments, with a director and a board. The `Institution` reading is unusually strong here — the Chatham House model *is* an institutional design, and NUPI was created to reproduce it — so it is declared as a secondary reading under the multi-root rule and decomposed below as `Legitimacy` and `Norm / Regulation`.

### What is this social instance?

> An independent research institute for international affairs: political scientists, lawyers, and economists producing analysis, advising ministries, testifying in public, and convening the debate — funded by the Research Council, the Ministry of Foreign Affairs, the Ministry of Defence, and European and private sources.

**Layer.** **Ontic** — the institute, its staff, its board, and its publications exist extra-mentally. Its analyses are Noetic instruments; the expectation that a small state's foreign policy can be reasoned about in advance is the shared anticipation the institute serves.

**Facet.** **Ideational**, by dominant mechanism: the institute is reorganized by producing and circulating political knowledge, and its public events are the delivery form of it.

**Boundary.** The institute as a body: warrants, groups, roles, activities, rules, obligations, relations, resources, run, and present state. The international order it studies is not decomposed here, nor are the ministries it advises.

Lineage: post-war European institution-building in the study of international affairs → the Norwegian Parliament founding NUPI in 1959 on the Chatham House model, fusing an initiative from the parliamentary and foreign-ministry environment with one from a political-science chair → expansion from a foreign-policy information centre into a three-pillar research institute with an international guest-researcher programme → a body that the state both funds and uses, while insisting that its professional conclusions stay its own.
