# Johann Joachim Becher

<img src="https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/Special:FilePath/Johann%20Joachim%20Becher%201.jpg"
     alt="Johann Joachim Becher"
     width="220"
     align="left"
     style="margin: 0 25px 15px 0;">

**Johann Joachim Becher** (1635–1682) was a German physician, alchemist, and the most original theorist of early Austrian cameralism — imperial commercial counsellor (*Commerzienrat*) at Vienna from 1666, organizer of the *Kommerzkollegium*, founder of manufactures (silk, wool, textiles), and author of the phlogiston precursor *Physica subterranea* (1669).

His *Politischer Discurs* (1668) treats the state as an economic community of peasants, artisans, and merchants, develops the rudiments of a theory of market forms (monopoly, polypoly, propoly), and makes the promotion of productive forces — manufactures, workhouses, trading companies, commercial banks — the center of state action.

> Becher is the founding case of the cameralist thesis that runs through this corpus: national wealth is **organized productive capability**, and the state's task is to build it.
