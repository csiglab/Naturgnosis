# Thomas Hobbes


> ..
> 

## Work

| **Work** | **Year** | **Type** | **Description** |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| *Elementorum Philosophiae Sectio Prima: De Corpore* | 1655 | Philosophy / Science | Early work on natural philosophy, physics, and materialism; lays the groundwork for Hobbes’ mechanistic view of nature. |
| *De Cive (On the Citizen)* | 1642 | Political Philosophy | Explores social contract theory, the nature of government, and civil society; emphasizes the necessity of a strong sovereign to avoid chaos. |
| *Leviathan* | 1651 | Political Philosophy | Hobbes’ most famous work; develops a full theory of the social contract, justifying absolute sovereignty as essential to prevent the “state of nature” (chaos and violence). |
| *De Corpore Politico* | 1655 | Political Philosophy | Continuation of Hobbes’ exploration of political structures, sovereignty, and law; builds on ideas from *De Cive*. |
| *Behemoth* | 1681 | Political History / Commentary | Historical analysis of the English Civil War; explains the causes and consequences of political instability. |
| *De Homine* | 1658 | Philosophy / Psychology | Treatise on human nature, perception, and psychology; complements *De Corpore* and *De Cive* by explaining the mechanics of human behavior. |
