# Public Policy

> This note examines the nature of `Policy`, the criteria that distinguish `Public Policy` from other forms of policy, and their structural relationships with `Strategy`, `Rule`, and `Plan`.

> **Policy** is an authoritative specification of action that provides normative guidance for subsequent decisions and operations. It translates a `Strategy`'s selection of ends and means into a governing directive, within which `Rule`s prescribe or constrain conduct and `Plan`s organize the execution of intended actions.

> **Public Policy** is a `Policy` established or adopted by a public authority to govern decisions and interventions concerning matters of public concern.

## Formulation

### What Is a `Public Policy`?

> A **Public Policy** is an authoritative specification of public action that establishes the normative direction, objectives, and prescriptions governing interventions within a domain of public concern.

### What Makes Something a `Public Policy`?

An instance qualifies as a `Public Policy` when it satisfies the following constitutive criteria:

| Criterion                | Description                                                                                              |
| ------------------------ | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Public Authority**     | Derives its authority from a recognized public institution or legitimate public decision-making process. |
| **Public Concern**       | Addresses a matter within the domain of public affairs.                                                  |
| **Action Specification** | Prescribes, directs, constrains, or authorizes public action.                                            |
| **Normative Force**      | Establishes a governing directive, commitment, or framework for public decisions and conduct.            |

### What social element type does this social instance belong to?

**Policy belongs to the `Policy` social element type (Action category, `Action Guidance` branch, Normative layer)** — a deliberate course of action adopted by an authority, guiding subsequent decisions. Guidance orients action; organization arranges it.

It is distinct from `Strategy` (the ends/means selection over a horizon), from `Rule` (a specific codified constraint prescribing or prohibiting conduct), from `Plan` (a concrete arrangement of future actions specifying ends, means, and sequence), and from `Program` (a coordinated set of projects under one objective).

### What is this social instance?

> The authorized course: an authority declares where collective doing shall head, and the declaration binds what follows — instruments are chosen, agencies tasked, rules laid down, resources committed, and results reviewed against the mandate. Domains run from economic to foreign policy; every domain decomposes into its policies, and every policy into mandate, instrument, goal, target, agency, rule, and review. The course is operationalized through legal, regulatory, programmatic, or fiscal instruments.

## What is the structure of a Public Policy?

| Category                   | Element                             | Description                                                                                            |
| -------------------------- | ----------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| Problem Definition         | Public problem                      | Identifies the condition or situation requiring public attention.                                      |
| Objective Definition       | Objectives                          | Specify the intended conditions or changes.                                                            |
| Normative Foundations      | Principles, values, and obligations | Establish the principles, values, and obligations informing the policy.                                |
| Intervention Design        | Policy instruments                  | Provide the means through which public authorities can intervene.                                      |
| Intervention Design        | Target population                   | Identifies the people, organizations, or entities whose circumstances or conduct the policy addresses. |
| Institutional Organization | Institutional arrangements          | Assign authority, responsibilities, and relationships among participating institutions.                |
| Resource Organization      | Resources                           | Establish the financial, human, material, and informational means available.                           |
| Implementation             | Implementation arrangements         | Organize the activities through which the policy is put into effect.                                   |
| Monitoring and Evaluation  | Monitoring and evaluation           | Establish how implementation, performance, and effects are assessed.                                   |


## What Is the Life Cycle of a Public Policy?

| Order | Stage                           | Description                                                                      | Action Set                                                       |
| ----: | ------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------- |
|     1 | **Problem Identification**      | Identify and characterize a condition requiring public attention.                | Problem analysis, evidence gathering, causal diagnosis.          |
|     2 | **Agenda Setting**              | Establish the problem as a priority for public decision-making.                  | Priority setting, issue framing, agenda prioritization.          |
|     3 | **Policy Formulation**          | Develop and assess alternative policy specifications.                            | Alternative generation, impact analysis, feasibility assessment. |
|     4 | **Policy Adoption**             | Authorize a selected policy through the applicable decision-making process.      | Deliberation, selection, approval, formal authorization.         |
|     5 | **Implementation**              | Translate the policy specification into operational arrangements and actions.    | Resource allocation, task assignment, coordination, execution.   |
|     6 | **Monitoring**                  | Observe implementation and measure performance against established expectations. | Data collection, indicator measurement, compliance tracking.     |
|     7 | **Evaluation**                  | Assess the policy's relevance, effectiveness, efficiency, and effects.           | Outcome analysis, impact assessment, performance evaluation.     |
|     8 | **Policy Revision**             | Modify the policy in response to evaluation findings or changing conditions.     | Policy reassessment, specification adjustment, reauthorization.  |
|     9 | **Continuation or Termination** | Maintain, replace, or discontinue the policy as appropriate.                     | Continuation, replacement, termination, transition.              |

## QA

### Why use the term `Gubernata`?

> Spanish *política* means both policy and politics: the purposeful, instrumental course of action and the contestation for power. Where the language fuses them, the instrument is read as partisanship and the contest as strategy — see [The Policy-Politics Conflation Hypothesis](note.html?n=general/the-policy-politics-conflation-hypothesis-language-structure-and-development-outcomes.md). *Gubernata* — Latin neuter plural of *gubernare*, to steer; the things steered — names only the policy side: the authorized course operationalized through instruments, binding on action regardless of who contests power. Use it wherever Spanish would force the ambiguous *política*, and keep `Politics` for the contestation itself.

### What Is the Relationship Between Implementation and Instrumentalization?

Instrumentalization occurs during both formulation and implementation:

* **Formulation:** Selection and design of policy instruments.
* **Implementation:** Operationalization and configuration of those instruments to put the policy into effect.

**Instrumentalization concerns the means; implementation concerns putting the policy into effect.**
