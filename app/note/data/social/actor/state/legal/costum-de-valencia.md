# Costum de València

> The **Costum de València** (plural *Costums de València*) was the codified customary-law corpus promulgated by James I of Aragon after the Christian conquest of Valencia (1238–1239) to regulate the city and, by extension, the new Kingdom of Valencia. A *costum* is customary law raised to a binding norm — akin to a Castilian *fuero* — and the code has been called "the first legal code of the new kingdom and also its birth certificate" (Furió). Recast and renamed the **Furs de València** around 1250, it was sworn at the first Valencian Corts in 1261.

### What social element type does this social instance belong to?

**Costum de València belongs to the `Norm / Regulation` social element type (Normative category)** — a shared protocol stabilizing interaction, here codified into a written legal code (*fueros*) with binding force. Its layer is **Normative**, read on the Synontic stratum: the code's authority exists through recognized law, not merely as a written text. Its facet is **legal**, with a political face — the dominant mechanism is the constitution of a kingdom's legal order and the allocation of rights and obligations. In the social graph it is filed under the category `Rule` (explicitly codified constraint: law, regulation).

It is distinct from the `Institution` it helps constitute — the foral regime with its Justícia and Corts — from the actor that promulgated it (the crown of Aragon), and from a `Charter` (a founding instrument). The code is the rule-corpus those arrangements apply. Readable secondarily as a concrete epistemic artifact (a legal text and its manuscripts), a reading kept as prose and cross-linked, not as a second root.

### What is this social instance?

> A body of codified customs and royal privileges that fixed the law of the capital and claimed to be the general law of the realm. It was compiled, probably during or just after the siege of the city, by a team of jurists of the royal chancery headed by Pere Albert, canon of Barcelona Cathedral, who took as their base the rubrics of the first nine books of Justinian's *Code*; the text was divided into two parts (books I–V and VI–IX) of roughly 750 chapters (fueros) each, drawing also on the *Digest*, the *Liber Iudiciorum*, and the *Costums de Lleida*, with concordances to *De batalla facienda*, the *Liber feudorum maior*, the *Usatges de Barcelona*, and the Decretals.

> First named as the *Costum* in a privilege of 21 May 1239, it was promulgated around the end of 1238 or the spring of 1239 — the king granting a complete code to the city with the same purpose as the extensive fueros being granted in the north of Valencia, but created *ex novo* on a conquered territory out of the *ius commune*, Roman, and canon law. Around 1250 James I recast it to make it the single law of the kingdom under the name *Furs de València*; the prologue forbade judges to use any other customs "en la ciutat o alcun loch del terme altre del regne de València". Between 1240 and 1261 it was extended by royal privileges and translated into the vernacular, the new redaction being promulgated and sworn at the first Valencian Corts. In practice it applied fully only in the city and the places the king held directly: lands granted to nobles and churchmen followed the Fuero de Zaragoza or the *Costums de Lleida*, so the kingdom remained a jurisdictional mosaic.
