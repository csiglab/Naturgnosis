# Philosophia Naturalis et Operis

> Reality is the total set of phenomena, entities, and processes that exist or occur, independently of an observer's perception or conceptualization, and which can, in principle, interact, change, or produce effects.

> Philosophia Naturalis is the scaffolding used to parse the **ontic element set of reality**: cutting the flux into segments, typing the elements that populate them, and licensing their decomposition.

> This note is a guide connecting Reality with its Description — delimitation, segmenting, typing, and validation. It is the complement of Philosophia Artium Technicarum et Operis and Philosophia Artium Epistemicarum et Operis: where the technicarum note systematizes how reality is transformed for a purpose, and the epistemicarum note systematizes how inquiry warrants a construction about it, this one systematizes the observer-independent furniture that any transformation and any construction must answer to.

> Field observation is a training ground for rigorous natural description because it enforces segment-bound, level-aware, limitation-checked affirmations about what actually exists. The value lies in transferring this ontic discipline — rather than its specific catalogs — into more complex domains like social systems.

## Formulation

### What is the nature of `natural practice`?

> Natural practice: (Reality; Observation, Inference) → Segmented Ontic Description & Validation.

> A natural activity is any operation performed by an observer to cut, characterize, and validate a segment of observer-independent reality at the appropriate level of organization.

> The goal of natural description is (1) to construct and maintain the scaffolding mechanisms of description — the segment, the level of organization, the limitation checklist — and (2) to type the ontic elements that populate the segment, which obtain independently of any description of them.

### What is the role of `nature` in human experience?

> Nature is what every description is answerable to: the constraint space that resists, confirms, and outruns every representation.

> Naming an ontic element enables explanation, prediction, and intervention: it compresses observations into a claim about what is there, and it disciplines that claim by exposing it to what observation and inference can refute.

> What is worth describing are the recurring patterns that buy such compression — and the fundamental limitations (see the checklist below) that bound what any description can achieve.

> The labour of natural description goes hand in hand with a layout of reality — that is its segment template: every descriptive move presupposes a cut over the event stream, and every ontic distinction earns its keep through explanatory, predictive, or interventive use.

### What grounds `natural practice`?

> Natural practice is grounded in the observer-independent existence of what is described: entities and processes with causal effects that obtain whether or not anyone observes or models them.

> Foundation - **Reality -** The ontological substrate comprising the physical, chemical, biological, and earth structures that constrain and outrun description. Basalt cliff, folded protein, monsoon circulation, mycelial network.

### What is the reality we seek to describe?

> Observer-independent reality as delimited above: entities and processes with causal effects, dynamic and interconnected, accessible imperfectly through observation and inference. Reality is decomposed for description in the Ontic Order below.

### At which level of organization do we describe?

> The hierarchical stratification of structural and functional components, from microscopic interactions to emergent macroscopic behaviors — crucial when describing systems with multiscale dynamics, memory, or adaptive laws.

- **Micro-level:** Individual elements and their local interactions.
- **Meso-level:** Subsystems, modules, clusters of interactions.
- **Macro-level:** Emergent patterns, global behavior, systemic properties.

> Description is **hierarchical**: different descriptions require different levels of analysis. Every ontic element carries its level of organization, and the same name at two levels denotes two nodes, decomposed separately and linked.

### Which limitations bound every natural description?

> Apply as a checklist to each decomposed instance: Complexity, Nonlinearity, Uncertainty, Chaos, Emergence, Scale, Data Availability, Computational Complexity, Irreducibility, Partial Observability, Non-Repeatability, Measurement Disturbance, Many-Body coupling. Each is an ontic property of the segment, not a failure of method — record which ones the instance exhibits.

### What is the ontology of the target?

> What is reality like as parsable? Which elements make it up? How do we identify them and how do they hang together? Ontology answers by constraining what can be typed; its operative constructs for parsing (substances, entities, mechanisms, events, processes, states, trajectories, phenomena, properties, aspects, spaces, times, environments, substrates, constraints) live in the Ontic Order table below.

> The table covers the **Ontic layer only**. Synontic elements — norms, institutions, protocols, affordances, significant relations — belong to Philosophia Socialium et Operis; information belongs to Philosophia Artium Epistemicarum et Operis.

### What Is the Structure of an `Ontic Element`'s Decomposition Tree?

> How can we characterize the ontic aspect of human experience?

> A taxonomy (conceptual structure) that renders the *observer-independent* element set of reality intelligible: what exists, what occurs, what is borne, what is situated, and what bounds it.

> An `Ontic Element` is a concept that denotes an element of observer-independent reality, analytically identifiable as an instance of an ontic element type and capable of being recursively decomposed into other ontic elements through explicitly typed relationships. Note: This is a deliberately loose definition of the reality denoted by the concept.

#### What is the fundamental structure of an ontic element's `Decomposition Tree`?

```text
Ontic Element (parent)

```

> Instance paths use `(→ Relationship Type)` throughout. Typing still reads from grouping segments; the link name states the relation asserted between them, and every link must take a type from the relationship table.

### Which are the tags(adjectives) that can be used to enrich the nodes of the decomposition?

> `Tags` are analytical qualifiers attached to a node to characterize its properties, composition, relationships, formation, or mode of operation without creating a separate subtype for every variation.

Usage rules

- Tags qualify nodes; they do not replace the underlying type. For example, a Weathered [Substantive] remains a substantive.
- Use a subtype when the distinction defines a recognized kind. Use a tag when the distinction qualifies an instance or can apply across multiple kinds.
- Allow multiple tags per node. A `Process` can be `Irreversible`, `Dissipative`, and `Coupled` simultaneously.
- Do not confuse a tag with a relationship. `Probabilistic` describes the dynamics; `(→ Generation)` expresses a relation.
- Avoid redundant tags. If a subtype already entails a characteristic in the intended context, do not add the same tag unless it provides meaningful additional information.
- Distinguish a known absence from missing information. `Non-Repeatable` should be used only when the failure of repetition is established, not merely because repetition has not been attempted.

## How to decompose any natural instance?

> A decomposition recursively expands an ontic instance tree from its root: bind the root instance, then grow its subtree by attaching only links the `Permitted Link Forms` table and the relationship table allow, down to instance leaves. The reference tables above are scaffolding — necessary but not sufficient, and never normative: they say which links *may* appear, not which decomposition is correct for any given element. Choosing instances, depth, and stopping point remains the decomposer's work on the instance itself.

> See the worked case in QA below (### (Case Study) What is the recursively decomposed instance tree of a Quarry?). Read the case table as the worked in-path-typed tree: the empty table here is filled the same way, typing each row from its nearest enclosing grouping segment and labelling every link.

> Note: Instance rows in the Instance Tree Path name instances, never types. Type names appear only as backticked grouping segments and never terminate a branch.

> **Migration note:** the earlier Tabular view (six categories over seventeen element types) and Recursive view (placeholder binding with spine expansion) are replaced by the `Permitted Link Forms` table, the relationship table, and the path-typed instance table above. Legacy instance notes still cite the old views, style instances in bold, and leave links unlabelled; their natural type names survive as kinds of the ontic types, so their rows remain readable, but they are to be rewritten in this form when touched.

> **Constructing the instance path:** (1) trace — write the full path from `Ontic Element` through the licensed kind chain to the instance's type; (2) label — give every link its `(→ Relationship Type)` from the relationship table; (3) bind — name the root instance; (4) extend — append each child by exactly one licensed, labelled link; (5) check — every leaf resolves to an instance, no bare type terminates a branch, and no bare arrow remains.

**Rules:**

- **Form of the Table:** `Instance Tree Path | Description`.
- **Instance Tree Path:** A unique, recursively extended path identifying an instance within the decomposition tree. It is the **full path from `Ontic Element`**: it begins at `Ontic Element`, descends through the licensed kind chain to the ontic element type to which the instance being decomposed belongs or is related, then appends each successive child instance through an explicitly labelled relationship, using the form `` `Parent` `(→ Relationship Type)` Child ``. Each successive path must preserve the complete path from `Ontic Element` to the current instance. Type grouping segments are styled `` `code` ``; instance nodes are plain Title Case text.
- **Relationship Labelling:** Every link between a parent and child must name its relation from the relationship table, using the notation `(→ Relationship Type)`. Bare arrows are not permitted, and a label never replaces the grouping segment that carries a type.
- **Permitted Links:** Every type-to-type relationship must conform to a link form allowed by the `Permitted Link Forms` table. A link form licenses a possible relationship; it does not establish that the relationship actually exists in the instance being decomposed.
- **Layer Gate:** Only Ontic-layer types may appear as grouping segments. A synontic element decomposes under the socialium guide and a meta-ontic element under the epistemicarum guide; it may be cross-linked, never typed here.
- **Instance Grounding:** Each child must represent an element of the particular instance being decomposed, rather than merely a type that could theoretically occur within it. Type labels may be used to identify or organize instances, but classification alone does not establish that a corresponding instance exists.
- **Recursive Expansion:** Any ontic instance identified in the tree may itself be decomposed recursively; any row may serve as a decomposition root.
- **Decomposition Scope:** Select the aspects of the instance that need to be represented. A decomposition need not include every conceivable aspect of reality; it must adequately cover the aspects relevant to its stated purpose.
- **Description:** Describe what the instance is, what role it plays, or why it is included in the decomposition. Descriptions clarify the instance but do not substitute for explicit typing or labelling.
- **Stopping Condition:** Stop expanding a branch when its instance is sufficiently specified for the decomposition's purpose, when further expansion is unnecessary, or when the available evidence does not justify additional decomposition. A leaf is a stopping point in the current decomposition, not necessarily an intrinsically indivisible element. Every leaf must resolve to an ontic instance.
- **Completeness:** A decomposition is complete relative to its declared scope and stopping criteria, not merely because every branch terminates. Relevant constituents, relations, structures, processes, states, and other aspects must not be omitted simply because the tree is syntactically valid.
- **Unlicensed or Uncertain Relationships:** If a necessary relationship is not covered by the reference table, do not silently invent a permitted link or substitute an unrelated one. Identify the gap and extend the reference table when justified. If the existence of a relationship is uncertain, mark that uncertainty rather than presenting the relationship as established.
- **Instance Identity:** Distinguish different instances even when they share the same type or description. The full `Instance Tree Path` identifies an instance's position within the particular decomposition; it does not, by itself, establish a globally unique identity outside that tree.
- **Tree Structure:** Each non-root row must extend an existing parent path by exactly one child relationship. The same instance may appear in multiple branches when it plays different roles in the decomposition; each occurrence must have its own path. Ensure a rich set of intermediate (internal) nodes — both types and instances — aiding understanding: composition, causation, regulation, transformation, and scale relationships.
- **Level of Organization:** Every instance row carries its level (micro, meso, macro). A same-named element at another level is a second node with its own path, decomposed separately and linked.
- **Limitation Checklist:** For each decomposed instance, record which limitation factors of the checklist above the segment exhibits. They are ontic properties of the segment, never method failures, and they belong to the decomposition's scope, not to a separate note.
- **Non-Normativity of Reference Tables:** The reference tables constrain which link forms may be used. They do not prescribe a universal decomposition order, determine which children must be included, or guarantee that a decomposition is conceptually adequate.
- **Well-formedness:** an instance decomposition is well-formed when every non-root row extends an existing path by exactly one link — a `(→ Instance)` step from a type segment, or a type-to-type link the `Permitted Link Forms` table licenses — every link is labelled with a type from the relationship table, every grouping segment carries a licensed type, branch order is never skipped, and every leaf resolves to an instance. Any row may serve as a decomposition root for recursive decomposition.
- **No repetition:** The root's own type must not be unnecessarily repeated as an intermediate grouping node. Exception: a same-type segment is allowed when it scopes a genuine instance family that would otherwise hang untyped (e.g. a `Natural System` grouping scoping the sub-systems family inside a Catchment decomposition); a same-type segment with only generic description and no scoping function stays forbidden.
- **Well-Form Instance Tree Path Rule:** Ensure the decomposition provides a rich set of intermediate (internals) nodes - both - type and instances, aiding understanding. The set of intermediate instance nodes representing relationships such as subtyping, composition, causation, regulation, or other useful structural relationships.
* Style Rules for Intermediate Nodes
  * **Instances:** Style intermediate nodes that represent actual ontic instances as plain text (no adornment).
  * **Naming:** Name every instance node in Title Case — capitalize every whitespace- or hyphen-separated word (`bench face` -> `Bench Face`, `groundwater inflow` -> `Groundwater Inflow`); preserve established all-caps acronyms (`DNA`, `pH`). Type segments keep their table casing. The rule governs node names in Instance Tree Paths only; descriptions stay sentence-case prose.
  * **Composite Instance Naming**: A *composite* instance — a nested ensemble (an `Ontic Element Set`, `Natural System`, `Ecosystem`, or sub-domain set), not an ordinary object, substance, or mechanism — takes a name ending in `Set` once it sits at a depth greater than 2. Depth counts every segment of the Instance Tree Path, backticked type groupings included, the root type grouping being depth 1. At depth 2 or less the suffix is permitted but never required: `Ontic Element Set` -> `Reef System Set` (depth 2) keeps it, `Ontic Element Set` -> `Catchment Biota Set` (depth 3) requires it.
  * **Types:** Style bare ontic element types used as grouping nodes as `` `code` ``.
  * **Links:** Style every connector as `` `(→ Relationship Type)` `` — `(→ Kind)`, `(→ Part)`, `(→ Generation)`, … — and never write a bare `→`.
  * **Distinction:** Never style an instance and a type in the same way; the distinction must be immediately visible.
  * **Grouping types:** A type used only to group instances is not itself an instance and must not terminate a branch.
  * The path link - is `(→ Relationship Type)`.

## QA

### Where does the epistemic scaffolding stop and the ontic description start?

> At the cut: everything on the template side (segment boundaries, variables, scales, representation forms) is scaffolding and belongs to the epistemic decomposition; everything affirmed about the segment's furniture belongs here. When in doubt, ask which side would change if the instruments changed — what survives is ontic.

### How do technique-laden natural instances decompose?

> The instrument technically (`guideline/ambiguity_resolution.md`), the formation naturally: decompose the device under the technical grammar, the measured reality under this one, and cross-link at the Observation Interface.

### Why label every link?

> Because a bare arrow hides which relation is asserted. `(→ Part)`, `(→ Generation)`, `(→ Predication)`, and `(→ Bound)` are different claims about the same pair of nodes: a mechanism generating a process is not a part of it, and a constraint bounding a system is not one of its attributes. The label is what makes a path checkable against the `Permitted Link Forms` table and what keeps two trees from silently sharing a connector they do not share.

### What is the ultimate generic link form?

> `<<Ontic Element>> (→ <<Relationship Type>>) <<Ontic Element>>` — the template every permitted link form specializes: a parent type, a labelled relation, a child type. Slots are bound per decomposition: `<<Ontic Element>>` := the type of the element being decomposed (or of the child, in an inner slot), `<<Relationship Type>>` := a label from the relationship table. It states the form, not a licence: the specific rows decide which parent–child pairs are permitted and by which label.

### How does this `Ontic Order` relate to the epistemicarum's `Ontic` link form?

> This note holds the type table; the epistemicarum's `Epistemic Element (→ Kind) Ontic` points here. There is one ontic grammar, cited where inquiry needs it, never duplicated as a rival table. A natural note types its rows here and cites the epistemicarum guide only for how inquiry targets the result.

### Where do synontic elements decompose?

> Under Philosophia Socialium et Operis. Norms, institutions, protocols, affordances, and significant relations are real, but their existence depends on distributed cognition and interaction; typing them here would conflate the layer that exists independently of observers with the layer that exists through them. An ontic tree records only the bearer, the substrate, and the effects — the norm itself is cross-linked, never typed as an ontic element.

### Where does information decompose?

> Under Philosophia Artium Epistemicarum et Operis, on its meta-ontic side. Information is a construct about ontic elements, not one of them; a signal, a record, or a dataset is an epistemic construction carried by an ontic substrate. Decompose the substrate here and the information there.

### Why is there no `Processual` type?

> Because it would duplicate `Process`. The ontic template distinguishes the existential category *Processual* (occurrences extended in time) from the dynamical type `Process` (causally connected event sequences generating a state-trajectory), but they name the same occurrences. One type per structural job: `Process` carries the structure, and `processual` survives as a tag on the instance.

### Is `Entity` the same as `Substantive`?

> No. `Substantive` is the self-identical existent with bounded persistence; `Entity` is the unit treated as a thing in modeling — a bearer that can be counted, referred to, and related. Most natural particulars answer to both (a redwood is a substantive and an entity), but the readings differ: a vortex is an entity without being a substantive, since it is a pattern sustained by flow, and a rock formation is a substantive counted as one entity. Type the reading the decomposition is for.

### Is a `Limitation Factor` really ontic?

> It is kept as an ontic element by decision. It names a standing of the segment — its hidden joint network, its one-off history — not a defect of the method that observes it, and typing it keeps the bound attached to the instance instead of floating in prose. It is a kind of `Constraint`, not a peer of the furniture, so it never competes with the elements it bounds. The checklist above remains the recording gate; the type exists so a decomposition can point at the bound when the bound itself must be named.

### Why is the category `Ontic Element Set` required?

> An **Ontic Element Set** is required to represent coherent ensembles whose members belong to different ontic types but are held together by a common segment, substrate, or environment. Without it, the taxonomy can describe the individual elements of a reef — corals, carbonate substrate, currents, thermal limits — but it lacks a type for representing **the ensemble itself**.

### What is the difference between an `Ontic Element` and a `Substantive`?

> `Ontic Element` is the category: any element of observer-independent reality that can be analytically identified by an ontic element type and decomposed by the permitted links. `Substantive` is one type within it — a self-identical existent with bounded persistence. Processes, events, states, properties, mechanisms, constraints, and limitation factors are ontic elements without being substantives. Every substantive is an ontic element; not every ontic element is a substantive.

### What distinguishes `Natural System`, `Ecosystem`, and `Complex System`?

> `Ecosystem` and `Complex System` are kinds of `Natural System`, itself a kind of `Substantive`; the distinction is the emphasis of the description. `Natural System` is the general bounded set of interacting components. `Ecosystem` emphasizes interdependence with an environment and semi-permeable boundaries. `Complex System` names systems whose collective behavior resists component-level reduction. An instance may answer to several: a catchment is a system, readable as an ecosystem and, at the scale of its flood response, as a complex system. Read it under the type its decomposition is for; when the reading is contested, grow one tree per reading rather than merging them.

> Note: Ontic Element Type is recursive. The type of order of every ontic element is the **Ontic Order**. The `Permitted Link Forms` table below licenses how an element of one type decomposes into elements of another, and the **relationship table** names the connector that states each licensed relation. Template slots (`<<…>>`), where used, are bound with `:=` instead of instances. Relationships use `(→ Relationship Type)` throughout.

> **Note on layers:** the template of reality divides into **Ontic** (elements that exist independently of observers), **Synontic** (elements that exist through distributed cognition and interaction), and **Meta Ontic** (information). This guide licenses the **Ontic** layer only: a norm, institution, protocol, or affordance decomposes under the socialium guide, and information decomposes under the epistemicarum guide. No row carries two layers.

> **Note on levels:** every ontic element carries its level of organization (micro: elements and local interactions; meso: subsystems and modules; macro: emergent patterns and systemic behavior). The same name at two levels denotes two nodes, decomposed separately and linked. Level is a gate on every decomposition, not a type.

> **Note on generic composites:** Different names may be used to refer to generic composites of ontic elements, such as *biome*, *catchment*, *reef system*, or similar concepts. Each carries its own semantics. For simplicity we use **`Ontic Element Set`** as the generic type; ensemble scopes (biomes, catchments, reef systems) bind the placeholder as `Ontic Element Set`. `Substantive` heads the existential spine, with `Natural System` and `Organism` among its natural kinds.

> Recursion is introduced by allowing a composite to have another composite as an instance: an `Ontic Element Set` may contain `Ontic Element Set`s (a reef within a catchment); a `Natural System` may contain further systems and whole sets besides its objects. Nesting composites in composites is what makes the type tree recursive.
