# Philosophia Artium Epistemicarum et Operis

> Reality is the total set of phenomena, entities, and processes that exist or occur, independently of an observer’s perception or conceptualization, and which can, in principle, interact, change, or produce effects.

> Philosophia Artium Epistemicarum is the scaffolding used to parse, and render intelligible, reality.

> This note is a guide connecting Reality (Nature) with its Representation — modeling, computation, formal systems, algorithms, observers, and representations. It is the complement of Philosophia Artium Technicarum et Operis: where that note systematizes intervention in reality, this one systematizes how reality becomes intelligible.

> Physics is a training ground for rigorous modeling because it enforces precise, testable, and feedback-rich representations of reality. The value lies in transferring this epistemic discipline — rather than its specific models — into more complex domains like social systems.

## Index

## Formulation

### What is the nature of epistemic practice?

> Epistemology: (Reality; Ontology) → Description & Validation.

> An epistemic activity is any operation performed by an observer to construct, transform, evaluate, or apply knowledge about reality.

> The goal of epistemology is (1) to construct and maintain the scaffolding mechanisms of inquiry, and (2) to construct, validate, and derived a set of  Concrete Epistemic Artifact Set (DCESA) that represents reality.

### What is the role of the `episteme` in human experience?

> The episteme renders reality intelligible: it compresses observations into descriptions that explain, predict, and enable intervention — and it disciplines thinking through formal artifacts rather than mental representations alone.

> Humanity identifies aspects of reality worth describing by discovering recurring patterns that enable explanation, prediction, intervention, or compression of experience. A worthy aspect compresses observations of reality by minimizing description length while maximizing our ability to intervene.

### What ground 'epistemic practice'?

> Epistemic practice is grounded in an observer-independent reality accessed through observation and inference.

> **Ontology and epistemology are the right hand and left hand of knowledge construction: they are mutually constraining duals of the same epistemic act.**
>
> - **Ontology** = what *is* (constraint space of reality)
> - **Epistemology** = how it is *accessed/represented* (constraint space of knowing)
> - They are not separate domains, but **co-dependent operators on the same reality-model interface.**

### What is the reality we seek to represent?

> Observer-independent reality: it exists whether perceived or modeled; it is composed of entities and processes with causal effects; it is dynamic and interconnected, producing emergent patterns; and it is accessible — imperfectly — through observation and inference constrained by what actually exists.

> Reality is decomposed for inquiry in the Ontic Order tree below.

### At which level of organization do we inquire?

> The hierarchical stratification of structural and functional components, from microscopic interactions to emergent macroscopic behaviors — crucial when modeling systems with multiscale dynamics, memory, or adaptive laws.

- **Micro-level:** Individual elements and their local interactions.
- **Meso-level:** Subsystems, modules, clusters of interactions.
- **Macro-level:** Emergent patterns, global behavior, systemic properties.
- Systems thinking is **hierarchical**: different questions require different levels of analysis.

### What is the ontology of the target?

> What is reality like? Which elements make it up? How do we identify them? How do we think deeply about change — and about change as an element of reality itself? Ontology answers by constraining what can be known; its constructs for parsing (segments, entities, processes, phenomena) live in the Artifact tree below, and its full template in the Ontic Order branch of the table.

### How can we characterize the epistemical aspect of human experience?

> A taxonomy (conceptual structure) that renders the *inquiry-oriented* and *representation-oriented* practice of agents intelligible.

> The labour of epistemic practice goes hand in hand with a layout of reality — that is its ontic template: every epistemic move presupposes an ontic parsing, and every ontic distinction earns its keep through epistemic use.

> Note: Epistemic Element Type is recursive. Its elements are read under two types of order: the **Epistemic Order** (inquiry: orientation, agency, target, access, representation (including constitution), construction, operation, evaluation, communication, infrastructure, and conditions, plus `Aggregate` collections) and the **Ontic Order** (segments of reality and the existential/dynamical layering of what *is*). The single table below holds both branch-rooted trees, each growing from `(root)`. Each branch root carries its path and child rows extend it; child descriptions state the role-in-parent, canonical definitions live on root rows. Template rows (`Meta`, and the `Aggregate` recursion rows) carry `<<…>>` slots bound with `:=` instead of instances. `Constraint` appears once per constraining domain — evaluation, conditions, and substrate — read it with its parent. Scaffold rows are those supporting, structuring, or validating inquiry; target rows (domains, segments, observables, objects), and all ontic content rows, are inquiry's objects and targets. Type paths use `->` throughout.

> This tries to capture the epistemic circuit  -- Why? → Who? → What? → How accessed? → What is done? → What is produced? → How judged? → Under what conditions? → What supports it?

## How to decompose any epistemic instance?

> An epistemic instance is decomposed by identifying the concrete epistemic elements that observe, encode, represent, transform, validate, or sustain that particular instance. Each identified element is then classified according to its epistemic element type and may itself be recursively decomposed according to the decomposition rules of that type.

> See the worked case in QA below (### (Case Study) What is the recursively decomposed instance tree of Arithmetic?). Read the case table as the worked in-path-typed tree: the empty table here is filled the same way, typing each row from its grouping segments and the declared root binding.

> Begin with the ontic questionnaire — which entities, substances, mechanisms, processes, events, and phenomena constitute the fundamental ontic elements of the target science? — then type each answer below.

> Note: The Instance Tree Path only contains instances — not epistemic element types.

> **Epistemic Element Type Tree Path:** the expansion path typing one instance row (fourth column) — `(root) := <<Epistemic Element>>` bound to the row's table type, continued by exactly one licensed expansion: a spine-ordered suffix or a composite nesting. In other words, a Tree Path is what the single table generates. It is a type-level path (`->`), never to be confused with the Instance Tree Path (first column), which strings instances with `→`.

> **Constructing the path:** (1) type the instance — find its row in the table above; (2) bind — write `(root) := <<Epistemic Element>>` as that type; (3) extend — continue with exactly one licensed expansion from the bound position (spine-ordered suffix or composite nesting under the bound root); (4) check — single types and spine-ordered chains always license; anything else must match a nested composite.

The tree is governed by the following rules:

* **Root:** The root is the epistemic element type to which the epistemic instance being decomposed belongs or is related.
* **Structure:** Intermediate nodes provide the structure needed to organize the decomposition - they can be epistemic instances - or epistemic types. Epistemic Types cannot be the final nodes in the tree - may be used as grouping nodes, but they are not themselves instances.
* **Leaves:** Every leaf must resolve to an epistemic instance.
* **Typing:** Every instance is typed — by its fourth-column Tree Path where the table carries one, otherwise by its nearest enclosing grouping segment, with the decomposition root's type declared once. Rows carrying `<<…>>` placeholders (`<<Epistemic Element>>`, `Epistemic Construction Set`) are templates, not grouping types: bind the slot (`:=`) instead of nesting instances beneath them.
* **Recursion:** Any epistemic instance identified in the tree may itself be decomposed recursively.
* **Well-formedness:** an instance decomposition is well-formed when every instance row's type path is a path the table generates: bind `(root) := <<Epistemic Element>>` to the root instance's type and continue down-branch in order; branch order is never skipped. Any row may serve as a decomposition root for recursive decomposition.
* **No repetition:** The root's own type must not be unnecessarily repeated as an intermediate grouping node. Exception: a same-type segment is allowed when it scopes a genuine instance family that would otherwise hang untyped (e.g. an `Epistemic Standard` grouping scoping the standards family inside an Arithmetic decomposition, whose fourth-column counterpart is `(root) := <<Epistemic Element>> -> ... -> Epistemic Standard`); a same-type segment with only generic description and no scoping function stays forbidden.
* **Well-Form Instance Tree Path Rule:** Ensure the decomposition provides a rich set of intermediate (internals) nodes - both - type and instances, aiding understanding. The set of intermediate instance nodes representing relationships such as composition, representation, transformation, validation, or other useful structural relationships.
* Style Rules for Intermediate Nodes
  * **Instances:** Style intermediate nodes that represent actual epistemic instances as plain text (no adornment).
  * **Naming:** Name every instance node in Title Case — capitalize every whitespace- or hyphen-separated word (`measurement record` -> `Measurement Record`, `dataset field` -> `Dataset Field`); preserve established all-caps acronyms (`API`, `CSV`). Type segments keep their table casing. The rule governs node names in Instance Tree Paths only; descriptions stay sentence-case prose.
  * **Composite Instance Naming**: A *composite* instance — a nested ensemble (a sub-`Aggregate`, sub-domain set, or same-typed family), not an ordinary object, record, or mechanism — takes a name ending in `Set` once it sits at a depth greater than 2. Depth counts every segment of the Instance Tree Path, backticked type groupings included, the root type grouping being depth 1. At depth 2 or less the suffix is permitted but never required: `Aggregate` -> `Arithmetic Inquiry Set` (depth 2) keeps it. (The Arithmetic tree below exercises no composite past depth 2, so the requirement bites only in deeper decompositions.)
  * **Types:** Style bare epistemic element types used as grouping nodes as `` `code` ``.
  * **Distinction:** Never style an instance and a type in the same way; the distinction must be immediately visible.
  * **Grouping types:** A type used only to group instances is not itself an instance and must not terminate a branch.
  * The path link - is →.
* **Epistemic Element Type Tree Path**: In the definition table this column is a template carrying the `<<Epistemic Element>>` root slot; once the root instance's type is bound (`(root) := …`), the instantiated path contains only concrete epistemic element types — no expansion patterns or placeholders.

### How do we figure out how nature works in practice?

> Not by looking randomly at isolated aspects, nor by relying uncritically on derived artifacts such as mathematical formalisms that represent a system only indirectly. Instead: ground inquiry in experiments, attend to deviations from current theory, and focus on unexplained processes, phenomena, or event streams — using discrepancies to locate gaps in understanding.

### Which aspects of reality are worth describing?

> Those exhibiting recurring patterns that enable explanation, prediction, intervention, or compression of experience — e.g. motion, mass, force, opportunity cost, trade-offs, Production–Possibility Frontier. **(Reader: notice the recursion here.)**

> Two different kinds of artifacts compress reality, and they must not be confused:
>
> - **Fundamental descriptive artifacts (concepts/aspects)** — concepts directly describing recurring structures of reality, enabling compression; meaningful standalone (motion, mass, force, opportunity cost). These identify **what** is described.
> - **Epistemic scaffolding tools** — frameworks supporting construction, organization, and reasoning about descriptions (numbers, geometry, logic, probability, calculus, graphs). These provide **how** descriptions are represented and manipulated.

### What is an epistemic activity?

> Any operation performed by an observer to construct, transform, evaluate, or apply knowledge about reality.

### What is a description?

> A structured affirmation about a segment of reality, carried by an epistemic artifact.

### What is an explanation?

> A very special form of description — maybe the ultimate form — clarifying cause, mechanism, or relation.

### What is the relationship between a mechanism and a concrete Physical Production Technical Object — or any other kind of technical object?

> A mechanism is what an organized object does together; the object is what bears the mechanism. A `Mechanism` (Ontic Order, Existential) is an arrangement of structures or interactions capable of generating, regulating, or constraining processes — gear train, enzyme complex, feedback mechanism. A technical object (`Production Technical Object` → physical like turbine, virtual like database, composed of `Constitutive Technical Object`s like bearing, CPU, schema) is the bounded existent in which such an arrangement is realized. Four relations, no mergers:
>
> - **Realization, not identity.** A mechanism is never free-floating; it is realized by an organized object. But it is not a part: constitutive objects compose the object (bill of materials), while the mechanism is the organized interaction of those parts producing the function — the same reason a mechanism is not identical to any single underlying process (it may coordinate several; cf. Philosophia Technicarum).
> - **Many-to-many.** One object realizes several mechanisms (a turbine: gas expansion, bearing friction, governor feedback); one mechanism type spans objects and even spaces (feedback in a thermostat, a market, a proof-checking loop).
> - **Epistemic bridge.** To know an object — model, explanation, prediction — is to identify the mechanisms it realizes under which constraints. The mechanism is where the ontic object meets the epistemic construction: models represent it, explanations cite it.
> - **Validation by intervention.** A mechanism-claim is warranted when blocking, altering, or transplanting the arrangement changes the effect as predicted — validated the way descriptions are (prediction error, replication, intervention), not by inspecting parts alone.
>
> What must not be done: do not file a mechanism as another part in the object's composition, and do not merge the two into a hybrid type. Decompose twice when needed — the object technically (parts, acts, configurations), the mechanism epistemically (model, explanation, standard) — and cross-link the trees per `guideline/ambiguity_resolution.md`.

### How do we evaluate descriptions of reality?

> An evaluation is a comprehensive assessment of a description, encompassing its validity and other relevant criteria.

### How do we validate our descriptions of reality?

> By testing the artifact against reality: prediction error, replication, and failed or successful intervention.

### Which aspects of reality result from multiplicative interaction?

> **Interaction-derived quantities** are ontic aspects quantifying the **joint state** of two or more independent ontic variables: momentum, kinetic energy, angular momentum, gravitational force, heat, revenue, cost, wage bill, information gain.
