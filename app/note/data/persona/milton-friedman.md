## **Monetarism**

- Developed the **Monetary Theory of Inflation** – "Inflation is always and everywhere a monetary phenomenon."
- Argued for control of money supply growth to manage inflation.

## **Permanent Income Hypothesis**

- Proposed that consumers base spending on **expected long-term income** rather than current income.
- This fundamentally challenged Keynesian models, which emphasized current income.
- It reshaped how economists understand savings, consumption, and fiscal policy.

## **Critique of Keynesian Economics**

- Challenged short-run stabilization policies.
- Emphasized **market self-correction** and limits of fiscal policy.

## **Natural Rate of Unemployment**

- Introduced the idea that there's a **minimum level of unemployment** that cannot be reduced by monetary policy without causing inflation.

## **Policy Advocacy**

- Advocate of **free markets**, **limited government**, **school vouchers**, **voluntary military**, and **deregulation**.

## **Monetary History and Theory**

- **A Monetary History of the United States, 1867–1960** (with Anna Schwartz):
    - This book argued that **monetary policy** (money supply management) had a central role in economic cycles.
    - Their analysis of the **Great Depression** famously argued that it was worsened by poor monetary policy by the Federal Reserve.
    - This revived interest in the quantity theory of money and laid the foundation for **monetarism**.

## **Critique of Stabilization Policy**

- Friedman showed that **government efforts to stabilize the economy** (e.g., using fiscal or monetary tools to smooth business cycles) often have **unintended effects** due to:
    - Policy lags
    - Inaccurate forecasting
    - Market expectations
- He argued that such policies could be **destabilizing**, not helpful.

## Technical Work

> 

### 📘 **Technical Books & Monographs**

| Title | Year | Main Contribution |
| --- | --- | --- |
| *Income from Independent Professional Practice* | 1945 | Empirical study of income distribution and taxation among professionals |
| *A Theory of the Consumption Function* | 1957 | Introduced the **Permanent Income Hypothesis** |
| *Studies in the Quantity Theory of Money* | 1956 | Modern restatement and empirical analysis of the **Quantity Theory of Money** |
| *A Program for Monetary Stability* | 1960 | Advocated for **rules-based monetary policy** and monetary growth targeting |
| *A Monetary History of the United States, 1867–1960* | 1963 | Showed that monetary forces drove major economic fluctuations, including the **Great Depression** |
| *The Optimum Quantity of Money and Other Essays* | 1969 | Theoretical essays on money, including the **Friedman Rule** on optimal inflation |
| *Monetary Trends in the U.S. and the U.K.* | 1982 | Long-term analysis of money growth, inflation, and output in comparative context |

### 📄 **Key Technical Journal Articles**

| Title | Year | Journal | Main Contribution |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| *The Methodology of Positive Economics* | 1953 | In *Essays in Positive Economics* | Argued that **predictive power**, not assumptions’ realism, defines scientific value |
| *The Quantity Theory of Money — A Restatement* | 1956 | In *Studies...* | Empirical restatement of classical **monetary theory** |
| *Money and Business Cycles* | 1953 | *Review of Economics and Statistics* | Linked **money supply changes** with **business cycle fluctuations** |
| *The Role of Monetary Policy* | 1968 | *American Economic Review* | Introduced **natural rate of unemployment**, critiqued **Phillips Curve** |
| *A Monetary Theory of Nominal Income* | 1971 | *Journal of Political Economy* | Modeled **monetary effects on nominal GDP**, challenged fiscal-dominant views |
