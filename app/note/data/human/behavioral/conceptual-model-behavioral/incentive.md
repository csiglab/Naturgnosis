# Incentive

> An **incentive** is an *action → feedback* pair, designed to influence an agent’s future behavior by linking actions to consequences such as rewards or penalties.
>

> A **quantifiable mechanism**—such as a financial payment, access right, performance metric, or sanction—**engineered to modify the utility function** of an agent in a decision-making system, such that the agent's optimal strategy (under bounded or rational expectations) is altered in a predictable direction toward or away from specific behaviors.
>

> **Caution:** An *incentive* is not an *edict*—it will not necessarily alter behavior at the individual level - or group level.
>

## Index

## **Incentive Structure**

A **multi-layered configuration of interdependent incentives**, formally represented as a mapping $\mathcal{I}: A \rightarrow \mathbb{R}^n$, where AA is the set of agent actions and $\mathbb{R}^n$  is a reward vector space. This structure governs the **distribution, hierarchy, interaction, and time-dependence of incentives** within a system, aiming to influence agent behavior across roles, states, or subsystems under informational and strategic constraints.

## **Incentive Alignment**

A condition in which the **individual utility-maximizing behavior of agents** under the given incentive structure **coincides with the system-level optimal outcome** (e.g., social welfare, organizational performance, or equilibrium stability). Mathematically, it implies that the Nash equilibrium (or dominant strategy equilibrium) of the game induced by the incentive structure is Pareto optimal or satisfies a predefined system objective.
