# Emotion

> In this note, we explore the phenomena of emotion at multiple levels of description, including behavioral, physiological, and cognitive dimensions.
>

> **Emotion** is a concept that refers to a complex set of responses—both internal and external—arising from the perception of significant stimuli, encompassing changes in physiology, expression, subjective experience, and action tendencies.
>

> An **emotion** is a type of complex state that characterizes the psychological and physiological response to a significant internal or external stimulus. This state is manifested by changes in subjective experience, observable behavior, and bodily processes.
>

> **Emotions** are **software-like emergent states** that arise from the activity of that system (and others, like the endocrine system).
>

> **Emotion** – This is a **complex state** that emerges from the activity of the nervous system (especially the brain), the endocrine system, and bodily feedback. It is **functional and dynamic**, not a physical structure. Emotions are patterns of responses—psychological, physiological, and behavioral—triggered by stimuli.
>

## **Physiology**

> What is the **Physiology** of Emotions?
>

Emotions arise from **coordinated activity across multiple physiological systems**, including:

1. **Central Nervous System** – Especially limbic structures (amygdala, hippocampus, hypothalamus) and prefrontal cortex, which evaluate stimuli and generate affective states.
2. **Autonomic Nervous System** – Mediates rapid bodily responses (heart rate, respiration, sweating) through sympathetic and parasympathetic activation.
3. **Endocrine System** – Hormonal changes (cortisol, adrenaline, oxytocin) modulate arousal, stress, and social bonding.
4. **Somatic Nervous System** – Produces facial expressions, gestures, and other motor behaviors associated with emotions.
5. **Interoceptive Feedback** – Signals from the body (e.g., gut, heart, muscles) contribute to the subjective experience of emotions.

## History

> What is the intellectual history of the notion & research on Emotion?
>

| **Field** | **Contribution** | **Era** | **Thinker** |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Philosophy | Conceptualized emotions as passions affecting reason and moral behavior. | Ancient (Classical) | Aristotle, Plato, Stoics |
| Philosophy | Integrated emotions with moral and spiritual life; emphasized virtues and vices. | Medieval | Augustine, Thomas Aquinas |
| Philosophy (Psychology) | Emotions as faculties of mind influencing behavior and cognition. | 17th-18th c. | Descartes, Spinoza, Hume |
| Psychology | Proposed emotions arise from bodily changes; linked physiological states to feelings. | Late 19th c. | William James, Carl Lange |
| Psychology | Systematic identification and classification of basic emotions. | Late 19th c. | Charles Darwin, William James |
| Psychology | Introduced cognitive appraisal theory: emotions arise from evaluation of events’ significance. | 20th c. | Richard Lazarus |
| Psychology (Ethology) | Emphasized evolutionary and adaptive function of emotions; social and survival roles. | Mid-20th c. | John Bowlby, Konrad Lorenz |
| Psychology  (Neuroscience) | Identified neural circuits underlying emotions (amygdala, limbic system). | 20th-21st c. | Joseph LeDoux, Paul Ekman |
| Affective Science | Studied secondary and complex emotions; social and cultural influences. | Late 20th-21st c. | Carol Izard, Antonio Damasio, Richard Shweder |
| Affective Computing | Formalized emotions for modeling, AI, and human-computer interaction. | 21st c. | Rosalind Picard, Jonathan Gratch |
