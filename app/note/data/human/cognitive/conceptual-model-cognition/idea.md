# Idea

> Yo vi,  distingui y capturé (representation).
>

> La palabra **"idea"** proviene del griego antiguo **ἰδέα (idéa)**, que significa "forma", "apariencia" o "aspecto". Esta, a su vez, deriva del verbo **εἴδω (eídō)**, que significa "ver".
>

> Mental representations of thoughts, notions, or concepts that can lead to creative insights or solutions.
>

> Note: Thinking is much more than ideas;   ideas are always conscious.
>

> Note: Direct map to reality. (Low Level of Abstraction)
>

## Etymology

> La palabra **"idea"** proviene en última instancia de la raíz indoeuropea ***weyd-***, que combinaba los significados de *ver* y *saber*. Por tanto, una *idea* es, etimológicamente, algo que se **ve con la mente** o se **sabe por percepción**.
>

1. **Español:** *idea*
2. **Latín:** *idea*
3. **Griego antiguo:** ἰδέα (*idéa*) — “forma, aspecto, apariencia”
4. **Del griego ἰδεῖν (*ideîn*)** — “ver” (forma aorista de εἴδω *eídō*, “yo veo”)
5. **Del protoheleno:** *weyd-* — “ver, saber”
6. **Del protoindoeuropeo:** ***weyd-*** — raíz verbal que significa **“ver”** o **“saber”**

Detalle de la raíz protoindoeuropea ***weyd-***:

- Significados fundamentales: “ver”, “saber”, “percibir con la mente”.
- Esta raíz ha dado lugar a muchas palabras en diversas lenguas indoeuropeas:
    - **Latín:** *vidēre* (ver) → *video*, *visión*, etc.
    - **Sánscrito:** *veda* (conocimiento sagrado)
    - **Gótico:** *witan* (saber) → inglés *wit*, *wise*, *witness*
    - **Antiguo eslavo eclesiástico:** *věděti* (saber)
    - **Griego:** εἶδον (vi), εἴδωλον (imagen, ídolo)

### **Theories of Ideas**

| **Theory** | **Description** | **Philosopher** | **School** |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Theory of Forms** | Posits two realms: the imperfect physical world and the perfect, unchanging realm of Forms. Knowledge is recollection (*anamnesis*). | **Plato** | Rationalism / Idealism |
| **Hylomorphism** | Reality consists of matter (*hyle*) and form (*morphe*). Forms inhere in objects rather than existing separately. | **Aristotle** | Moderate Realism |
| **Innate Ideas** | The mind possesses inherent knowledge (e.g., God, self, mathematics) prior to experience. | **Descartes** | Rationalism |
| **Tabula Rasa** | The mind begins as a "blank slate"; all knowledge derives from sensory experience. | **Locke** | Empiricism |
| **Immaterialism** | Only minds and ideas exist; material objects are mere perceptions (*esse est percipi*). | **Berkeley** | Idealism |
| **Radical Empiricism** | All ideas originate from sensory impressions; skepticism toward causality, self, and necessary truths. | **Hume** | Empiricism / Skepticism |
| **Transcendental Idealism** | The mind structures reality through innate categories (space, time, causality). Noumena vs. phenomena. | **Kant** | Transcendental Idealism |
| **Absolute Idealism** | Reality is the dialectical unfolding of the Absolute Spirit (thesis → antithesis → synthesis). | **Hegel** | Absolute Idealism |
| **Sense & Reference** | Distinguishes *Sinn* (sense—how an idea is presented) from *Bedeutung* (reference—what it points to). | **Frege** | Analytic Philosophy |
| **Picture Theory** | Propositions logically "picture" states of affairs; meaning derives from mirroring reality. | **Wittgenstein** (Early) | Logical Atomism |
| **Language Games** | Meaning arises from use in social practices; rejects private language or fixed essences. | **Wittgenstein** (Late) | Ordinary Language Philosophy |
