# Cognition

> In this note we will explore the **Effective Abstraction of Cognition** as it is studied in Cognitive Science. Cognition can be understood as a **functional-level abstraction**—a way of describing the mind in terms of its **operations and processes**—without committing to the underlying physical or representational form.
>

> The word *cognition* comes from the Latin *cognitio*, meaning "a getting to know," derived from *cognoscere* ("to know, learn, or become acquainted with"), combining *co-* ("together") and *gnoscere* ("to know")
>

## Formulation

> **Function** = **Why** a component exists (its purpose).
>

> **Process** = **What** the component does to fulfill its function (the series of steps or transformations it performs).
>

> **Effective Mechanism (Underlying Structure)** = **How** it achieves that function (the structural or operational means that implement the process).
>

| **Function** | **Process** | **Effective Mechanism (Underlying Structure)** |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Perception** | Transforming sensory input into patterns | Sensory systems + interpretive schemas |
| **Attention** | Selecting and prioritizing stimuli | Focused resource allocation, filtering mechanisms |
| **Memory** | Encoding, storing, retrieving information | Working memory, long-term stores, associative networks |
| **Learning** | Adjusting behavior/knowledge over time | Adaptive rules, updating weights, pattern refinement |
| **Reasoning** | Drawing inferences, manipulating symbols | Logical rules, probabilistic models, schemas |
| **Problem-Solving** | Applying strategies to reach goals | Goal hierarchies, heuristics, strategy sets |
| **Language** | Structuring and communicating thought | Symbolic codes, grammar, compositional structure |
| **Decision-Making** | Evaluating options and choosing actions | Utility models, preference structures, evaluative rules |
| **Metacognition** | Monitoring/regulating cognition | Self-models, control loops, reflective structures |

## **Form**

> Forms of cognition represents different flavors - or areas of the human cognition.
>

| **Form** | **Description** | **Case(s)** |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Basic Cognition** | Simple perception, attention, and memory; fundamental cognitive operations shared by many animals | Sensory recognition, short-term memory, basic attention |
| **Higher-Order Cognition** | Abstract thinking, planning, reasoning, and self-reflection; more developed in humans | Problem-solving, strategic planning, metacognition |
| **Social Cognition** | Understanding others’ intentions, beliefs, and emotions | Theory of mind, empathy, social reasoning |
