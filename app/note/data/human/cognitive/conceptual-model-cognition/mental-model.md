# Mental Model

> A mental model is a representation of some domain or situation that supports understanding, reasoning, and prediction. Mental models permit reasoning about situations not directly experienced. They allow people to mentally simulate the behavior of a system. Many mental models are based on generalizations and analogies from experience. These generalizations are not always accurate; researchers have identified striking cases of widespread erroneous mental models. An understanding of typical mental models—both correct and incorrect—is important both in order to design devices that people will use correctly and in order to create effective instructional materials.
>

> Mental models are conceived of as a cognitive structure that forms the basis of reasoning, decision making, and, with the limitations also observed in the attitudes literature, behavior.
>

> Mental models are personal, internal representations of external reality that people use to interact with the world around them.
>

## Index

## **Mental Models**

> Mental models are cognitive frameworks or mental representations that people use to understand and reason about the world.
>

There are several different types of mental models, including:

- **Conceptual models**: These mental models represent abstract concepts or ideas, such as systems, processes, or theories. They help people understand complex phenomena by simplifying and organizing information into coherent structures.
- **Analogical models**: These mental models use analogies or comparisons to help people understand new concepts or ideas by linking them to familiar ones. For example, a computer mouse can be understood as a pointer or a tool for navigation, similar to a physical mouse.
- **Mathematical models**: These mental models represent quantitative relationships between variables or phenomena. They help people understand complex systems or processes by using mathematical equations or simulations.
- **Mental imagery models**: These mental models use visual or sensory representations to help people understand and remember information. For example, imagining a story in vivid detail can help people remember it better.
- **Metaphorical models**: These mental models use metaphors or figures of speech to help people understand complex or abstract concepts. For example, describing the brain as a “supercomputer” or the internet as a “web” helps people understand these concepts by relating them to familiar ones.
- **Cognitive maps**: These mental models represent spatial or geographic relationships between objects or locations. They help people navigate their physical environment and understand complex spatial relationships.
