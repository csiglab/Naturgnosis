# Mind

> Note: This is a highly speculative abstraction, bordering on bad science or pseudoscience. It aims more to provoke thought than to claim rigor.
>

> The mind is an emergent, dynamic, and self-organizing functional system instantiated by the coordinated activity of neural and extraneural substrates, responsible for the integration, modulation, and adaptive regulation of informational, representational, and affective processes that give rise to cognition, intentionality, perception, emotion, memory, imagination, and volition, and whose operations are context-sensitive, temporally extended, and recursively self-modifying across multiple hierarchical levels of abstraction.
>

> Note: Creating a  theory of mind is  one  of the two aspects of a great philosophical system - the other being a theory of nature.
>

## Terminology

> Which is the terminology used in `Philosophy of Mind`?
>

| Term | Definition |
| --- | --- |
| **Mind** | The set of cognitive faculties including consciousness, perception, thinking, judgment, and memory. |
| **Consciousness** | The state or quality of awareness of self and environment; subjective experience. |
| **Intentionality** | The capacity of mental states to be about, or represent, objects and states of affairs. |
| **Qualia** | The subjective, qualitative aspects of experience, such as “what it feels like” to see red. |
| **Mental State** | A condition of the mind, such as beliefs, desires, sensations, or emotions. |
| **Dualism** | The view that mind and body are fundamentally distinct kinds of substances. |
| **Physicalism / Materialism** | The doctrine that everything about the mind can be explained in physical terms. |
| **Functionalism** | The theory that mental states are defined by their causal roles, not by their internal constitution. |
| **Identity Theory** | The view that mental states are identical to brain states or physical processes. |
| **Emergentism** | The perspective that mental phenomena emerge from but are not reducible to physical processes. |
| **Panpsychism** | The view that consciousness or mind-like qualities are fundamental and pervasive in nature. |
| **Intentional Content** | The “aboutness” or representational content of a mental state. |
| **Mental Causation** | The relationship by which mental states can bring about physical or other mental states. |
| **Zombie (Philosophical Zombie)** | A hypothetical being physically identical to a human but lacking conscious experience. |
| **Epiphenomenalism** | The theory that mental states are byproducts of physical processes and do not affect the physical world. |
| **Phenomenology** | The study of structures of experience from the first-person perspective. |
| **Emergent Property** | A property that arises from complex systems but is not reducible to the system’s components. |
| **Intentional Stance** | Dennett’s strategy of interpreting the behavior of entities by treating them as rational agents with beliefs and desires. |
