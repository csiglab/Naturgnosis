# Language

> ¿What is the Language System?  ¿What’s the relation of the **Language System** vs **Communication System (Signal Transmission)**? ¿How **language** get **acquired**?
>

> A **language** is, according to Chomsky, a **state** obtained by a specific **mental computational system** that develops naturally and whose exact parameters are set by the **linguistic environment** that the individual is exposed to as a child.
>

> Language is a good crutch(muletilla) to compensate for a lack of understanding (complete world model).
>

> **Language** is a **horrible way** to describe **our internal model of the world**. Much of our knowledge cannot be accurately expressed in language. If it were, we could learn everything from books. But for most things, we need **practical experience**.
>

> **Language** discretizes knowledge, which makes it easier to store knowledge compactly but also makes it **inaccurate**.
>

> **Communication**: Language's primary function is to allow people to communicate with each other. It enables individuals to express their thoughts, ideas, emotions, and intentions to others, facilitating social interactions and relationships.
>

> Can the idea of  **`reference frames`** be used in **`linguistics`**?
>

> The study of language has, as Chomsky states, three aspects: determining what the system of knowledge a language user has consists of, how that knowledge is acquired, and how that knowledge is used.
>

## Field Structure

> Here is a table summarizing the details provided:
>

| **Aspect** | **Description** |
| --- | --- |
| **Phonetics** | Examines the physical properties of speech sounds and how they are produced, transmitted, and perceived. |
| **Phonology** | Focuses on the systematic organization and patterns of sounds within languages, including the study of phonemes, syllables, and sound structures. |
| **Morphology** | Analyzes the structure and formation of words, including the study of morphemes and how they combine to create meaning. |
| **Syntax** | Investigates the rules and principles governing the structure and arrangement of words to form meaningful sentences and phrases. |
| **Semantics** | Explores the meaning and interpretation of words, phrases, and sentences, including studying lexical semantics, compositional semantics, and pragmatics. |
| **Pragmatics** | Examines how context, social factors, and speaker intentions influence the meaning and use of language in communicative situations. |

## Language Modelling Frameworks

> **Linguists** use various modeling frameworks to understand and analyze language structure, acquisition, processing, and evolution.
>

> **Cognitive architecture:** A cognitive architecture is a theoretical framework that outlines the structures and processes of the human mind, often implemented computationally to model human-like intelligence.
>

| **Theory** | **Description** | **Key Proponents** |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Generative Grammar** | Focuses on the innate structures of language, proposing the ability to generate infinite sentences from finite rules is unique to human language. | Noam Chomsky |
| **Functionalism** | Emphasizes the functions of language and its social and cultural purposes, focusing on language use in various contexts. | - |
| **Cognitive Linguistics** | Views language as part of cognitive faculties, emphasizing the relationship between linguistic structure and cognitive processes like perception, attention. | - |
| **Construction Grammar** | Argues that language consists of constructions or form-meaning pairings, ranging from simple to complex. | - |
| **Systemic Functional Linguistics** | Examines how language functions to express meaning in different social and cultural contexts. | Michael Halliday |
| **Optimality Theory** | Proposes that language forms arise from the interaction between conflicting constraints, with the most "optimal" output being selected. | - |
| **Dependency Grammar** | Focuses on the dependency relations between words in a sentence rather than on phrase structure rules. | - |
| **Montague Grammar** | Applies formal logic to the analysis of natural language, aiming to describe the syntax, semantics, and pragmatics of language in a unified way. | - |
| **Lexical Functional Grammar** | Concentrates on the structure of words and the functional structure of sentences, emphasizing the role of syntax in determining meaning. | - |
| **Head-Driven Phrase Structure Grammar** | A highly lexicalized approach that integrates features from other theories, focusing on properties of individual words and their role in syntactic structures. | - |

### What’s the role of Language in Representation?

> The symbols of language, whether in speech or writing, function as re-representations. They do not directly capture the objects, concepts, or experiences they refer to; instead, their meaning arises from social conventions and shared agreements among members of a linguistic community. In this sense, language provides a structured, symbolic framework through which mental concepts can be encoded, communicated, and manipulated.
>

| Role of Language | Function in Representation | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Medium | Externalizes mental representations | Writing a concept down |
| Structuring | Shapes organization of concepts | Grammar encoding relations |
| Cognitive tool | Supports thinking, memory, reasoning | Internal self-talk or logic |
| Social mediation | Enables shared understanding | Scientific discourse |
| Limitation | Partial, biased, ambiguous | “Chair” vs “silla” distinctions |
