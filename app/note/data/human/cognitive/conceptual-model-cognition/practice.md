## Theory

| **Framework Component** | **Key Research Examples** | **Key Findings** | **Connection to Framework** |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Co-evolution of Mental Representations & Practice** | Ericsson et al. (1993) - *Deliberate Practice in Music* | Expert violinists' practice quality (not just quantity) predicted skill level. Mental representations guided focused practice, which refined representations. | Demonstrates bidirectional relationship: representations shape practice → practice upgrades representations. |
|  | Chase & Simon (1973) - *Chess Expertise & Chunking* | Chess masters stored board patterns as "chunks" in long-term memory; novices lacked these structured representations. | Shows how mental representations (chunking) enable effective practice and further refinement. |
| **Building Initial Mental Models** | Bandura et al. (1961) - *Bobo Doll Experiment* | Children learned aggressive behaviors by observing adults, forming mental models through observation before practice. | Supports "instruction/observation" as foundation for initial representations. |
|  | Chi et al. (1989) - *Self-Explanations in Physics* | Students who explained examples to themselves built more accurate physics models than passive learners. | Highlights active engagement with instruction as critical for initial representation formation. |
| **Deliberate Practice Refining Representations** | Ericsson et al. (1993) - *Berlin Academy of Music* | Top musicians practiced with specific goals, error correction, and feedback—challenging their representations. | Embodies "focused practice that challenges/refines representations." |
|  | Beilock et al. (2008) - *Putting Under Pressure* | Expert golfers' performance collapsed under pressure when focusing on step-by-step execution (disrupting automated representations). | Shows how practice refines representations until they become automated (experts) vs. novices needing structured practice. |
| **Continuous Updating & Alignment** | Dweck (2006) - *Mindset Interventions* | Students with "growth mindset" updated mental representations after failure; "fixed mindset" students resisted updates. | Illustrates "continuously updating representations" through experience and feedback. |
|  | Schön (1983) - *The Reflective Practitioner* | Professionals improved by "reflecting-in-action"—adjusting mental models during practice based on outcomes. | Demonstrates real-time alignment of practice and evolving understanding. |
| **Practice Without Representation → Ineffective** | Gick & Holyoak (1980) - *Duncker's Radiation Problem* | Participants failed to apply analogical solutions without explicit guidance to connect representations. | Shows practice (problem-solving) fails without a coherent mental framework. |
|  | McDaniel et al. (2009) - *Preparation for Future Learning* | Students who explored problems *before* instruction developed flawed representations that hindered later learning. | Confirms unstructured practice without initial models leads to dead ends. |

| **Theoretical Framework** | **Core Principles** | **Support for the Model** |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Ericsson's Deliberate Practice** | Mastery requires practice designed *by* mental representations to target weaknesses. | Explains how representations evolve through feedback loops during focused practice. |
| **Anderson's ACT-R** | Skills transition from declarative (explicit) to procedural (automated) through practice. | Shows how practice transforms mental representations into automated expertise. |
| **Vygotsky's ZPD** | Learning occurs in the "Zone of Proximal Development" with scaffolding. | Initial representations (scaffolding) enable practice beyond current ability → representations expand as skills grow. |

---

### Practical Implications from Research

1. **Start with structured observation/instruction** (Bandura, Chi) to build foundational mental models.
2. **Use deliberate practice** (Ericsson) with specific goals and feedback to refine representations.
3. **Cultivate a growth mindset** (Dweck) to enable continuous updating of mental models.
4. **Align practice with current understanding** (Schön) through reflection and adjustment.
5. **Avoid premature unstructured practice** (McDaniel) without initial mental frameworks.

This table demonstrates how the mental representation framework is empirically grounded across cognitive psychology, expertise development, and learning science research.
