# Information Search

> Information **searching models** are frameworks or theories that describe how individuals search for information, whether it’s through libraries, databases, search engines, or other means. These models help researchers and information professionals understand the cognitive processes and strategies involved in information seeking.
>

| **Information Searching Model** | **Description** |
| --- | --- |
| **Kuhlthau’s Information Search Process (ISP) Model** | Describes a series of stages in information seeking: initiation, selection, exploration, formulation, collection, and presentation. |
| **Ellis’s Information Seeking Behavior Model** | Outlines stages in information seeking: starting, chaining, browsing, differentiating, monitoring, and extracting, emphasizing the dynamic and iterative nature of the search process. |
| **Belkin’s Anomalous States of Knowledge (ASK) Model** | Focuses on “anomalous states of knowledge,” where users identify a gap in their knowledge and actively seek information to address it. |
| **Wilson’s Model of Information Behavior** | Highlights cognitive, affective, and situational factors influencing information-seeking behavior, such as cognitive style, information needs, and perceived value. |
| **Bates’s Berrypicking Model** | Views information seeking as an iterative and opportunistic process, where users “pick” information based on evolving needs and the information encountered. |
| **Marchionini’s Information Seeking in Electronic Environments (ISEE) Model** | Emphasizes the roles of electronic information environments, search tools, strategies, and interactions in the information-seeking process. |
| **Case’s Stages of Intellectual Development in Information Seeking (SIDIS) Model** | Explores the impact of individuals’ intellectual development and problem-solving abilities on their information-seeking behavior. |
| **Ingwersen and Järvelin’s Cognitive Work in Information Seeking (COW) Model** | Focuses on cognitive processes and strategies during information seeking, such as relevance judgments and query reformulation. |
| **Spink’s Information Interaction Model (II Model)** | Emphasizes the interaction between the user, task, and system in information seeking, recognizing the dynamic nature of the process. |
| **Vakkari’s Berry-Picking in Information Retrieval for Work Tasks (BIRWT) Model** | Examines information-seeking behavior in the context of work tasks, highlighting how work tasks drive information-seeking activities. |
