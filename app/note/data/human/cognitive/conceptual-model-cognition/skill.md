## Skills Research

> Skills research is a subfield of cognitive science that studies how people acquire, develop, and refine abilities across various domains. It explores the cognitive, neural, and behavioral mechanisms underlying expertise and performance improvement.
>

Areas:

- [ ]  **Stages of Skill Acquisition**
    - *Dreyfus Model*: Novice → Advanced Beginner → Competent → Proficient → Expert
    - *Fitts & Posner Model*: Cognitive → Associative → Autonomous
    - *Chunking & Pattern Recognition*: Experts recognize patterns faster than novices.
- [ ]  **Deliberate Practice & Expertise**
    - *Ericsson’s Theory of Deliberate Practice*: Focused, goal-oriented training leads to expertise.
    - *Role of Feedback*: Timely corrections accelerate learning.
    - *10,000-Hour Rule (Misconception)*: Expertise depends on quality, not just time spent.
- [ ]  **Motor & Procedural Skill Learning**
    - *Implicit vs. Explicit Learning*: Some skills are acquired unconsciously.
    - *Neural Correlates*: Basal ganglia, motor cortex, and cerebellum play key roles.
    - *Automation & Reflexes*: Skills shift from controlled to automatic processes.
- [ ]  **Cognitive Load & Skill Retention**
    - *Cognitive Load Theory*: Learning is affected by working memory limitations.
    - *Spacing & Interleaving Effects*: Optimal practice schedules improve retention.
    - *Skill Transfer*: Some skills generalize across domains (e.g., problem-solving).
- [ ]  **Real-World Applications**
    - **Education**: Adaptive learning technologies & curriculum design.
    - **Sports & Performance**: Training regimens for athletes and musicians.
    - **Medical & Technical Training**: Simulation-based learning for high-stakes professions.

## Models of Skill Acquisition

> The **Skills Model**, often referred to in the context of expertise and competency development, outlines the stages through which an individual progresses to acquire and refine a skill.
>

![](https://stephenlethbridge.com/wp-content/uploads/2015/02/The-Dreyfus-Model.png)

A well-known example is the "Dreyfus Model of Skill Acquisition," which identifies five stages:

1. **Novice:** Beginners strictly follow the rules and guidelines, with little situational perception and discretionary judgment.
2. **Advanced Beginner:** Individuals recognize situational elements, although their performance relies heavily on rules and guidelines.
3. **Competent:** At this level, individuals can see their actions within a larger context or goal, leading to a more deliberate planning and decision-making process.
4. **Proficient:** Proficient individuals can intuitively understand situations and deliberate on the most important or relevant aspects, leading to more nuanced and adept performance.
5. **Expert:** Experts intuitively grasp situations and can fluidly adapt their skills to new situations without conscious effort, often innovating new solutions or methods.

## Skill Evaluation Model

> Skills characterization methods are approaches used to identify, describe, and evaluate skills in various contexts, including educational settings, workplaces, and personal development.
>

| **Method** | **Description** |
| --- | --- |
| **Competency Frameworks** | Structured models that define specific skills, knowledge, and behaviors required for a role or industry. |
| **Skills Inventories** | Comprehensive lists of skills associated with particular jobs or fields, used for workforce assessment and development. |
| **Behavioral-Anchored Rating Scales (BARS)** | Evaluation method using specific behavioral examples to describe performance levels for a skill. |
| **360-Degree Feedback** | Multi-source feedback system where employees are evaluated by peers, subordinates, supervisors, and clients. |
| **Self-Assessment Tools** | Questionnaires or surveys for individuals to reflect on their skills, strengths, and improvement areas. |
| **Skill Gap Analysis** | Identifies the gap between required job skills and an individual’s current capabilities to highlight areas for development. |
| **Proficiency Levels and Descriptors** | Systems defining skill levels (e.g., beginner, intermediate, advanced) with detailed descriptions. |
| **Rubrics** | Assessment tools with detailed criteria for evaluating work quality or skill performance, often used in education. |
| **Job Analysis Techniques** | Methods like interviews, observations, and surveys to identify tasks, responsibilities, and required skills. |
| **Digital Badges and Micro-Credentials** | Online representations of earned skills and competencies linked to specific learning experiences. |
| **Performance Assessments** | Evaluations based on tasks or projects requiring skill application to provide competency evidence. |
| **Simulation and Role-Playing** | Interactive methods for demonstrating skills in controlled, realistic scenarios. |
| **Portfolio Assessment** | Collection of work samples and achievements showcasing skill development over time. |
