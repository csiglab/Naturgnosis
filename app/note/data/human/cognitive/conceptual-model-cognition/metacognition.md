# Metacognition

> **Metacognition** is the **ability** to reflect on and regulate one’s own cognitive processes, such as monitoring one’s own performance, planning, and self-correction.

## Structure

>

| **Component** | **Description** | **Function** | **Examples / Notes** |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Perception** | Receiving and processing sensory input | Converts raw sensory data into meaningful patterns | Vision, hearing, touch |
| **Attention** | Selectively focusing cognitive resources | Filters and prioritizes stimuli | Focus on relevant information |
| **Working Memory** | Temporary storage and manipulation of information | Holds info actively for reasoning and problem solving | Mental math, following instructions |
| **Long-Term Memory** | Storage of knowledge and experiences over time | Maintains facts, events, and skills | Semantic memory (facts), procedural memory (skills) |
| **Concept Formation** | Organizing knowledge into categories and abstracting common features | Enables generalization and categorization | Forming the concept of “dog” from experiences |
| **Language & Symbols** | Using symbols to represent concepts and relations | Facilitates complex thought and communication | Words, signs, mathematical notation |
| **Reasoning & Problem Solving** | Applying logic and strategies to manipulate information and reach conclusions | Supports decision making and inference | Deductive reasoning, puzzles |
| **Decision Making** | Evaluating options and selecting actions | Chooses behaviors based on goals and predictions | Choosing what to eat or which route to take |
| **Metacognition** | Awareness and regulation of own cognitive processes | Monitors and controls thinking and learning | Self-reflection, planning study strategies |

## Capabilities

| **Capability** | **Description** |
| --- | --- |
| **Metacognitive Awareness** | Understanding one's own thought processes, strengths, and weaknesses. |
| **Self-Monitoring** | Tracking one's own learning or problem-solving progress. |
| **Self-Regulation** | Adjusting strategies based on monitoring results. |
| **Goal Setting** | Defining specific learning objectives. |
| **Planning** | Developing a strategy to achieve goals efficiently. |
| **Cognitive Flexibility** | Adapting to new information, perspectives, or methods. |
| **Error Detection & Correction** | Identifying and fixing mistakes in thinking or execution. |
| **Knowledge Transfer** | Applying knowledge from one domain to another. |
| **Reflection** | Evaluating past experiences to improve future learning. |
| **Metacognitive Questioning** | Asking questions to deepen understanding. |
| **Self-Explanation** | Articulating reasoning to reinforce understanding. |

## **Models**

- **Self-Regulated Learning Model**: Zimmerman, B.J. (1989). A social cognitive view of self-regulated academic learning. Journal of Educational Psychology, 81(3), 329-339.
- **Metacognitive Awareness Model**: Schraw, G. and Dennison, R.S. (1994). Assessing metacognitive awareness. Contemporary Educational Psychology, 19(4), 460-475.
- **Transfer-Appropriate Processing Model**: Morris, C.D. and Bransford, J.D. (1972). Perceiving, remembering, and communicating information. Journal of Verbal Learning and Verbal Behavior, 11(6), 717-726.
- **Dual-Process Theory**: Metcalfe, J. and Mischel, W. (1999). A hot/cool-system analysis of delay of gratification: dynamics of willpower. Psychological Review, 106(1), 3-19.
- **Monitoring and Control Model**: Nelson, T.O. and Narens, L. (1990). Metamemory: A theoretical framework and new findings. Psychology of Learning and Motivation, 26, 125-173.
- **Cognitive Load Theory**: Sweller, J. (1994). Cognitive load theory, learning difficulty, and instructional design. Learning and Instruction, 4(4), 295-312.
