# Reasoning

> AKA Human Brain Computation.
>

> Studying reasoning from a scientific point view.
>

> **"Pensar"** proviene del latín *pensare*,  "evaluar" o "sopesar en la mente", lo que evolucionó hasta significar "reflexionar" o "meditar". Computaciones Mentales.
>

> **"Razón"** proviene del latín *ratio*, *rationis*, que significa "cálculo", "cuenta" o "disposición",  es el resultado de **pensar**. Razonar es un tipo de pensar lógico que produce razones o justificaciones.
>

| **Reasoning Method** | **Description** |
| --- | --- |
| **Deductive Reasoning** | Starts with general premises and leads to a specific conclusion; if premises are true, the conclusion must be true. |
| **Inductive Reasoning** | Involves making generalizations based on specific observations or evidence; conclusions may be probable but not guaranteed. |
| **Abductive Reasoning** | Infers the best explanation for a set of observations; often used when there is incomplete information. |
| **Analogical Reasoning** | Draws parallels between similar situations or concepts to infer conclusions or insights; relies on recognizing relationships. |
| **Critical Thinking** | Evaluates arguments and claims systematically; involves analyzing, synthesizing, and interpreting information. |
| **Heuristic Reasoning** | Uses mental shortcuts or rules of thumb to simplify decision-making and problem-solving; may lead to biases. |
| **Creative Thinking** | Generates new ideas or solutions by thinking outside conventional frameworks; often involves divergent thinking. |
| **Logical Reasoning** | Applies formal logic to derive conclusions based on established principles and rules; includes syllogistic reasoning. |
| **Probabilistic Reasoning** | Assesses likelihoods and uncertainties when making decisions or predictions based on available data. |
| **Common-Sense Reasoning** | Relies on everyday knowledge and practical experiences to draw conclusions about the world. |
| **Intuitive Reasoning** | Involves making quick judgments based on gut feelings or instinct without the need for conscious reasoning. |
| **Systematic Reasoning** | Involves a structured approach to problem-solving, using organized methods to ensure thoroughness. |
| **Contextual Reasoning** | Takes into account the surrounding context or environment when interpreting information or making decisions. |
| **Social Reasoning** | Involves understanding and interpreting social cues, norms, and dynamics in decision-making and judgments. |
| **Ethical Reasoning** | Evaluates moral principles and values to determine the rightness or wrongness of actions or decisions. |
