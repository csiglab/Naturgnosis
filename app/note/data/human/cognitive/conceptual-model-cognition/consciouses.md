## Index

## Formulation

> What is Consciouses?
>

> Consciousness is a state of cognition in which a system experiences and is aware of its own representations. It involves having a representation of a representation, allowing the system to monitor, reflect on, and integrate information about itself and its environment.
>

## Theory

> What is the form of a theory of Consciouses?
>

### Characterization

A theory of consciousness typically provides:

1. **Explanatory framework** – Identifying the mechanisms, processes, and structures that give rise to conscious experience.
2. **Phenomenal characterization** – Describing the qualitative, subjective aspects of experience (“what it is like”).
3. **Functional mapping** – Linking consciousness to cognitive faculties such as attention, working memory, agency, and meta-cognition.
4. **Integrative model** – Showing how consciousness emerges from or interacts with neural, computational, or systemic processes.
5. **Predictive or normative claims** – Indicating how conscious states influence behavior, decision-making, or self-monitoring.

### Proposal

| Theory | Description | Criticism |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Higher-Order Thought (HOT)** | Consciousness arises when a mental state is the object of another, higher-order representation. | May not explain raw phenomenal experience; some conscious states seem non-reflective. |
| **Global Workspace Theory (GWT)** | Consciousness emerges when information is broadcast globally across cognitive systems, integrating perception, memory, and action. | Mechanistic focus may overlook subjective experience; unclear how “global broadcast” produces qualia. |
| **Integrated Information Theory (IIT)** | Consciousness corresponds to the degree of integrated information (Φ) within a system. | Difficult to measure Φ empirically; may overgeneralize to non-biological systems. |
| **Recurrent Processing Theory** | Consciousness arises from recurrent (feedback) neural processing rather than feedforward activity alone. | Explains visual awareness well, but may not generalize to all conscious phenomena. |
| **Predictive Processing** | Conscious experience is a result of hierarchical predictions and error-correction in cognitive systems. | Abstract; may describe computational principles without fully explaining phenomenology. |

## Act

> How do we become conscious of things?
>

> See more in
>

| Mechanism | Description | Instance(s) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Naming** | Assigning a label to an object, concept, or phenomenon externalizes a mental representation, allowing awareness and reflection. | Recognizing a tree and saying “tree”; identifying a feeling as “anger”; categorizing a mathematical concept as “derivative.” |
| **Attention** | Focusing cognitive resources on a particular stimulus or internal representation brings it into conscious awareness. | Noticing a sound in a noisy room; observing a detail in a painting. |
| **Perception** | Sensory processing of stimuli provides the raw data that can be consciously experienced once integrated. | Seeing a red apple; feeling warmth on the skin. |
| **Reflection / Meta-cognition** | Thinking about one’s own thoughts or experiences allows conscious recognition and understanding of mental states. | Realizing you are anxious; evaluating your own reasoning process. |
| **Memory retrieval** | Accessing stored representations can bring past experiences into present awareness. | Remembering a childhood event; recalling a fact during problem-solving. |

## Relation

> Which other aspect of cognition are related with Consciouses?
>
- **Agency** – The ability to act intentionally based on conscious decisions.
- **Attention** – The selective focus on certain stimuli or internal representations.
- **Working memory** – Holding and manipulating information in mind for conscious processing.
- **Self-awareness** – Awareness of one’s own mental states and representations.
- **Meta-cognition** – Thinking about one’s own thinking; monitoring and evaluating cognitive processes.
- **Perception** – The conscious registration and interpretation of sensory input.
- **Intentionality** – The “aboutness” of conscious states; how thoughts and experiences are directed toward objects or concepts.
