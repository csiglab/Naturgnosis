# Abstraction

> The **concept of abstraction** is extremely difficult to define because it is a mental construct; however, making the effort to understand it is worthwhile, as it is arguably the most important mental operation in the realm of representation.
>

> Abstraction can be technically defined as the cognitive process through which one extracts the essential properties of an object; process; or even a mental construct, enabling generalized representation and reasoning beyond particular instances.
>

> Abstraction can be formally defined in terms of **compression**, among other perspectives. Interestingly, the concept of compression can also be described—but not fully formalized—through abstraction, revealing a fascinating duality between the two processes.
>

## Index

## Instance

> What are the subtypes of the process of abstraction?
>

| **Sub-type** | **Description** | **Case** |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Generalization** | Extracting common features from specific cases to form broader concepts | Recognizing that all squares are rectangles |
| **Idealization** | Simplifying reality by ignoring certain constraints to focus on essential properties | Physics: modeling a frictionless plane |
| **Conceptualization** | Creating a mental concept that represents multiple instances or experiences | Forming the idea of “justice” |
| **Categorization** | Grouping objects or ideas based on shared attributes or criteria | Sorting animals into mammals, reptiles, birds |
| **Symbolization** | Representing complex ideas through symbols for easier manipulation or communication | Using letters, numbers, or icons |
| **Partial Representation** | Selecting and representing only certain facets or aspects of a concept, depending on the context and purpose, while ignoring the rest. | Using “water under the bridge” to highlight the “let go and move on” facet of the bridge concept rather than its full physical meaning. |

## Technique

> How to create abstraction?
>

| **Technique** | **Description** | **Case** |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Identify essential features** | Focus on the core properties or behaviors of an object or concept, ignoring irrelevant details. | Representing a triangle by its angles (60°, 60°, 60°) rather than its color, orientation, or size. |
| **Categorization (Grouping)** | Organize objects or concepts into categories based on shared attributes. | Sorting animals into mammals, birds, and reptiles based on physiological features. |
| **Commonalities** | Find patterns or shared traits across multiple instances to form broader concepts. | Observing that all squares have four equal sides and four right angles, regardless of size. |
| **By Simplification (Idealization)** | Remove or ignore constraints or complexities that are not central to the analysis. | Modeling a frictionless plane in physics to study motion without accounting for friction. |
| **Analogy (Mapping)** | Transfer structural or functional relationships from one domain to another to aid understanding. | Explaining electrical circuits using the flow of water through pipes as an analogy. |
| **Compression** | Reduce information by summarizing or encoding it while preserving essential patterns. | Representing a dataset of exam scores using mean, median, variance, or a histogram instead of listing every score. |

## Relation

> Which other mental or non-mental processes are related to abstraction; but are not part a type of abstraction?
>

| **Related Process** | **Relation to Abstraction** | **Description** |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Memory (Working & Long-term)** | Supports abstraction | Retains information and experiences that abstraction draws upon; allows comparison across instances. |
| **Attention** | Enables abstraction | Selectively focuses on relevant features while filtering out irrelevant details. |
| **Imagination (Visualization)** | Facilitates abstraction | Allows mental simulation of concepts, relationships, or systems beyond direct perception. |
| **Reasoning (Deductive & Inductive)** | Operates on abstractions | Uses abstract concepts to infer new knowledge or generalize from examples. |
| **Problem-Solving** | Applies abstraction | Uses abstract representations to devise solutions without relying solely on concrete examples. |
| **Language (Symbolic Thought)** | Expresses and communicates abstraction | Provides tools to encode, manipulate, and share abstract ideas. |
| **Perception** | Supplies raw input for abstraction | Provides sensory information that abstraction processes to detect patterns or essential features. |
| **Learning** | Generates content for abstraction | Accumulates experiences that form the basis for identifying commonalities and patterns. |
| **Creativity** | Explores and extends abstraction | Enables novel combinations or reinterpretations of abstract concepts. |
| **Understanding** | Consolidates abstraction | Integrates multiple abstractions into a coherent mental model, enabling deeper insight and the ability to transfer knowledge across domains. |
