# Cognitive Development

> Human cognition is both an innate faculty and a capacity that can be developed and improved.
>

> Human cognition stems from innate mental abilities shared across our species, which are then expanded by building upon knowledge and skills passed down through generations.
>

> Stand on the shoulders of giants - …
>

**We will gather data about cognition in different societies, focusing on aspects such as:**

- Perception and sensory processing

- Memory and oral traditions
- Language and communication structures
- Problem-solving and reasoning methods
- Numerical cognition and counting systems
- Spatial awareness and navigation skills
- Cultural tools and external representations (e.g., symbols, artifacts)
- Social cognition and collective knowledge transmission

## Humanity Cognitive History

> See more in [Evolution of Modelling](https://app.notion.com/p/Evolution-of-Modelling-1b1c0f5171ec802fb376d07beffc6523?pvs=21).
>

| **Period** | **Approx. Date Range** | **Key Cognitive Features** | **Representative Innovations / Milestones** | **Social & Cultural Impact** |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Pre-Linguistic Cognition | >200,000 BCE | Sensorimotor intelligence; tool use; spatial reasoning; symbolic proto-communication | Acheulean tools, fire control, cooperative hunting | Foundation for cooperative survival and cultural transmission |
| Proto-Language & Mythic Mind | 200,000–40,000 BCE | Symbolic thinking; narrative structuring; ritual and myth creation | Early burials, ochre use, cave art beginnings | Social cohesion through shared myths and oral tradition |
| Upper Paleolithic Cognitive Revolution | 40,000–10,000 BCE | Fully developed language; abstract representation; complex planning | Cave art (Lascaux), musical instruments, composite tools | Cultural diversification; accelerated knowledge transmission |
| Neolithic Cognitive Consolidation | 10,000–3,000 BCE | Agricultural planning; calendrical systems; proto-writing | Domestication, megalithic structures, counting systems | Emergence of organized religion, surplus economies |
| Early Literate Societies | 3,000–500 BCE | Written language; codified laws; formal mathematics | Cuneiform, hieroglyphics, early philosophy (Egypt, Mesopotamia, China) | Bureaucracies, archival memory, structured education |
| Classical Rationalism | 500 BCE–500 CE | Systematic philosophy; deductive logic; theoretical science | Socratic method, Euclidean geometry, Indian logic, Chinese classics | Foundation for formal epistemology and scientific reasoning |
| Medieval Scholastic Synthesis | 500–1500 CE | Integration of theology and philosophy; preservation of ancient texts | Universities, scholastic disputation, Islamic Golden Age science | Maintenance and expansion of classical knowledge |
| Scientific-Method Cognition | 1500–1800 CE | Empiricism; hypothesis testing; mechanistic worldview | Galileo, Newton, Bacon, Descartes | Industrial preconditions; global scientific networks |
| Industrial-Era Cognitive Expansion | 1800–1950 CE | Mass literacy; applied science; statistical reasoning | Steam engines, public education, modern medicine | Democratization of knowledge, rise of technical professions |
| Information Age Cognition | 1950–2000 CE | Digital computation; systems thinking; global connectivity | Computers, internet, cybernetics | Global information exchange, rapid innovation cycles |
| Networked Cognitive Ecology | 2000–Present | Collective intelligence; AI-augmented reasoning; data-driven decision-making | Social media, machine learning, knowledge graphs | Transformation of epistemic authority, real-time global coordination |

## Study

| **Year** | **Researcher(s)** | **Region** | **Study Description** | **Key Findings** |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1969 | Brent Berlin & Paul Kay | Various (Global) | *Basic Color Terms: Their Universality and Evolution* | Showed universal patterns in color naming across cultures despite linguistic differences. |
| 2002 | Peter Gordon | Amazon, Brazil (Pirahã tribe) | *Numerical Cognition in the Pirahã Tribe* | Found that Pirahã people have limited number words, affecting precise numerical cognition. |
| 1974 | Richard Nisbett & Yuri Miyamoto | USA and East Asia | *Culture and Cognitive Style* | Demonstrated East Asian holistic vs. Western analytic cognitive styles in perception and reasoning. |
| 1981 | Jean Lave | Liberia (tailors in Monrovia) | *Cognition in Practice: Mind, Mathematics, and Culture in Everyday Life* | Highlighted how mathematical cognition is situated in social and cultural contexts. |
| 1994 | Dan Sperber | General / Theoretical | *Explaining Culture: A Naturalistic Approach* | Argued that cognition is shaped by cultural environments and transmission mechanisms. |
| 2010 | Lera Boroditsky | Various (Tzeltal speakers in Mexico, etc.) | *How Language Shapes Thought* | Showed that languages with different spatial terms influence speakers’ spatial cognition. |
