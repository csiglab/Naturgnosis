# Cognitive

> In this note, the study of **`cognitive processes`** will be approached from a **foundational perspective**—we study problems and systems using both **top-down** and **bottom-up** analyses, rather than adopting an **epistemological framework** like that of **Cognitive Science**.
>

> There is no single definitive book in cognitive science that comprehensively covers all foundational systems and concepts. The field is inherently interdisciplinary, and key knowledge is distributed across works in psychology, neuroscience, linguistics, computer science, and philosophy.
>

> In cognitive science, the goal is to construct models of cognitive agents, formally characterize their cognitive processes, and explain how these processes are implemented and supported by underlying neurobiological mechanisms (i.e., the brain's hardware).
>

> Note: There are not great books to study cognitive sciences, so basically do papers.
>

Goals:

- In our study of cognition, we will examine cognitive processes as faculties of the brain, employing effective abstraction and neurobiological methods.
- Another part of our research will focus on the variation of cognition—that is, how different societies develop and use their shared cognitive faculties.

**Index Qaestionum**:

- How does the brain **process information**, and what neural **mechanisms underlie cognition**?
- How do people **perceive** and **interpret** sensory information, such as visual or auditory **input**?
- How do people **attend** to and focus on specific information, and what factors influence attentional processes?
- How do people **learn**, **remember**, and **retrieve information**, and what factors affect memory processes?
- How do people **understand** and **produce language**, and what neural and cognitive processes are involved?
- How do people **reason** and **make decisions**, and what factors influence these processes?
- How do people **solve problems**, and what cognitive strategies do they use?
- How do individual differences, such as age, personality, and culture, influence cognitive processes?
- How can cognitive science research inform the development of artificial intelligence and cognitive technologies?
- How to tell apart good and bad(just - so theories) theories that used adaptability arguments?
- How to capture the **evolutionary history** of neurobiological systems: neuron, etc?
- Is the concept of mind even a Scientific Concept or Just an **Effective Abstraction**?

## Ontology

> What do we observed (Study) in Cognitive Science?
>

> In cognitive science, modeling does not treat brain structures and cognitive functions as separate domains but as levels in a hierarchy: structures give rise to mechanisms, mechanisms support functions, and functions can crystallize into structural substrates for higher-order processes. For instance, memory may be understood both as a function of neural activity and as a structural foundation for reasoning. Cognition thus emerges from a recursive interplay between structure and function.
>

> **The "mind" is not a direct scientific concept like “neuron” or “force,” but rather an *effective abstraction* that can be decomposed into scientific constructs.** It acts as a conceptual umbrella for a set of cognitive functions — some of which are scientifically robust and operationalizable.
>

>

| **Category** | **Study Target** | **Description** | **Cognitive Research** | **Research Justification** |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Biological Basis** | Brain | Physical substrate of cognition, including structures, networks, and pathways | Study of neural networks, brain regions, connectivity, and neurophysiology | Understanding the brain’s structure and function is essential to explain how cognitive processes emerge and are implemented biologically |
|  | Neural Substrate | Biological mechanisms supporting cognition, from neurons to circuits | Examination of neurons, synapses, microcircuits, and neurophysiological mechanisms | Identifying neural substrates is crucial for connecting brain physiology to cognitive function and behavior |
| **Functional Level** | Cognition | Mental processes underlying perception, attention, memory, learning, reasoning, and decision-making | Experimental and computational studies on perception, attention, memory, learning, reasoning, decision-making | Studying cognition enables modeling, predicting, and improving human thought, behavior, and learning strategies |
|  | Language | Comprehension, production, and acquisition of language | Psycholinguistic experiments, corpus analysis, language modeling | Reveals underlying cognitive structures, aids in AI NLP, and informs developmental linguistics |
|  | Attention | Mechanisms by which the brain selects relevant stimuli and suppresses irrelevant information | Behavioral tasks (e.g., Stroop test), neuroimaging (fMRI, EEG) | Behavioral tasks (e.g., Stroop test), neuroimaging (fMRI, EEG) |
|  | Perception | How sensory information (visual, auditory, tactile) is detected, organized, and interpreted | Experiments on visual illusions, auditory processing, cross-modal integration | Explains human experience, behavior, and decision-making; foundational for AI models simulating perception |
|  | Decision Making | How choices are made under uncertainty or risk | Behavioral economics experiments, modeling (Bayesian, reinforcement learning) | Supports economics, psychology, AI, and policy-making |
|  | Reasoning | Strategies humans use to solve novel problems and infer new knowledge | Puzzle-solving tasks, computational modeling, cognitive task analysis | Insight into reasoning aids education, AI reasoning systems, and cognitive assessment |
|  | Learning | Acquisition of knowledge or skills through experience or instruction | Experiments on reinforcement learning, skill acquisition, neural plasticity | Central to education, AI algorithms, skill training, and rehabilitation |
| **Phenomenological Level** | Mind | Emergent phenomena arising from brain activity, including consciousness and self-awareness | Research on consciousness, subjective experience, thought processes, and introspection | Investigating the mind links neural activity to subjective experience and behavior, bridging biology and phenomenology |
| **Representational** | Representation | How knowledge, concepts, and experiences are encoded, stored, and manipulated | Study of memory encoding, symbolic vs. connectionist representations, and hybrid models | Understanding representations clarifies how information is processed, stored, and retrieved, informing both neuroscience and AI |
|  | Memory | Encoding, storage, and retrieval of information across short-term, working, and long-term memory | Memory recall tests, neuroimaging, computational modeling | Informs learning strategies, neurodegenerative disease understanding, and artificial memory systems |
| Social Cognition | Social Cognition | Understanding and predicting others’ thoughts, intentions, and behaviors | Theory of mind tasks, social interaction studies, neural correlates | Theory of mind tasks, social interaction studies, neural correlates |
| **Theoretical** | Cognitive Architecture | Theoretical frameworks describing the organization and dynamics of cognition | Modeling with frameworks like ACT-R, SOAR, LIDA, and other cognitive architectures | Provides systematic structure to simulate, understand, and replicate cognitive processes in AI, experiments, and theory |

## Epistemology

> How do we observed, model and validate in Cognitive System?
>

> Observing (How, What),  Modelling(How, What), Validation(How).
>

> **Note:** Cognitive science is not only experimental; it also relies on theoretical research is also a thing.
>

### **Observation**

| **Target System** | **Observation Technique** | **Description** |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Neural structures** (brain regions, circuits, cells) | fMRI, EEG/MEG, single-cell recording, optogenetics, lesion studies | Reveal activity patterns, connectivity, and causal contributions of brain structures to cognition. |
| **Physiological Dynamics** (neurochemistry, electrophysiology, blood flow) | PET scans, microdialysis, electrophysiology, neurochemical assays | Track metabolic activity, neurotransmitter changes, and time-resolved electrical activity underlying cognitive states. |
| **Behavioral Performance** (tasks, decisions, memory recall) | Reaction time tasks, accuracy measures, psychophysical experiments | Capture how cognitive functions manifest in observable performance. |
| **Cognitive Processes** (attention, memory, reasoning) | Controlled tasks, eye-tracking, dual-task interference, computational modeling | Infer the organization of mental operations and their constraints. |
| **Computational Modeling** | Cognitive architectures, Bayesian/connectionist models, symbolic simulations | Model internal information processing structures that explain behavior and neural data. |
| **Social & Interactive cognition** | Naturalistic observation, conversation analysis, virtual agents, neuroimaging during interaction | Explore cognition in real-world, social, or ecological contexts. |
| **Comparative Cognition** | Animal cognition tasks, maze tests | Studying cognition in animals to find similarities/differences |
| **Developmental Studies** | Infant studies, longitudinal designs | Cognitive growth, learning milestones |

### Modelling

> See more in [Modelling](note.html?n=nature/conceptual-model-natural-system/brain.md)
>

> How do we represent brain functions (and the underlying processes and systems)?
>

| **Modeling Technique** | **Description** | **Representation Capacity** |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Symbolic / Rule-Based Models** | Use explicit symbols and rules to represent knowledge and reasoning | Abstract concepts, logical relations, rules, and sequences |
| **Connectionist / Neural Networks** | Networks of simple units (neurons) that learn patterns through weighted connections | Distributed representations, patterns, associations, and learning dynamics |
| **Bayesian Models** | Probabilistic models using Bayes' theorem to represent uncertainty and update beliefs | Probabilistic reasoning, uncertainty, and inference |
| **Cognitive Architectures** | Integrated computational frameworks that simulate entire cognitive systems | Multi-process cognition: memory, perception, decision, action |
| **Dynamical Systems Models** | Models cognition as time-dependent changes in system state variables | Continuous processes, temporal dynamics, sensorimotor coordination |
| **Embodied / Enactive Models** | Models cognition as arising from interaction of agent and environment | Sensorimotor contingencies, real-time interaction dynamics |
| **Hybrid Models** | Combine symbolic, connectionist, and other approaches | Flexible, can represent both abstract knowledge and distributed patterns |

## Theory

> Universal ideas about how cognition works.
>

| **Theory** | **Description** | **View of Representation** | **Representation** |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Information Processing Theory** | Views cognition as a series of stages where information is input, processed, stored, and output. | Representation is internal, structured as symbols and data manipulated through sequential stages. | Symbolic, stage-based formats (e.g., input buffers, memory stores). |
| **Connectionism (Parallel Distributed Processing)** | Models cognition using networks of simple units that learn through weighted connections. | Representation is emergent, distributed across many nodes and weights. | Sub-symbolic, distributed activation patterns. |
| **Embodied Cognition** | Argues cognition arises through body-environment interactions, not just brain processes. | Representation is not central; cognition may occur without abstract representations. | Sensorimotor patterns, affordances, action-oriented representations. |
| **Bayesian Cognitive Science** | Uses probabilistic models and Bayesian inference to explain perception and reasoning under uncertainty. | Representation encodes probabilistic beliefs about the world. | Probability distributions, priors/posteriors. |
| **Cognitive Architecture (e.g., ACT-R, SOAR)** | Comprehensive models simulating multiple cognitive functions within a unified framework. | Representation integrates symbolic (rules, chunks) and sub-symbolic (activation, learning parameters). | Hybrid: symbolic rules + numerical parameters. |
| **Dynamical Systems Theory** | Describes cognition as continuous, time-dependent changes in states of complex systems. | Representation is often deemphasized; cognition is trajectories in a high-dimensional state space. | Continuous state trajectories, attractors, dynamics. |
| **Symbolic AI (Classical Cognitive Science)** | Treats cognition as manipulation of symbols and rules, akin to formal logic. | Representation is explicit, structured, and symbol-based. | Symbols, rules, syntactic structures. |
| **Distributed Cognition** | Emphasizes cognition distributed across people, tools, and environment rather than solely in the brain. | Representation exists not only in the mind but also in artifacts, environments, and social systems. | External artifacts, shared representations, cognitive scaffolds. |
| **Evolutionary Psychology** | Explains cognitive processes as adaptations shaped by natural selection. | Representation is domain-specific, tied to evolved cognitive modules. | Mental modules, adaptive heuristics, specialized schemas. |
| **Dual Process Theory** | Proposes two types of cognitive processes: fast, automatic (System 1) and slow, deliberate (System 2). | Representation differs between the two systems: intuitive, associative vs. explicit, rule-based. | Associative networks (System 1); symbolic rules (System 2). |
| **Society of Mind** | Proposes that intelligence emerges from interactions of numerous simple, mindless agents within the mind. | Representation is distributed across many specialized agents. | Local agent structures, rule-sets, micro-representations. |
| **Frame Theory** | Represents knowledge in structured forms called "frames," which divide knowledge into substructures for stereotyped situations. | Representation is hierarchical and structured into slots and fillers. | Frames, schemas, slot-filler structures. |
| **CLARION** | A hybrid cognitive architecture integrating implicit (connectionist) and explicit (symbolic) processes. | Representation combines unconscious (distributed) and conscious (symbolic) knowledge. | Dual: distributed neural patterns + explicit symbolic rules. |
