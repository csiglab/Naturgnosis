Formulation

What epistemic element type does this instance belong to?

Concrete Epistemic Artifact — one realized concept instance (here: Bird) — produced by applying an Epistemic Operator (intension checks and transformations), and usable as an Epistemic Standard (non-empty intension, intension–extension consistency, prototype existence). Schema: Philosophia Artium Epistemicarum et Operis.

What is this epistemic instance?

A Concept worked through the prototype Bird: intension (has feathers, has wings, lays eggs, can fly, warm-blooded) and extension (sparrow, eagle, robin; boundary cases penguin, ostrich, kiwi, bat, archaeopteryx). Type: a structured epistemic artifact partitioning reality into instances and non-instances.

What grounds this reading?

It encodes categorical affirmations about reality: things belong together by virtue of shared properties, enabling recognition, inference, and communication. Specialize narrows the extension, generalize broadens it, differentiate splits, abstract merges — each recorded in the transformation trace.

Type Definition

A Concept is a structured epistemic artifact that partitions reality into instances and non-instances through a definable intension (conditions) and observable extension (members).

Epistemic Role

Concepts encode categorical affirmations about reality: they assert that certain things belong together by virtue of shared properties, enabling recognition, inference, and communication.

Diagnostic questions

What fails membership?

Constraint indicators flip from satisfied to violated — the instance is rejected from the extension until its properties conform.

What moves the intension/extension boundary?

Specialize adds a property and narrows the extension; generalize removes one and broadens it; differentiate splits by distinguishing property; abstract removes distinguishing features.
