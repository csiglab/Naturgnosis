# Electrophoresis

> **Electrophoresis** is a laboratory technique used to separate molecules, such as proteins, nucleic acids (DNA, RNA), or other charged biomolecules, based on their size, charge, and other physical properties by applying an electric field.
>

## Index

## How It Works

- **Electric Field:** A sample is placed in a gel or another medium, and an electric field is applied. Because most biomolecules are charged (either positively or negatively), they will move in response to the electric field.
- **Separation:** The molecules move through a medium (usually a gel, like agarose or polyacrylamide) toward the electrode of opposite charge. The rate of movement depends on factors such as:
    - **Charge:** Molecules with a higher charge will move faster toward the opposite pole.
    - **Size:** Smaller molecules typically move more easily and faster through the gel compared to larger molecules, which get slowed down.
    - **Shape:** The shape of the molecule can also affect how it moves.

## Types of Electrophoresis

Here’s a **table of types of electrophoresis**, including their **principles**, **applications**, and **key features**:

| **Type of Electrophoresis** | **Principle** | **Applications** | **Key Features** |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Agarose Gel Electrophoresis** | Separates DNA or RNA fragments by size using an agarose gel matrix. | DNA/RNA analysis, PCR product verification, restriction enzyme digestion. | Simple, cost-effective, and suitable for large DNA fragments (100 bp to 25 kb). |
| **Polyacrylamide Gel Electrophoresis (PAGE)** | Separates proteins or small DNA/RNA fragments by size using a polyacrylamide gel. | Protein analysis, SDS-PAGE, DNA sequencing. | High resolution, suitable for small molecules (1–500 kDa for proteins). |
| **SDS-PAGE (Sodium Dodecyl Sulfate-PAGE)** | Separates denatured proteins by molecular weight using SDS to impart uniform charge. | Protein molecular weight determination, purity analysis. | Denatures proteins, provides high resolution for proteins. |
| **Native PAGE** | Separates proteins in their native (non-denatured) state based on size and charge. | Studying protein complexes, enzyme activity assays. | Preserves protein structure and function. |
| **Two-Dimensional Gel Electrophoresis (2D-GE)** | Separates proteins first by isoelectric point (pI) and then by molecular weight. | Proteomics, analyzing complex protein mixtures. | High-resolution separation of thousands of proteins. |
| **Capillary Electrophoresis (CE)** | Separates molecules in a thin capillary tube based on size and charge. | DNA sequencing, protein analysis, small molecule separation. | High efficiency, rapid analysis, and minimal sample volume. |
| **Pulsed-Field Gel Electrophoresis (PFGE)** | Separates large DNA molecules by alternating the direction of the electric field. | Genomic DNA analysis, bacterial strain typing. | Resolves very large DNA fragments (up to 10 Mb). |
| **Isoelectric Focusing (IEF)** | Separates proteins based on their isoelectric point (pI) in a pH gradient. | Protein purification, 2D-GE first dimension. | High resolution for proteins with different pI values. |
| **Immunoelectrophoresis** | Combines electrophoresis with antibody detection to identify specific proteins. | Clinical diagnostics, detecting immunoglobulins. | Identifies and quantifies specific proteins in complex mixtures. |
| **Agarose-Polyacrylamide Composite Gel** | Combines agarose and polyacrylamide for improved separation of large and small molecules. | Separating large DNA fragments and small proteins. | Combines the benefits of both agarose and polyacrylamide gels. |
| **Agarose-Starch Gel Electrophoresis** | Uses a starch-agarose matrix for separating proteins or nucleic acids. | Historical method for protein and enzyme analysis. | Less common today, replaced by PAGE and agarose gels. |
| **Agarose-Acrylamide Gradient Gel** | Uses a gradient of agarose and acrylamide for improved resolution. | Separating complex mixtures of proteins or nucleic acids. | Provides a broad separation range. |
| **RNA Electrophoresis** | Separates RNA molecules by size using denaturing conditions (e.g., formaldehyde). | RNA integrity analysis, Northern blotting. | Ensures RNA remains denatured and linear during separation. |
| **DNA Electrophoresis** | Separates DNA fragments by size using agarose or polyacrylamide gels. | DNA fragment analysis, cloning, genotyping. | Standard technique for DNA analysis. |
| **Protein Electrophoresis** | Separates proteins based on size, charge, or pI using various gel types. | Protein analysis, purity assessment, biomarker discovery. | Versatile technique for studying protein mixtures. |
