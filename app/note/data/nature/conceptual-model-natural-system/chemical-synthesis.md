# Chemical Synthesis

> **Chemical synthesis** refers to the process of creating new chemical compounds by combining or transforming simpler chemical substances through various chemical reactions.
>

## Taxonomy

Chemical synthesis can be classified into various types based on the reactions' complexity and the nature of the starting materials. These include:

- **Organic Synthesis** involves the creation of organic compounds containing carbon atoms using carbon-based starting materials and reactions.
- **Inorganic Synthesis:** This field focuses on creating inorganic compounds that do not contain carbon atoms using metal-based or other non-carbon-based starting materials.
- **Medicinal Chemistry:** This field involves explicitly the synthesis of compounds for pharmaceutical applications, such as drug candidates and active pharmaceutical ingredients (APIs).
- **Polymer Synthesis** involves creating polymers, which are large molecules composed of repeating units, from monomers through polymerization reactions.

Chemical synthesis is crucial in developing new materials, drugs, agrochemicals, and specialty chemicals, contributing to various industries and scientific advancements.

## Steps

Chemical synthesis involves several key steps:

1. **Design:** The process begins with designing the desired compound or target molecule. This involves determining the final product's chemical structure, properties, and desired characteristics.
2. **Selection of Starting Materials:** Next, suitable starting materials, reactants, or reagents are selected based on availability, cost, and compatibility with the desired synthetic pathway.
3. **Reaction:** The selected starting materials are subjected to one or more chemical reactions under specific conditions, such as temperature, pressure, and catalysts or solvents. These reactions may involve bond formation, bond cleavage, or other transformations to create the desired product.
4. **Purification:** After the reaction, the crude product is often purified to remove impurities and by-products. Purification techniques may include filtration, distillation, chromatography, and recrystallization.
5. **Characterization:** The purified product is then characterized using analytical techniques such as spectroscopy (e.g., NMR, IR, UV-Vis), chromatography, mass spectrometry, and elemental analysis to confirm its identity and purity.
