# Regulation

> ..
>

| **Regulatory Mechanism** | **Description** | **Examples in Living Systems** |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Negative Feedback** | Maintains homeostasis by counteracting changes from a set point. | Blood glucose regulation (insulin and glucagon); temperature regulation (sweating to cool down). |
| **Positive Feedback** | Amplifies changes, often leading to a definitive endpoint. | Childbirth (oxytocin during labor); blood clotting (platelet aggregation). |
| **Hormonal Regulation** | Chemical messengers regulate metabolism, growth, and other physiological processes. | Thyroid hormone (T4) for metabolism; cortisol for stress response; estrogen and testosterone for reproduction. |
| **Nervous System Regulation** | Rapid coordination of responses through electrical signals and neurotransmitters. | Reflex arc (e.g., withdrawal from heat); brain signaling for motor control. |
| **Enzyme Regulation** | Enzymes catalyze biochemical reactions and their activity is regulated by factors like concentration. | Phosphofructokinase in glycolysis; enzyme inhibition by products or external factors (e.g., drugs). |
| **Gene Expression Regulation** | Controls the expression of genes at transcriptional or translational levels. | Lac operon in bacteria; regulation of Hox genes in development. |
| **Immune System Regulation** | Defends the body against pathogens through a coordinated immune response. | Activation of T-cells and antibodies; inflammatory response. |
| **Circadian Rhythms** | Regulates biological processes in a 24-hour cycle, responding to light and darkness. | Melatonin release for sleep-wake cycles; regulation of body temperature and hormone release. |
