## Sources

> …
>
1. **Water (H₂O)**: Water is the most abundant source of hydrogen on Earth. Through processes like electrolysis, hydrogen can be extracted from water by splitting it into hydrogen and oxygen.
2. **Hydrocarbons**: Natural gas, oil, and coal contain hydrogen in the form of hydrocarbons (e.g., methane, CH₄). Steam methane reforming is a common industrial method to extract hydrogen from natural gas.
3. **Biomass**: Organic materials like plants, algae, and waste products contain hydrogen. Processes such as gasification or pyrolysis can release hydrogen from biomass.
4. **Methane Hydrates**: Found in ocean sediments and permafrost, methane hydrates are ice-like structures that trap methane molecules, which can be a source of hydrogen.
5. **Volcanic Gases**: Volcanic activity releases gases that sometimes contain small amounts of hydrogen.
6. **Geothermal Sources**: In some geothermal areas, hydrogen gas is naturally emitted from the Earth's crust.
7. **Biological Processes**: Certain microorganisms, such as algae and bacteria, produce hydrogen through metabolic processes like photosynthesis or fermentation.

## Uses

>

| **Category** | **Uses of Hydrogen** |
| --- | --- |
| **Industrial Applications** | Ammonia production (fertilizers) <br> - Petroleum refining (hydrodesulfurization, hydrocracking) <br> - Methanol production |
| **Energy and Fuel** | Fuel cells (electricity for vehicles, buildings) <br> - Rocket fuel <br> - Energy storage for renewables |
| **Chemical Production** | Hydrogenation of fats/oils <br> - Hydrochloric acid production |
| **Metal Processing** | Reducing agent for metal ores <br> - Welding and cutting metals |
| **Food Industry** | Food preservation <br> - Hydrogenated oils for food products |
| **Environmental Applications** | Carbon capture <br> - Clean energy transition (decarbonization) |
| **Electronics** | Semiconductor manufacturing <br> - Flat-panel display production |
| **Research and Development** | Nuclear fusion (deuterium and tritium) <br> - Experimental energy technologies |
| **Heating and Power Generation** | Hydrogen blending with natural gas <br> - Direct combustion for heat/electricity |
