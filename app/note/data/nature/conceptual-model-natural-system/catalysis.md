# Catalysis

> **Catalysis** is a process in chemistry where a substance (catalyst) facilitates a chemical reaction by lowering the activation energy required for the reaction to occur, thus increasing the rate of the reaction without being consumed in the process. Catalysis plays a crucial role in various industrial processes, environmental remediation, and biological systems.
>

> Catalysis encompasses various models and theories to describe the mechanisms and kinetics of catalytic reactions.
>

Some common models of catalysis include:

1. **Langmuir-Hinshelwood Mechanism**: This model proposes that catalysis occurs through the adsorption of reactant molecules onto the catalyst surface, followed by their reaction to form products.
2. **Eley-Rideal Mechanism**: In this mechanism, one reactant molecule adsorbs onto the catalyst surface, while the other reacts with the adsorbed species to form products.
3. **Mars-van Krevelen Mechanism**: This model involves the simultaneous oxidation or reduction of the catalyst surface and the reactants. It considers both the surface chemistry of the catalyst and the gas-phase reactions.
4. **Enzyme Catalysis Models**: Enzymes, being biological catalysts, follow specific models such as the Michaelis-Menten kinetics or allosteric regulation models.
5. **Transition State Theory**: This theoretical framework describes the kinetics of catalytic reactions by considering the formation and stability of transition states, which are intermediates in the reaction pathway.
6. **Surface Science Approaches**: These models utilize techniques from surface science to understand catalytic processes at the molecular level, including adsorption energies, surface coverage, and surface reaction kinetics.
7. **Computational Catalysis Models**: Computational methods, such as density functional theory (DFT) or molecular dynamics simulations, are used to predict and analyze catalytic reactions by modeling the interactions between catalysts and reactants at the atomic scale.

These models are often employed to elucidate the mechanisms of catalytic reactions, optimize catalyst design, and improve the efficiency of industrial processes.
