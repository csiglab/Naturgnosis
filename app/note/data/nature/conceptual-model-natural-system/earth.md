## Structure

> What is the structure of the earth? Is the idea of an earth layer an effective abstraction? How do we validate this structure?
>

| **Layer** | **Description** | **Material** | **Key Properties** |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Crust** | Outermost solid layer; includes continental and oceanic crust. | Solid rock | Thickness: 5–70 km; low density; rich in silicates |
| **Lithosphere** | Rigid outer shell including crust and uppermost mantle. | Solid | ~100 km thick; brittle; participates in tectonic motion |
| **Mantle** | Thick, mostly solid layer beneath the crust; convects slowly. | Solid (plastic behavior) | ~2,900 km thick; composed of peridotite; drives plate tectonics |
| **Asthenosphere** | Upper mantle zone below lithosphere; mechanically weak. | Partially ductile solid | Allows lithospheric plates to move; flows over geological time |
| **Outer Core** | Liquid layer surrounding the inner core; iron-nickel alloy. | Liquid | ~2,200 km thick; generates Earth's magnetic field via convection |
| **Inner Core** | Solid innermost layer; primarily iron-nickel alloy. | Solid | Radius ~1,220 km; extremely high pressure and temperature; rotates slowly |

## Earth Orbit Model

> The **length of Earth's orbit around the Sun** (approximately 365.25 days) is determined by several key factors related to the laws of physics and the characteristics of the Earth-Sun system.
>

Here are the primary factors that influence the orbital period:

1. **Distance from the Sun (Semi-Major Axis)**

- The average distance between the Earth and the Sun, known as the **semi-major axis**, is about **149.6 million kilometers** (93 million miles).
- According to **Kepler's Third Law of Planetary Motion**, the orbital period of a planet is directly related to its average distance from the Sun. The farther a planet is from the Sun, the longer its orbital period.

2. **Gravitational Force**

- The gravitational pull of the Sun keeps the Earth in its orbit.
- The strength of this force depends on the **mass of the Sun** and the **mass of the Earth**, as described by  **Newton's Law of Universal Gravitation**:
$F = \frac{G \cdot M \cdot m}{r^2}$.

3. **Orbital Velocity**

- The Earth's **orbital velocity** (speed at which it travels around the Sun) is about **107,000 km/h (67,000 mph)**.
- This velocity is a balance between the Sun's gravitational pull (which tries to pull the Earth inward) and the Earth's inertia (which tries to keep it moving in a straight line).
- The velocity determines how long it takes for the Earth to complete one full orbit.

4. **Shape of the Orbit (Eccentricity)**

- The Earth's orbit is not a perfect circle but an **ellipse** with a small eccentricity (0.0167).
- While the shape of the orbit affects the Earth's distance from the Sun at different points in the year (perihelion and aphelion), it does not significantly change the overall orbital period.

5. **Mass of the Earth**

- The Earth's mass influences its orbital period because it affects the gravitational interaction with the Sun.
- However, since the Sun's mass is so much larger, the Earth's mass has a relatively small effect on the orbital period.

6. **External Influences (Perturbations)**

- The gravitational pull of other celestial bodies, such as the Moon and other planets, can cause slight perturbations in the Earth's orbit.
- These influences are minor and do not significantly alter the Earth's orbital period.

## Does the length of a year change over time, and if so, what causes these variations?

### **Orbital Changes Affecting the Year’s Length**

Earth's orbit around the Sun is influenced by gravitational interactions with other planets, mainly Jupiter and Venus. These cause:

- **Secular changes**: Over **millions of years**, Earth's orbit slowly shifts due to gravitational perturbations.
- **Milankovitch cycles**: Eccentricity, obliquity, and precession affect Earth's position over tens to hundreds of thousands of years.

### Earth's Rotation is Slowing Down

Due to tidal interactions with the Moon, Earth's rotation is gradually slowing. This means:

- **Days are getting longer** by about **1.7 milliseconds per century**.
- If the length of a day changes, the number of days in a year (Earth’s orbits per rotation) changes slightly too.
- However, the total duration of a year in seconds remains **mostly stable** because Earth's orbital speed compensates for it.

### **Solar Mass Loss & General Relativity Effects**

- The Sun is **losing mass** as it radiates energy. A lower solar mass weakens its gravitational pull on Earth, causing Earth's orbit to slowly **expand** over time.
- According to **general relativity**, Earth's orbit very slightly shrinks due to gravitational wave emission, but this effect is minuscule compared to solar mass loss.
