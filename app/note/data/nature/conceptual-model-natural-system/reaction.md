# Reaction

> Chemical Reaction:  **Reactants → [Conditions + …]  Products.**
>

> A **chemical reaction** is a process in which one or more substances, called **reactants**, undergo a transformation to form one or more different substances, called **products**, involving the making and breaking of chemical bonds, resulting in a change in the molecular or ionic structure.
>

### Key points:

- Involves **rearrangement of atoms**.
- Changes **chemical composition**.
- Often accompanied by energy changes (heat, light, etc.).
- Governed by conservation laws (mass, energy).

## Reaction Modelling

> Chemical synthesis is creating chemical compounds by combining or transforming simpler substances into more complex ones through chemical reactions.
>

> "**Models of Chemical Reactions**" are theoretical frameworks or mathematical representations used to describe and predict the behavior and outcomes of chemical reactions based on principles of chemistry and physics.
>

> Chemical Process: Set of Chemical substances → Set of Chemical substances
>

> **A chemical reaction** is a kind of process in which interconversion of chemical species takes place. Because of this, we can say that a chemical reaction is a chemical process.
>

## Terminology

1. **Reactants**: Substances that undergo chemical changes in a reaction.
2. **Products**: New substances formed as a result of a chemical reaction.
3. **Chemical Equation**: Representation of a chemical reaction using chemical formulas and symbols.
4. **Stoichiometry**: The quantitative relationship between reactants and products in a chemical reaction.
5. **Balanced Equation**: A chemical equation where the number of atoms of each element is equal on both sides.
6. **Exothermic Reaction**: A reaction that releases heat to the surroundings.
7. **Endothermic Reaction**: A reaction that absorbs heat from the surroundings.
8. **Catalyst**: A substance that increases the rate of a chemical reaction without being consumed in the process.
9. **Reaction Rate**: The speed at which reactants are converted into products.
10. **Activation Energy**: The minimum energy required for a reaction to occur.
11. **Collision Theory**: The theory that chemical reactions occur when reactant molecules collide with sufficient energy and proper orientation.
12. **Reaction Mechanism**: The step-by-step sequence of elementary reactions by which a chemical reaction occurs.
13. **Equilibrium**: The state in which the rate of the forward reaction equals the rate of the reverse reaction, and the concentrations of reactants and products remain constant.
14. **Le Chatelier's Principle**: The principle stating that if a system in equilibrium is disturbed, it will adjust to counteract the disturbance and restore equilibrium.
15. **Redox Reaction**: A reaction involving the transfer of electrons between reactants.
16. **Acid-Base Reaction**: A reaction in which an acid reacts with a base to form a salt and water.
17. **Precipitation Reaction**: A reaction in which an insoluble solid (precipitate) forms from the mixing of two solutions.
18. **Combustion Reaction**: A reaction in which a substance reacts rapidly with oxygen, often producing heat and light.
19. **Substitution Reaction**: A reaction in which one atom or group of atoms is replaced by another.
20. **Electrolysis**: The process of using electrical energy to drive a non-spontaneous chemical reaction.
21. **Photochemical Reaction**: A reaction that requires light energy to proceed.

## Essential Laws Underlying Chemical Reactions

| Law | Governs | Key Idea |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Conservation of Mass | Mass | Mass constant during reaction |
| Definite Proportions | Composition | Fixed element ratios in compounds |
| Multiple Proportions | Composition of multiple compounds | Simple whole number ratios |
| Conservation of Energy | Energy | Energy conserved in reaction |
| Reaction Rate Laws | Kinetics | Reaction speed depends on concentrations |
| Equilibrium Law | Reaction equilibrium | Concentration ratio constant at equilibrium |
| Avogadro’s Law | Gaseous volumes | Equal volumes → equal molecules |
