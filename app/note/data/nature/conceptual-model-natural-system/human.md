## Weight Dynamics

### 🔑 **Energy Balance:**

- **Caloric surplus:** The most direct cause of weight gain is consuming **more calories than you burn** over time.
- Excess energy is stored primarily as **fat**.

### 🧠 **Hormonal and Biological Factors:**

- **Genetics:** Can influence metabolism, fat storage, appetite, and body shape.
- **Hormones:** Insulin, leptin, ghrelin, cortisol, thyroid hormones all regulate hunger, fat storage, and energy use.
- **Age:** Metabolism slows with age, making weight gain more likely.
- **Sex:** Men and women tend to store fat differently (visceral vs subcutaneous).

### 🍟 **Diet Composition:**

- **Ultra-processed foods:** High in sugar, fat, and salt promote overeating and are low in satiety.
- **Sugary beverages:** Provide calories without fullness (e.g., sodas, sweetened juices).
- **High portion sizes** and frequent snacking can also lead to unintentional overconsumption.

### 🪑 **Physical Activity:**

- **Sedentary lifestyle:** Less movement means fewer calories burned.
- **Low muscle mass:** Muscle burns more energy than fat, even at rest.

### 🧠 **Psychological Factors:**

- **Stress:** Can lead to emotional eating or higher cortisol, which promotes fat storage.
- **Depression/anxiety:** May affect eating habits and activity levels.
- **Sleep deprivation:** Linked to hormonal imbalances and increased cravings.

### 🌍 **Environmental and Social Factors:**

- **Food environment:** Easy access to high-calorie, low-nutrient foods.
- **Cultural norms:** Overeating during celebrations or in certain social settings.
- **Marketing & media:** Can drive preferences for unhealthy foods.
- **Economic factors:** Healthier food is often more expensive or less available.

### 💊 **Medical Conditions & Medications:**

- **Hypothyroidism**, **Cushing’s syndrome**, **PCOS**, and others can lead to weight gain.
- Some **medications** (antidepressants, antipsychotics, corticosteroids) can cause increased appetite or fluid retention.

## QA

### What percentage of body mass is cells?

> Approximately **50–65% of human body mass** is made up of **cells**.
>

> Cell **count** and cell **mass** are very different:
>
> - You have ~**30 trillion human cells**, but not all are equal in mass.
> - Muscle and fat cells contribute most to **weight**, while red blood cells dominate in **number**.

| **Component** | **% of Body Mass** | **Notes** |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Cells (total)** | ~50–65% | Includes muscle, fat, blood, organ, and skin cells |
| • **Muscle cells** | ~30–40% | Major contributor to cell mass |
| • **Fat cells** (adipocytes) | ~10–20% | Highly variable depending on individual |
| • **Blood cells** | ~7–10% | Mostly red blood cells |
| • **Organ and skin cells** | ~5–10% | Liver, brain, kidney, skin, etc. |
| **Non-cellular material** | ~35–50% | Includes water (outside cells), bone matrix, extracellular fluids |
