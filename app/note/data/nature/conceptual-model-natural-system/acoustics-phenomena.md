# Acoustics Phenomena

> **Acoustic Phenomena** are the interactions and behaviors of **mechanical waves in gases**, liquids, and solids, governed by the Navier-Stokes equations, wave equations, and boundary conditions, encompassing reflection, refraction, diffraction, interference, absorption, and nonlinear effects.
>

> **Acoustics** is the branch of physics that studies the generation, propagation, and reception of sound waves, analyzing wave mechanics, resonance, impedance, and transduction in various media through principles of continuum mechanics and thermodynamics.
>

**Sound**

- Sound is a **longitudinal pressure wave** that travels through an elastic medium.
- It is generated by the vibration of an object, which causes the surrounding medium (e.g., air molecules) to oscillate.
- These oscillations create regions of high pressure (**compressions**) and low pressure (**rarefactions**) that propagate away from the source.

### **Physical Properties of Sound Waves**

- **Frequency (f)**:
    - The number of oscillations per second, measured in **Hertz (Hz)**.
    - Determines the **pitch** of the sound (higher frequency = higher pitch).
- **Wavelength (λ)**:
    - The distance between two consecutive compressions or rarefactions.
    - Related to frequency by the formula: $\lambda = \frac{v}{f}$, where $v$ is the speed of sound in the medium.
- **Amplitude (A)**:
    - The maximum displacement of particles in the medium from their equilibrium position.
    - Determines the **loudness** of the sound (higher amplitude = louder sound).
- **Speed (v)**:
    - The speed at which the sound wave travels through the medium.
    - Depends on the medium's properties (e.g., density, elasticity).
    - In air at 20°C, the speed of sound is approximately **343 meters per second (m/s)**.
- **Phase**:
    - The position of a point in time on a waveform cycle.
    - Important for understanding interference and wave interactions.

## **Mathematical Representation**

> A simple sound wave (like a pure tone) can be represented as a **sine wave**:  $y(t) = A \cdot \sin(2\pi ft + \phi)$ :
>
- $y(t)$: Displacement of the medium at time $t$.
- $A$: Amplitude.
- $f$: Frequency.
- $\phi$: Phase angle.

> Complex sounds are represented as a sum of multiple sine waves (Fourier series):
$y(t) = \sum_{n=1}^{\infty} A_n \cdot \sin(2\pi f_n t + \phi_n)$:
>
- $A_n$: Amplitude of the n-th harmonic.
- $f_n$: Frequency of the n-th harmonic.
- $\phi_n$: Phase of the  n-th harmonic.

### **Propagation of Sound**

- Sound requires a medium to travel (it cannot propagate in a vacuum).
- The medium's properties (density, elasticity, temperature) affect the speed and behavior of sound waves.
- In air, sound waves spread out spherically from the source, losing energy (and thus amplitude) as they travel.

### **Perception of Sound**

- Sound waves are detected by the human ear and interpreted by the brain.
- The **ear** converts mechanical vibrations into electrical signals, which are processed to perceive:
    - **Pitch**: Determined by frequency.
    - **Loudness**: Determined by amplitude.
    - **Timbre**: Determined by the harmonic content and waveform shape.

### **Sound in Different Media**

- **Air**: Sound travels as longitudinal waves, with speed dependent on temperature and humidity.
- **Water**: Sound travels faster in water (~1,480 m/s) due to higher density and elasticity.
- **Solids**: Sound travels even faster in solids (e.g., ~5,000 m/s in steel) because of strong molecular bonds.

### **Technical Applications**

- **Acoustics**: Study of sound propagation, reflection, and absorption.
- **Signal Processing**: Analysis and manipulation of sound waves using tools like Fourier transforms.
- **Audio Engineering**: Recording, mixing, and reproducing sound.
- **Ultrasound Imaging**: Using high-frequency sound waves for medical imaging.
- **Sonar**: Using sound waves for navigation and detection underwater.
