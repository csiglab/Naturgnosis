# Suspension

> A suspension is a heterogeneous mixture in which solid particles are dispersed in a liquid but do not dissolve in it. These particles are typically large enough to eventually settle out under gravity if the mixture is left undisturbed.
>

### 🔑 Key Characteristics:

- **Dispersed phase:** solid
- **Continuous phase:** liquid
- **Particle size:** typically **> 1 µm** (micrometer)
- **Appearance:** cloudy or opaque
- **Stability:** unstable over time; particles tend to **sediment** unless stirred or stabilized

### 🧪 Common Examples:

- Mud (soil in water)
- Milk of magnesia (magnesium hydroxide in water)
- Some paints (solid pigments in water or oil)
- Flour in water

### 📊 Compared to Other Mixtures:

| Type | Particle Size | Visibility | Stability | Example |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Solution** | < 1 nm | Invisible | Very stable | Salt in water |
| **Colloid** | 1–1000 nm (1 µm) | Slightly visible under Tyndall effect | Stable | Milk (as emulsion) |
| **Suspension** | > 1000 nm (1 µm) | Visible | Unstable (settles) | Sand in water |
