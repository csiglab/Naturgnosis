# Mass

> Mass arises from the nonzero vacuum expectation value (VEV) of the Higgs field, leading to spontaneous symmetry breaking and giving mass to gauge bosons and fermions via Yukawa coupling terms.
>

> Mass is an **emergent property (????** 😇**)**: In contemporary physics, **mass is not a primitive input** but a **context-dependent emergent quantity**. For elementary particles in the Standard Model, mass arises via the **Higgs mechanism**, where fields acquire mass through interaction with a scalar field possessing a nonzero vacuum expectation value. For composite particles such as hadrons, mass is primarily the result of **nonperturbative dynamics of quantum chromodynamics (QCD)** — particularly confinement and chiral symmetry breaking — where mass emerges from the binding energy of massless or nearly massless constituents. Even in general relativity, mass is tied to the energy-momentum tensor and manifests as the curvature it induces in spacetime, further reinforcing its **relational and emergent character**.
>

> Mass is anything that makes something not move at the speed of light.
>

## Framework

> Mass can be classified into various types in physics based on different criteria.
>

Some common types of mass include:

1. Inertial mass: The measure of an object's resistance to changes in its motion, as described by Newton's second law of motion.
2. Gravitational mass: The property of an object that determines the strength of its gravitational attraction to other objects.
3. Rest mass: The mass of an object when it is at rest relative to an observer.
4. Relativistic mass is the mass of an object in motion relative to an observer, which increases with velocity according to Einstein's theory of special relativity.
5. Active mass: The mass that responds to forces applied to it, leading to acceleration.
6. Passive mass: The mass that responds to forces applied by other objects, leading to deformation or acceleration.
