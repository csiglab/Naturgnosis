## Taxonomy

Two main phases of **metabolism**:

1. **Catabolism**: The breakdown of molecules to produce energy, involving the input of nutrients and oxygen to generate ATP, carbon dioxide, and water.
2. **Anabolism**: The use of energy (ATP) to synthesize essential compounds like proteins and nucleic acids, producing complex molecules from simpler ones and generating waste products as a byproduct.

## Biochemical Metabolic Process

> A biochemical metabolic process is a **set of interconnected chemical reactions** within a living system that convert **nutrients** into **energy**, building blocks, and waste products to sustain life.
>

> Metabolism abstracts the flow of matter and energy through a system, emphasizing transformation, regulation, and integration rather than the specific molecular details.
>

> **Metabolism**:  {Nutrients (carbohydrates, proteins, fats), oxygen} → {Energy (ATP), carbon dioxide, water, waste products}
>

## Property

> Which properties are related with metabolic process ? Which things can be predicated on a metabolic process?
>

| **Property** | **Description** |
| --- | --- |
| **Energy transformation** | The process converts chemical energy in substrates into usable cellular energy (ATP, GTP). |
| **Material transformation** | Substrates (nutrients) are converted into biomolecules (proteins, lipids, nucleic acids) or waste products. |
| **Regulatory control** | Reaction rates and fluxes are modulated by enzymes, cofactors, and signaling pathways to maintain homeostasis. |
| **Systemic integration** | Metabolic reactions are interconnected and organized into pathways forming a coherent network. |
| **Dynamic** | Metabolism exists as a continuous flow of reactions; it is not static. |
| **Adaptability** | Metabolic fluxes can change in response to environmental or internal conditions. |
| **Thermodynamic constraints** | Reactions obey laws of energy conservation, free energy changes, and entropy; some are irreversible. |
| **Emergent function** | The process as a whole supports life functions (growth, repair, reproduction) beyond individual reactions. |
| **Relational existence** | Metabolism is defined relative to a living system and its environment; it has no independent existence. |

>

> **Nucleotide:**  A **nucleotide** is a **basic structural** unit of **nucleic acids**, such as DNA and RNA, composed of a nitrogenous base, a five-carbon sugar (**ribose** or **deoxyribose**), and one or more phosphate groups.

### Primary Human Organ System(s)

> **Note:** Every part of the human body participates in metabolism in some way. Metabolism is not confined to individual organs or tissues; rather, it is an **organizing principle** that integrates and coordinates the transformation of matter and energy across the entire organism. Each system contributes specific substrates, reactions, and regulatory functions, creating a network of interdependent metabolic processes.
>

| **Organ System** | **Description** | **Role in Metabolism** |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Digestive System** | Includes mouth, esophagus, stomach, intestines, liver, pancreas | Breaks down nutrients, absorbs macronutrients and micronutrients, provides substrates for metabolism |
| **Circulatory System** | Heart, blood vessels, blood | Transports nutrients, oxygen, hormones, and metabolic waste; distributes energy substrates to tissues |
| **Respiratory System** | Lungs, trachea, bronchi | Supplies oxygen for oxidative metabolism; removes CO₂ produced by catabolic processes |
| **Endocrine System** | Glands such as pancreas, thyroid, adrenal, pituitary | Regulates metabolism via hormones controlling energy storage, mobilization, and enzyme activity |
| **Muscular System** | Skeletal, cardiac, smooth muscles | Major site of energy consumption; metabolizes glucose, fatty acids, and amino acids for ATP production |
| **Liver** | Central organ of metabolism | Integrates carbohydrate, lipid, and protein metabolism; detoxifies metabolites; regulates blood glucose |
| **Adipose Tissue** | Fat storage tissue | Stores energy as triglycerides; releases fatty acids during energy demand |
| **Nervous System** | Brain, spinal cord, peripheral nerves | High energy demand; metabolizes glucose primarily; regulates systemic metabolic activities |
| **Urinary / Renal System** | Kidneys, ureters, bladder | Excretes metabolic waste; regulates water, ion balance, and pH affecting metabolic reactions |
| **Immune System** | Lymph nodes, spleen, white blood cells | Utilizes energy for immune responses; amino acid and nucleotide metabolism for proliferation |
| **Reproductive System** | Gonads, reproductive tract | Supports anabolic metabolism for gamete production; energy-intensive processes during pregnancy |

### Plant Metabolism

> **Note:** Like in animals, metabolism in plants is a network of interconnected biochemical processes that transform energy and matter to sustain growth, reproduction, and response to the environment. Plant metabolism is organized across cellular compartments and specialized organs (leaves, roots, stems, seeds) and integrates energy capture, nutrient assimilation, and biosynthesis.

### Toolset

> Which tools are used to investigate  metabolism?
>

| **Tool** | **Use** | **Role** |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Metabolomics** | Comprehensive profiling of metabolites in cells, tissues, or biofluids | Identifies metabolic states, pathways, and biomarkers; connects molecular metabolism to physiology and disease |
| **Mass Spectrometry (MS)** | Detects and quantifies metabolites, lipids, and proteins | High-sensitivity measurement of metabolic intermediates and flux |
| **Nuclear Magnetic Resonance (NMR) Spectroscopy** | Analyzes metabolite structures and concentrations | Provides structural information and quantification of metabolites in situ |
| **Stable Isotope Tracing** | Tracks labeled substrates (e.g., ¹³C-glucose) through pathways | Maps metabolic flux and pathway activity |
| **Enzyme Assays** | Measures activity of specific metabolic enzymes | Determines kinetics, regulation, and pathway functionality |
| **Flux Balance Analysis (FBA)** | Computational modeling of metabolic networks | Predicts pathway fluxes and emergent metabolic behavior |
| **Transcriptomics / Proteomics** | Measures gene and protein expression | Infers potential metabolic pathway activity and regulation |
| **CRISPR / Genetic Perturbation** | Knockout or modify genes involved in metabolism | Tests function of enzymes and pathways in vivo or in vitro |
| **Imaging Techniques (PET, MRI, Fluorescence)** | Visualize metabolic activity in tissues | Spatial mapping of metabolic processes in organisms |
| **High-Throughput Screening (HTS)** | Systematically tests small molecules on metabolic targets | Identifies modulators of metabolic pathways and enzymes |

### Modelling

> Which modelling methods  of frameworks are used to describe metabolic processes?
>

| Category | Method | Description | Use |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Constraint-Based** | **Flux Balance Analysis (FBA)** | Optimizes fluxes under steady-state and stoichiometric constraints | Predict growth, nutrient uptake, byproducts |
|  | **Flux Variability Analysis (FVA)** | Finds min/max fluxes per reaction near optimal objective | Assess network flexibility, essential reactions |
|  | **Gene Deletion Analysis** | Simulates knockout effects on fluxes | Identify lethal knockouts, drug targets |
| **Kinetic** | **Ordinary Differential Equations (ODEs)** | Models time-dependent metabolite dynamics | Simulate dynamic responses; mechanistic insight |
|  | **Metabolic Control Analysis (MCA)** | Quantifies enzyme control over fluxes | Identify key enzymes for pathway control |
| **Stoichiometric** | **Elementary Flux Modes (EFMs)** | Finds minimal feasible pathways at steady state | Analyze pathway routes, redundancy, capabilities |
| **Thermodynamics-Based** | **Thermodynamic FBA (TFBA)** | Adds thermodynamic feasibility to FBA | More physiologically realistic flux predictions |
| **Hybrid** | **Integrated FBA & Regulation** | Combines constraint-based, kinetic, and/or ML models | Bridges genome-scale overview with mechanistic detail |
