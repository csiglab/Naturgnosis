## Models of Neuron

> …
>

Here’s a table outlining key **neuronal models** commonly used in neuroscience, computational biology, and artificial intelligence:

| **Model** | **Description** | **Applications** |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **McCulloch-Pitts Model** | A simple binary threshold neuron model where the neuron fires if the weighted sum of inputs exceeds a certain threshold. | Basis for early neural network models and Boolean logic processing. |
| **Hebbian Learning** | A model of synaptic plasticity where neurons that fire together strengthen their connection. | Used to model learning and memory, basis for unsupervised learning methods. |
| **Leaky Integrate-and-Fire (LIF) Model** | A neuron integrates input signals over time and fires when the membrane potential reaches a threshold, with a "leak" for decaying input over time. | Simplified model for spiking neurons, used in studying neural coding and dynamics. |
| **Hodgkin-Huxley Model** | A detailed model of the action potential in neurons, accounting for ion channels and membrane potential dynamics. | Understanding the biophysics of neurons, action potential generation in the brain, and electrical activity in neurons. |
| **Izhikevich Model** | A computationally efficient model that captures different types of spiking neuron behaviors with fewer parameters than the Hodgkin-Huxley model. | Simulation of large-scale neural networks, modeling complex neuronal firing patterns like bursting and spiking. |
| **Wilson-Cowan Model** | Describes the dynamics of excitatory and inhibitory neuron populations using coupled differential equations. | Used to study population-level neural activity, cortical oscillations, and brain rhythms. |
| **FitzHugh-Nagumo Model** | A simplified version of the Hodgkin-Huxley model that focuses on the essential dynamics of action potentials, using fewer variables. | Applied to study excitable systems, including cardiac cells and neurons. |
| **Spiking Neural Network (SNN)** | A type of neural network that includes temporal dynamics, where neurons communicate by discrete spikes, capturing time-based activity. | Brain-inspired AI, neuromorphic computing, and real-time decision-making models. |
| **Resonator Neurons** | Neurons that respond preferentially to certain frequencies or patterns of input, reflecting the natural oscillations in neural circuits. | Studying brain oscillations, cognitive rhythms, and signal processing in neural systems. |
| **Liquid State Machine (LSM)** | A type of spiking neural network that processes temporal information, inspired by how neurons communicate using spikes over time. | Real-time computing, neuromorphic hardware, and adaptive control systems. |
| **Integrate-and-Fire Model** | A simplified model where the neuron integrates input signals and fires a spike when a threshold is reached. The process repeats after a reset. | Used to study spiking neuron dynamics and basic neural circuits, as well as large-scale simulations of brain activity. |

## Evolutionary History

> …
>

| **Stage** | **Key Events** | **Timeframe** | **Examples/Evidence** |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Origin of Neurons** | Neurons evolved in early multicellular animals. | ~600–700 million years ago (mya) | Common ancestor of cnidarians (e.g., jellyfish) and bilaterians. |
| **Early Nervous Systems** | Development of **nerve nets** (diffuse, decentralized networks). | ~600 mya | Cnidarians (e.g., jellyfish) and ctenophores (comb jellies). |
| **Evolution of Synapses** | Emergence of synaptic proteins (e.g., neurotransmitters, ion channels). | ~600 mya | Conserved synaptic genes in all animals. |
| **Diversification of Neurons** | Neurons specialized into sensory, motor, and interneurons. | ~540 mya (Cambrian explosion) | Bilaterians (e.g., vertebrates, insects, worms). |
| **Centralization** | Nervous systems became centralized with ganglia and nerve cords. | ~540 mya | Evolution of brains in bilaterians. |
| **Complex Nervous Systems** | Development of complex brains and sensory organs. | ~500 mya | Vertebrates (e.g., fish, mammals) and invertebrates (e.g., octopuses, insects). |
| **Myelination** | Evolution of myelin for faster signal transmission. | ~300 mya | Early vertebrates. |
| **Synaptic Plasticity** | Emergence of mechanisms for learning and memory. | ~300 mya | Vertebrates and some invertebrates. |
| **Modern Nervous Systems** | Highly complex brains with specialized regions (e.g., cortex, cerebellum). | ~200 mya to present | Mammals, birds, and cephalopods. |
