# Heredity

> A very complicated term -probably not even a concept - but a set of concepts related with a term.
>

> One has to be extreamly careful with the used of term.
>

## List of Theories

> …
>

| **Category** | **Theory/Concept** | **Key Features** | **Status** | **Date** |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Classical Theories** | Pangenesis | Traits transmitted by "gemmules" from all body parts. | Disproved | ~400 BCE (Hippocrates), ~1868 (Darwin) |
|  | Preformationism | Traits arise from miniature organisms (homunculi) in sperm or egg. | Disproved | 17th–18th Century |
|  | Blending Inheritance | Offspring are a mix of parental traits. | Disproved | 18th–19th Century |
| **Mendelian Genetics** | Law of Segregation | Each organism inherits two alleles per trait; alleles segregate during gamete formation. | Accepted | 1865 (Gregor Mendel) |
|  | Law of Independent Assortment | Genes for different traits assort independently during meiosis. | Partially Accepted (with exceptions due to linkage) | 1865 (Gregor Mendel) |
|  | Dominance | Dominant alleles mask recessive alleles in heterozygotes. | Accepted | 1865 (Gregor Mendel) |
| **Chromosomal Theory** | Chromosomal Theory of Inheritance | Genes are located on chromosomes; aligns with Mendelian inheritance during meiosis. | Accepted | 1902 (Sutton & Boveri) |
| **Molecular Biology** | DNA as the Hereditary Material | DNA carries genetic information; proved by Griffith, Avery, and others. | Accepted | 1928 (Griffith), 1944 (Avery), 1952 (Hershey & Chase) |
|  | Central Dogma | Genetic information flows DNA → RNA → Protein. | Accepted | 1958 (Francis Crick) |
|  | Mutations | Changes in DNA can alter traits and lead to evolution or disease. | Accepted | Early 20th Century |
| **Epigenetics** | Epigenetic Inheritance | Heritable changes in gene expression without DNA sequence changes (e.g., DNA methylation). | Accepted | 1942 (Waddington coined "epigenetics") |
| **Population Genetics** | Gene Pool | Total alleles in a population contribute to genetic variation. | Accepted | Early 20th Century |
|  | Hardy-Weinberg Principle | Describes genetic equilibrium in ideal populations. | Accepted | 1908 (Hardy & Weinberg) |
|  | Evolutionary Forces | Mutation, selection, gene flow, drift, and mating shape genetic variation over time. | Accepted | Early 20th Century |
| **Modern Developments** | Genomics | Study of entire genomes to understand complex traits and heredity. | Ongoing Research | 1990s (Human Genome Project began) |
|  | CRISPR and Gene Editing | Direct modification of DNA to alter hereditary traits. | Emerging Technology | 2012 (CRISPR breakthrough by Doudna & Charpentier) |
