# Electron

> An electron is a subatomic particle with a negative electric charge, found in all atoms, and is fundamental to electricity, magnetism, and chemical bonding due to its role in atomic structure and interactions.
>

> Electrons exhibit both particle-like and wave-like properties, a concept known as wave-particle duality. While they can be described as particles with mass and charge, they also demonstrate wave behavior, such as interference and diffraction, according to quantum mechanics.
>

## What is an electron — particle, wave, or something else entirely?

> Note: Some of this notion will be ‘effective theories’.
>

> **Note:** The idea of an *electron* is an **abstraction**, because what we call an "electron" is actually a **quantized excitation** of an underlying quantum field (the Dirac field). It is not a small physical object in space, but a localized, discrete manifestation of a deeper, field-theoretic structure.
>

> The electron **is not a particle traveling through space.**
>
>
> It is **an excitation of an underlying quantum field** that interacts, evolves, and manifests particle-like behavior during observation.
>

When we observe an electron:

- We detect a **point-like impact**.
- This is due to **quantum collapse** or decoherence, not because the electron *is* a particle.

When physicists say:

> “The electron is a particle,”
>

They **do not** mean:

- A tiny solid object like a billiard ball.
- A classical object flying through space with a definite position and velocity.

They **mean**:

- A **discrete, quantized** excitation of a **quantum field**.
- A mathematical abstraction that:
    - Carries specific **quantum numbers** (e.g. charge, spin, mass).
    - Interacts in well-defined ways.
    - Leaves **point-like traces** in detectors (e.g. cloud chambers, silicon sensors).
