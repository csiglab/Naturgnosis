# Biomolecule

> **Chemists** investigate the structure, function, and interactions of biological molecules, including proteins, nucleic acids, and carbohydrates. This can include studying the mechanisms of enzyme catalysis and metabolic pathways and the development of drugs and therapeutic agents.
>

## Taxonomy

> Biomolecules are organic molecules essential for life, including carbohydrates, lipids, proteins, and nucleic acids.
>

Below is a **table of biomolecules**, including their **types**, **functions**, **examples**, and **key features**:

| **Biomolecule** | **Types** | **Functions** | **Examples** | **Key Features** |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Carbohydrates** | Monosaccharides, Disaccharides, Polysaccharides. | Energy storage, structural support, cell signaling. | Glucose, sucrose, starch, cellulose. | Composed of carbon, hydrogen, and oxygen (C:H:O = 1:2:1). |
| **Lipids** | Fats, oils, phospholipids, steroids, waxes. | Energy storage, cell membrane structure, signaling molecules. | Triglycerides, cholesterol, phospholipids. | Hydrophobic; composed of fatty acids and glycerol. |
| **Proteins** | Enzymes, structural proteins, transport proteins, antibodies, hormones. | Catalysis, structural support, transport, immune response, signaling. | Hemoglobin, insulin, collagen, antibodies. | Composed of amino acids; have primary, secondary, tertiary, and quaternary structures. |
| **Nucleic Acids** | DNA, RNA. | Storage and transmission of genetic information, protein synthesis. | DNA (deoxyribonucleic acid), RNA (ribonucleic acid). | Composed of nucleotides (sugar, phosphate, nitrogenous base). |
| **Vitamins** | Fat-soluble (A, D, E, K), water-soluble (B-complex, C). | Cofactors for enzymes, antioxidants, bone health, vision. | Vitamin C (ascorbic acid), Vitamin D (calciferol). | Organic compounds required in small amounts for metabolic processes. |
| **Hormones** | Peptide hormones, steroid hormones, amino acid-derived hormones. | Regulation of physiological processes (growth, metabolism, reproduction). | Insulin, estrogen, adrenaline. | Act as signaling molecules; produced by endocrine glands. |
| **Enzymes** | Oxidoreductases, transferases, hydrolases, lyases, isomerases, ligases. | Catalyze biochemical reactions. | Amylase, DNA polymerase, catalase. | Proteins with specific active sites; highly efficient and specific. |
| **Nucleotides** | ATP, GTP, cAMP, NADH. | Energy transfer, signaling, coenzymes. | ATP (adenosine triphosphate), NADH (nicotinamide adenine dinucleotide). | Composed of a sugar, phosphate group, and nitrogenous base. |
| **Amino Acids** | Essential, non-essential. | Building blocks of proteins, precursors for other molecules. | Alanine, glycine, lysine. | 20 standard amino acids; differ in side chains (R groups). |
| **Fatty Acids** | Saturated, unsaturated, trans fats. | Energy storage, cell membrane components. | Palmitic acid, oleic acid, linoleic acid. | Long hydrocarbon chains with a carboxyl group. |
| **Steroids** | Cholesterol, sex hormones, corticosteroids. | Cell membrane structure, signaling molecules. | Testosterone, estrogen, cortisol. | Four-ring structure; derived from cholesterol. |
| **Polysaccharides** | Starch, glycogen, cellulose, chitin. | Energy storage (starch, glycogen), structural support (cellulose, chitin). | Starch (plants), glycogen (animals), cellulose (plant cell walls). | Long chains of monosaccharides; may be branched or unbranched. |
| **Glycoproteins** | Membrane proteins, antibodies. | Cell-cell recognition, immune response. | Antibodies, MHC proteins. | Proteins with attached carbohydrate chains. |
| **Lipoproteins** | HDL, LDL, VLDL. | Transport lipids in the bloodstream. | High-density lipoprotein (HDL), low-density lipoprotein (LDL). | Complexes of lipids and proteins. |
| **Nucleoproteins** | Chromatin, ribosomes. | DNA packaging, protein synthesis. | Histones, ribosomes. | Complexes of nucleic acids and proteins. |
