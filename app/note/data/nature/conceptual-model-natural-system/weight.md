# Weight

> The force of gravity acting on a mass.
>

> In physics, "weight" refers to the force exerted on an object due to gravity. Weight measures the gravitational force acting on an object's mass and is often expressed in force units, such as newtons $(N)$ or pounds-force (lbf).
>

Here are some key points about **weight in physics**:

In physics, weight is the force exerted on an object due to gravity. It is typically denoted by the symbol $(W)$ and is measured in newtons $(N)$. The weight of an object can be calculated using the formula:

$W = m \cdot g$

where:

- $(W)$ is the weight of the object,
- $(m)$ is the mass of the object,
- $(g)$ is the acceleration due to gravity.

The value of  $(g)$ varies depending on the location. On the Earth's surface, the standard acceleration value due to gravity is approximately $(9.81 , \text{m/s}^2 )$ .
