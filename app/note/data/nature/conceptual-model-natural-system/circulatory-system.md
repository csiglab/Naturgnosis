# Circulatory System

> The **circulatory system** is a network of blood vessels, including the heart, arteries, veins, and capillaries, responsible for transporting oxygen, nutrients, hormones, and waste products throughout the body to maintain homeostasis and support cellular functions.

## Structure

>

| **Component** | **Description** | **Structure** | **Key Biomolecules** |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Heart** | A muscular organ that pumps blood through the body. | Composed of four chambers: two atria (upper chambers) and two ventricles (lower chambers). | **Cardiac muscle cells**, **Myosin**, **Actin**, **Troponin** |
| **Atria** | The two upper chambers of the heart that receive blood. | Thin-walled chambers that pump blood into the ventricles. | **Cardiac muscle**, **Endocardium** |
| **Ventricles** | The two lower chambers of the heart that pump blood out to the body or lungs. | Thick-walled chambers; the left ventricle is more muscular because it pumps blood to the body, while the right ventricle pumps blood to the lungs. | **Cardiac muscle**, **Myocardium**, **Endocardium** |
| **Heart Valves** | Four one-way valves that prevent blood backflow: mitral, tricuspid, aortic, and pulmonary. | Composed of fibrous tissue that opens and closes in response to pressure changes. | **Collagen**, **Elastin**, **Fibroblasts** |
| **Pericardium** | The protective sac surrounding the heart. | A double-layered membrane that provides structural protection and reduces friction during heartbeats. | **Fibrous tissue**, **Serous fluid** |
| **Blood Vessels** | Tubes that carry blood throughout the body. | Includes arteries, veins, and capillaries. Each vessel type has unique structural features. | **Smooth muscle**, **Endothelium**, **Elastin**, **Collagen** |
| **Arteries** | Blood vessels that carry oxygenated blood away from the heart (except the pulmonary artery). | Thick, muscular walls to withstand high pressure; internal lining of endothelial cells. | **Smooth muscle**, **Elastin**, **Collagen**, **Endothelium** |
| **Veins** | Blood vessels that return deoxygenated blood to the heart (except the pulmonary veins). | Thin-walled with larger lumens; valves prevent backflow and ensure unidirectional blood flow. | **Smooth muscle**, **Endothelium**, **Valves** |
| **Capillaries** | Tiny blood vessels that facilitate exchange of gases, nutrients, and waste. | Very thin walls (one endothelial cell thick) to allow diffusion of gases and nutrients. | **Endothelial cells**, **Basement membrane** |
| **Arterioles** | Small arteries that lead into capillaries. | Thin muscular walls that regulate blood flow into capillary beds by constriction or dilation. | **Smooth muscle**, **Endothelium** |
| **Venules** | Small veins that drain blood from capillaries. | Thin-walled vessels that collect blood from capillaries and transport it to larger veins. | **Smooth muscle**, **Endothelium** |
| **Blood** | The fluid that circulates in the circulatory system. | Composed of plasma, red blood cells, white blood cells, and platelets. | **Plasma proteins** (e.g., albumin, fibrinogen), **Hemoglobin** |
| **Lymphatic Vessels** | Vessels that carry lymph, a fluid containing immune cells, waste, and proteins. | Thin-walled vessels with valves that transport lymph fluid to lymph nodes and back to the bloodstream. | **Lymphocytes**, **Lipids**, **Proteins** |
| **Lymph Nodes** | Small bean-shaped structures that filter lymph and store immune cells. | Consist of a capsule, outer cortex, inner medulla, and germinal centers. | **Lymphocytes**, **Macrophages**, **Dendritic cells** |

## Process

> …
>

| **Process** | **Description** |
| --- | --- |
| **Circulation** | The continuous flow of blood through the heart, arteries, veins, and capillaries to transport nutrients, gases, and waste products. |
| **Systemic Circulation** | The movement of oxygenated blood from the heart to the body’s tissues and the return of deoxygenated blood to the heart. |
| **Pulmonary Circulation** | The flow of deoxygenated blood from the heart to the lungs for oxygenation and back to the heart. |
| **Heartbeat (Cardiac Cycle)** | The rhythmic contraction (systole) and relaxation (diastole) of the heart muscle to pump blood. |
| **Oxygen Transport** | The binding of oxygen to hemoglobin in red blood cells for delivery to tissues. |
| **Carbon Dioxide Removal** | The transport of carbon dioxide from the tissues to the lungs for exhalation. |
| **Nutrient and Waste Exchange** | The exchange of oxygen, nutrients, and waste products between blood and tissues through capillaries. |
| **Blood Pressure Regulation** | The maintenance of pressure within blood vessels to ensure proper blood flow. |
| **Vascular Resistance** | The opposition to blood flow through blood vessels, largely determined by the diameter of arterioles. |
| **Cardiac Output** | The amount of blood the heart pumps per minute, determined by stroke volume and heart rate. |
| **Hemostasis** | The process that stops bleeding by clotting blood vessels in response to injury. |
| **Platelet Aggregation** | Platelets aggregate at the site of injury to form a temporary plug to prevent blood loss. |
| **Clot Formation** | The transformation of fibrinogen into fibrin, which forms a blood clot to seal a wound. |
| **Vasoconstriction** | The narrowing of blood vessels to increase blood pressure and reduce blood flow to non-essential areas. |
| **Vasodilation** | The widening of blood vessels to increase blood flow and decrease blood pressure. |
| **Lymphatic Drainage** | The process where excess interstitial fluid is collected by the lymphatic vessels and returned to the circulatory system. |
| **Fluid Exchange** | The exchange of water, nutrients, and waste between blood and interstitial fluid across capillary walls. |
