# Earth Crust

> The crust is the solid outer layer of the Earth, forming the surface we live on. It varies in composition, thickness, and properties depending on location.
>

## Taxonomy

| **Type** | **Description** | **Thickness** | **Material** | **Key Features** |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Continental Crust** | Forms the continents; older and more complex than oceanic crust. | ~30–70 km | Granitic rocks, sedimentary layers, metamorphic rocks | Low density (~2.7 g/cm³), supports mountains and continental landforms |
| **Oceanic Crust** | Forms the ocean floors; younger and thinner. | ~5–10 km | Basaltic and gabbroic rocks | Higher density (~3.0 g/cm³), tectonically recycled at subduction zones |
| **Upper Crust** | Uppermost portion of crust; brittle behavior. | Variable | Granites, sandstones, limestones | Earthquake focus often occurs here; mined for resources |
| **Lower Crust** | Deeper portion; more ductile under pressure. | Variable | Gabbros, metamorphic rocks | Transitions mechanically into the lithospheric mantle |

## Sedimentary Cover

> Lies above the **upper continental crust**; thickness ranges from a few centimeters to several meters.
>

## Soil

> The upper layer of loose, unconsolidated material that supports plant life; rich in organic matter.
>

> Soil is a subset of the sedimentary cover.
