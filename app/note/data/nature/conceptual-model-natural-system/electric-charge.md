# Electric Charge

> In this note, we explore one of the most fundamental concepts in physics — the notion of **electric charge**.
>

> **Electric Charge** is a fundamental physical property of matter, **carried (associated with) by particles** such as **electrons** (negative charge), **protons** (positive charge), and **ions** (atoms or molecules with a net charge) or other larger objects. In the presence of an **electric** or **magnetic field**,  charged particles experience forces and interact according to the laws  of electromagnetism.
>

> **Electric charge** is quantized, conserved, and plays a  central role in the structure of matter, the behavior of particles, and  the dynamics of electromagnetic phenomena, from atomic scales to cosmic  scales.
>

> **Electrical Phenomena**:  Electrical phenomena are physical occurrences that arise from the presence, movement, or interaction of electric charges. They are manifestations of the electromagnetic force, one of the four fundamental forces in nature, and encompass a wide range of observable effects, from microscopic charge separation to macroscopic current flow.
>

## Mater

> Which are the carrier of **Electric Charger**?
>

> See [Fundamental Interaction](note.html?n=nature/conceptual-model-natural-system/fundamental-interaction.md)
>

| Carrier | Description |
| --- | --- |
| Electron | A fundamental particle with negative electric charge; responsible for most electrical conduction in atoms and solids. |
| Proton | A positively charged particle in atomic nuclei; contributes to overall charge in matter. |
| Ion | An atom or molecule that has gained or lost electrons, carrying a net positive or negative charge. |
| Quark | Fundamental constituents of protons and neutrons; carry fractional electric charges. |
| Macroscopic bodies (e.g., humans, objects) | Collections of atoms and molecules can carry an **overall net charge** due to an excess or deficit of electrons. Humans, for example, can become electrically charged through friction (triboelectric effect) and discharge through contact or grounding. |
| Holes | Conceptual carriers in semiconductors, representing the absence of an electron in a lattice; behave as positive charges. |
| Muon | Heavier cousin of the electron; carries negative electric charge and can exist in cosmic rays and particle accelerators. |

## Change

> How the charge of an object change?
>

| Action | Change |
| --- | --- |
| **Friction (Triboelectric Effect)** | Transfer of electrons between materials in contact; object can gain or lose charge. |
| **Conduction** | Direct contact with a charged body allows electrons to move, equalizing charge. |
| **Induction** | Redistribution of charges within an object due to nearby electric fields without direct contact. |
| **Ionization** | Gain or loss of electrons through exposure to ionizing radiation or plasma. |
| **Electrolysis / Chemical Reactions** | Transfer of electrons during chemical reactions can change net charge of objects. |
| **Discharge / Grounding** | Excess charge is neutralized by flow of electrons to or from a large reservoir (like the Earth). |

## Phenomena

> What phenomena happens to an object when the charge changes?
>

> See more in [Electric Current](note.html?n=nature/conceptual-model-natural-system/electric-current.md),
>

| Phenomenon | Description |
| --- | --- |
| **Attraction (Repulsion)** | The object experiences forces with other charged bodies according to Coulomb’s law. |
| **Polarization** | Redistribution of charges within or on nearby neutral objects due to the object’s electric field. |
| **Electrostatic Discharge (ESD)** | Sudden flow of charge to neutralize potential differences; may produce sparks or current. |
| **Electric Potential Change** | The object’s electric potential relative to its environment changes, influencing further charge movement. |
| **Energy Storage (Release)** | The object stores electrostatic energy; changes in charge alter this energy. |
| **Mechanical Effects** | High charge densities can create repulsive forces that deform the object or surrounding materials. |
| **Electric Current** | Phenomenon resulting from the movement of electric charges; measurable as flow rate (Amperes). |

### At the most fundamental level, how is electric charge transmitted? Is it through the movement of charged particles?

> At the most fundamental level, electric charge is transmitted through the exchange of photons, which mediate the electromagnetic interaction between charged particles. This process is described by quantum electrodynamics (QED), where **charged particles (such as electrons)** interact by emitting and absorbing **virtual photons**. These virtual photons are the carriers of the electromagnetic force, facilitating the transfer of energy and momentum between charged particles. Thus, the movement of charged particles and their interactions are fundamentally governed by the exchange of these force-carrying photons.
>

> Note: Virtual Photons are abstractions or mathematical tools used in quantum field theory (QFT) to describe interactions, rather than real, observable particles.
>

### Is electric charge a fundamental property of matter at its most basic level, or does it emerge as a consequence of more fundamental interactions and symmetries in certain physical systems?

- **From the perspective of the Standard Model**, electric charge is a **fundamental property** of matter, tied to the $U(1$) gauge symmetry of electromagnetism and intrinsic to elementary particles.
- **From a broader perspective**, particularly in condensed matter physics or theoretical frameworks beyond the Standard Model, electric charge can also be viewed as an **emergent property**, arising from more fundamental interactions or collective behavior.
