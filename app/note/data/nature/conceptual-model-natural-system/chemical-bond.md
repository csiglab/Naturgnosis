# Chemical Bond

> A **chemical bond** is the association of [atoms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atom) or [ions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ion) to form [molecules](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Molecule), [crystals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crystal), and other structures.
>

## 🧭 Historical Development

| **Era** | **Discovery / Contribution** | **Key Figures** |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Ancient** | Philosophical ideas of elements and matter (Earth, Air, Fire, Water) | Greeks like **Empedocles**, **Democritus** |
| **1600s–1700s** | Observation of regular *reactions* between elements (e.g. acids + bases), but no bond theory yet | **Robert Boyle**, **Lavoisier** |
| **Early 1800s** | **Dalton** introduces atomic theory: atoms combine in fixed ratios | **John Dalton** |
| **1810s–1830s** | First use of **"affinity"** to describe how atoms stick together | **Berzelius**, **Davy** |
| **1850s–1900s** | Structural formulas; **Covalent bonds** emerge from molecular models | **Kekulé**, **Cannizzaro**, **Lewis** |
| **1916** | **Gilbert N. Lewis** proposes **electron pair bonds (covalent)** | “Lewis structures” |
| **1920s–1930s** | Quantum mechanics describes bonding using **wave functions** | **Heitler & London**, **Pauling** |
| **1939** | **Linus Pauling** formalizes chemical bond types in *The Nature of the Chemical Bond* | Nobel Prize 1954 |

## 🔬 What *Characterizes* a Chemical Bond?

> A **bond** is defined as a **force of attraction** that holds atoms together in a **stable arrangement**.
>

> Chemical bonds were discovered *gradually*, evolving from **philosophy to experimental science** to **quantum theory**.
>

> A bond is characterized by the **energetic and spatial arrangement** of **electrons and nuclei** in a **stable configuration**.
>

The characteristics vary by bond type, but here are **general features**:

| **Feature** | **Description** |
| --- | --- |
| **Electron Interaction** | Bonds involve **sharing, transferring, or pooling** of electrons |
| **Bond Energy** | The **energy required to break** the bond; reflects its strength |
| **Bond Length** | The distance between bonded atoms at which the system is most stable |
| **Polarity** | Difference in **electronegativity** can lead to **polar** or **nonpolar** bonds |
| **Directionality** | Covalent bonds are **directional** (specific angles); ionic bonds are not |
| **Bond Type** | Covalent, Ionic, Metallic, Hydrogen, etc., each with different **mechanisms** |
| **Quantum Nature** | Bonds arise from **overlapping electron orbitals** and **minimized energy** |

## Taxonomy

> Chemical bonds are the intermolecular forces that bind atoms into stable molecular structures.
>

> How elements interact  and how a molecular structure is form or change?
>

> How  do we know which forces are keeping together a molecular structure?
>

| **Category** | **Bond Type** | **Definition** | **Strength** | **Occurs Between** | **Example** |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Primary (Intramolecular) | **Covalent Bond** | Atoms share electrons | Strong | Nonmetals | H₂O, CO₂ |
|  | **Ionic Bond** | Electron transfer forms charged ions | Strong | Metal + Nonmetal | NaCl, MgO |
|  | **Metallic Bond** | Delocalized electrons shared in a metal lattice | Moderate-Strong | Metals | Cu, Fe |
|  | **Coordinate Bond** | Both electrons in bond donated by one atom | Moderate | Metal complexes, ions | NH₄⁺, [Fe(CN)₆]³⁻ |
| Secondary (Intermolecular) | **Hydrogen Bond** | Weak attraction between H and electronegative atom (N, O, F) | Weak | Polar molecules | H₂O···H₂O, DNA base pairs |
|  | **Dipole–Dipole** | Attraction between polar molecules | Weak | Polar molecules | HCl |
|  | **Van der Waals** | Weakest forces from temporary dipoles | Very Weak | All atoms/molecules | Noble gases, alkanes |
| Specialized Covalent | **Peptide Bond** | Covalent bond linking amino acids in proteins | Strong | Amino acids | Proteins |
|  | **Disulfide Bond** | Covalent bond between two sulfur atoms (stabilizes protein structure) | Moderate | Sulfhydryl groups (–SH) | Insulin |

## Bonding Theories

> …
>
- [VSEPR theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/VSEPR_theory), a model of molecular geometry.
- [Valence bond theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Valence_bond_theory), which describes molecular electronic structure with localized bonds and lone pairs.
- [Molecular orbital theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Molecular_orbital_theory), which describes molecular electronic structure with delocalized molecular orbitals.
- [Crystal field theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crystal_field_theory), an electrostatic model for transition metal complexes.
- [Ligand field theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ligand_field_theory), the application of molecular orbital theory to transition metal complexes.
