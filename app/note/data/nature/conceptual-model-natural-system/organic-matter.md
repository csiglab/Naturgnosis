# Organic Matter

> …
>

| **Category** | **Description** | **Key Types** | **Functions** | **Examples** |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Carbohydrates** | Organic molecules composed of carbon, hydrogen, and oxygen, often in a 1:2:1 ratio. | Monosaccharides, disaccharides, polysaccharides | Provide energy, store energy, and serve as structural components. | Glucose, sucrose, starch, cellulose |
| **Proteins** | Large biomolecules made of amino acids linked by peptide bonds. | Enzymes, structural proteins, antibodies | Catalyze biochemical reactions, provide structure, and support immune function. | Hemoglobin, collagen, enzymes (e.g., amylase, DNA polymerase) |
| **Lipids** | Hydrophobic organic molecules, often containing fatty acids or related structures. | Fatty acids, triglycerides, phospholipids, sterols | Store energy, provide insulation, form cell membranes. | Fats, oils, phospholipids, cholesterol, steroids |
| **Nucleic Acids** | Organic molecules that store and transfer genetic information. | DNA, RNA | Store genetic information, transmit hereditary data, synthesize proteins. | DNA (Deoxyribonucleic acid), RNA (Ribonucleic acid), ATP |
| **Vitamins** | Organic compounds essential for normal growth and nutrition, often acting as coenzymes. | Water-soluble vitamins, fat-soluble vitamins | Regulate metabolism, enhance enzyme activity, support immune function. | Vitamin C, Vitamin A, Vitamin D, Vitamin B12 |
| **Hormones** | Organic compounds that act as chemical messengers in organisms. | Steroid hormones, peptide hormones, amines | Regulate physiological processes such as growth, metabolism, and reproduction. | Insulin, estrogen, testosterone, adrenaline |
| **Alkaloids** | Nitrogen-containing organic compounds derived from plants. | Morphine, caffeine, nicotine, quinine | Often have medicinal or psychoactive effects. | Caffeine, morphine, nicotine, quinine |
| **Terpenes** | Organic compounds produced by plants, characterized by repeating units of isoprene. | Monoterpenes, diterpenes, sesquiterpenes | Serve as defense mechanisms, attract pollinators, and provide aroma. | Menthol, limonene, camphor, turpentine |
| **Flavonoids** | Polyphenolic organic compounds found in plants, often with antioxidant properties. | Anthocyanins, flavonols, flavones | Antioxidant activity, UV protection, and signaling in plants. | Quercetin, anthocyanin, catechins (found in tea) |
| **Polysaccharides** | Long chains of monosaccharides used for energy storage and structure. | Starch, glycogen, cellulose | Energy storage, structural support (in plants and animals). | Starch (in plants), glycogen (in animals), cellulose (in plants) |
| **Amino Acids** | Organic compounds that are the building blocks of proteins. | Essential, non-essential amino acids | Protein synthesis, neurotransmitter production, metabolism. | Glycine, alanine, lysine, tryptophan |
| **Peptides** | Short chains of amino acids linked by peptide bonds. | Di-peptides, tri-peptides, oligopeptides | Act as hormones, neurotransmitters, or antimicrobial agents. | Glutathione, oxytocin, insulin |
| **Polyphenols** | Organic compounds with antioxidant properties, often found in plant-based foods. | Tannins, flavonoids, lignans | Antioxidant, anti-inflammatory, and protective in plants. | Resveratrol, curcumin, catechins, tannins |
| **Chlorophylls** | Green pigments found in plants that are crucial for photosynthesis. | Chlorophyll a, chlorophyll b | Absorb light energy for photosynthesis, provide green color. | Chlorophyll a, chlorophyll b |
| **Pigments** | Organic compounds responsible for coloration in organisms. | Carotenoids, anthocyanins, chlorophylls | Provide coloration for attraction, protection, and photosynthesis. | Beta-carotene, lycopene, anthocyanins |
| **Glycoproteins** | Proteins that have carbohydrate groups covalently attached. | Mucins, antibodies, membrane proteins | Cell signaling, immune responses, and cell-cell communication. | Erythropoietin, mucins, integrins |
