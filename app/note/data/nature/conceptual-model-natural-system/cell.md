# Cell

> A cell is the basic structural and functional unit of all living organisms, capable of performing essential life processes such as metabolism, growth, and reproduction.
>

> **Cell programming** refers to the processes by which cells control and regulate their functions, development, and behavior through genetic and biochemical mechanisms, often in response to environmental signals.
>

> All cells come from other cells. New cells are made through a process called **cell division**. During cell division, one cell grows and splits into two.
>

> A cell is a living unit; therefore, a multicellular organism is composed of many living units.\
>

> Cell parts are called organells → functions.
>

## Cell Structure

![Note. Incomplete Model of an Animal Cell.](/note/data/nature/conceptual-model-natural-system/cell/image.png)

Note. Incomplete Model of an Animal Cell.

| **Cell Structure** | **Description** | **Function** | **Biomolecules Involved** |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Cell Membrane (Plasma Membrane)** | A phospholipid bilayer with embedded proteins. | Regulates the movement of substances in and out of the cell, maintaining homeostasis. | **Phospholipids**, **Proteins** (e.g., receptor proteins, ion channels, transporters), **Cholesterol** |
| **Cytoplasm** | A gel-like substance inside the cell, excluding the nucleus. | Supports and suspends organelles; facilitates molecular transport. | **Water**, **Proteins**, **RNA**, **Ions**, **Carbohydrates** |
| **Nucleus** | Membrane-bound organelle containing DNA. | Stores genetic material (DNA) and coordinates cell activities such as growth and reproduction. | **DNA**, **RNA**, **Proteins** (e.g., histones, transcription factors) |
| **Nuclear Membrane (Envelope)** | Double-layered membrane surrounding the nucleus. | Protects the nucleus and regulates the exchange of materials between the nucleus and cytoplasm. | **Lipids** (phospholipids), **Proteins** (nuclear pore complexes) |
| **Nucleolus** | Dense region inside the nucleus composed of RNA and proteins. | Assembles ribosomal RNA (rRNA) and ribosomal subunits. | **rRNA**, **Proteins** (e.g., ribosomal proteins) |
| **Chromatin** | Uncoiled DNA and proteins inside the nucleus. | Contains the genetic blueprint for protein synthesis and cell division. | **DNA**, **Histones**, **Non-histone Proteins** |
| **Mitochondria** | Double-membraned organelles with their own DNA. | Produce ATP through cellular respiration; the "powerhouse" of the cell. | **Lipids** (phospholipids, cardiolipin), **Proteins** (e.g., ATP Synthase), **DNA**, **RNA** |
| **Endoplasmic Reticulum (ER)** | Membranous network within the cytoplasm. | **Rough ER**: synthesizes proteins.  **Smooth ER**: synthesizes lipids and detoxifies chemicals. | **Lipids**, **Proteins** (e.g., enzymes, ribosomes on rough ER) |
| **Golgi Apparatus** | Stack of membrane-bound vesicles. | Modifies, sorts, and packages proteins and lipids for secretion or delivery to other organelles. | **Lipids**, **Proteins** (e.g., glycosyltransferases, clathrin) |
| **Ribosomes** | Small complexes of RNA and protein found free in the cytoplasm or attached to the rough ER. | Synthesize proteins based on mRNA instructions. | **rRNA**, **Proteins** (e.g., ribosomal proteins, tRNA) |
| **Lysosomes** | Membrane-bound organelles containing digestive enzymes. | Break down waste materials, old cell components, and foreign invaders (e.g., bacteria). | **Proteins** (e.g., hydrolases, proteases, nucleases) |
| **Peroxisomes** | Membrane-bound organelles containing oxidative enzymes. | Detoxify harmful substances and break down fatty acids and hydrogen peroxide. | **Proteins** (e.g., catalase, oxidases), **Lipids** |
| **Vacuoles** | Large membrane-bound sacs. | Store nutrients, waste products, and maintain turgor pressure (especially in plant cells). | **Water**, **Proteins**, **Carbohydrates**, **Ions** |
| **Cytoskeleton** | Network of protein filaments (microtubules, microfilaments, intermediate filaments). | Provides structural support, aids in cell shape, movement, and division. | **Actin**, **Tubulin**, **Keratin**, **Vimentin**, **Dynamin** |
| **Centrioles** | Cylindrical structures composed of microtubules. | Play a key role in organizing the microtubules during cell division (mitosis). | **Tubulin** (specifically, tubulin dimers), **Proteins** (e.g., centrin) |
| **Chloroplasts (in plant cells)** | Membrane-bound organelles containing chlorophyll. | Carry out photosynthesis, converting sunlight into chemical energy in the form of glucose. | **Chlorophyll**, **Proteins** (e.g., photosystem proteins, rubisco), **Lipids** |
| **Cell Wall (in plant, fungal, and some prokaryotic cells)** | Rigid outer layer composed of cellulose (in plants). | Provides structural support, protection, and shape to the cell. | **Cellulose** (in plants), **Chitin** (in fungi), **Peptidoglycan** (in bacteria) |
| **Plasmodesmata (in plant cells)** | Channels that connect plant cells. | Allow for communication and transport of substances between plant cells. | **Proteins** (e.g., desmotubule proteins), **RNA**, **Carbohydrates** |
| **Flagella and Cilia** | Long and short hair-like structures extending from the cell membrane. | Facilitate movement of the cell or substances along the surface of the cell (e.g., mucus in humans). | **Tubulin** (microtubules), **Dynein**, **Kinesin**, **Proteins** (e.g., nexin) |

## Plant Cell

> Plant Cell vs Animal Cell.
>

![image.png](/note/data/nature/conceptual-model-natural-system/cell/image-1.png)

| **Feature** | **Plant Cell** | **Animal Cell** |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Cell Wall** | Present (made of cellulose) | Absent |
| **Shape** | Usually rectangular or box-like | Usually round or irregular |
| **Chloroplasts** | Present (for photosynthesis) | Absent |
| **Vacuole** | Large central vacuole | Small, temporary vacuoles (if any) |
| **Plastids** | Present (e.g. chloroplasts, chromoplasts, leucoplasts) | Absent |
| **Lysosomes** | Rare or absent | Present |
| **Centrioles** | Absent in most | Present |
| **Energy Storage** | Starch | Glycogen |
| **Mode of Nutrition** | Autotrophic (makes its own food) | Heterotrophic (ingests food) |
| **Cytokinesis** | Cell plate formation | Cleavage furrow formation |
