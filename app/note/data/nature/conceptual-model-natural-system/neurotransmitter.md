# Neurotransmitter

> A **neurotransmitter** is a chemical messenger that transmits signals across a synapse from one neuron (nerve cell) to another target cell, which can be another neuron, muscle cell, or gland cell. Neurotransmitters are released from the axon terminal of a neuron in response to an electrical signal and bind to specific receptors on the target cell, triggering a physiological response.
>

> They play a key role in communication within the nervous system, regulating processes like mood, movement, cognition, and many bodily functions.

## Taxonomy

| **Neurotransmitter** | **Type** | **Primary Function(s)** | **Common Effects / Roles** |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Acetylcholine (ACh)** | Excitatory / Inhibitory | Muscle activation; memory; autonomic nervous system | Muscle contraction, learning, attention |
| **Dopamine** | Excitatory | Reward, motivation, motor control | Pleasure, motor coordination, mood regulation |
| **Serotonin (5-HT)** | Mostly inhibitory | Mood regulation, appetite, sleep | Mood stabilization, appetite control, sleep cycles |
| **Gamma-Aminobutyric Acid (GABA)** | Inhibitory | Reduces neuronal excitability | Calming effect, anxiety reduction |
| **Glutamate** | Excitatory | Main excitatory neurotransmitter in the brain | Learning, memory, neural plasticity |
| **Norepinephrine (Noradrenaline)** | Excitatory | Fight or flight response; attention | Alertness, arousal, stress response |
| **Epinephrine (Adrenaline)** | Excitatory | Similar to norepinephrine; hormone and neurotransmitter | Stress response, heart rate increase |
| **Histamine** | Excitatory | Wakefulness, appetite control | Regulation of arousal, immune response |
| **Endorphins** | Peptide (Neuromodulator) | Pain relief, reward | Natural painkillers, euphoria |
| **Substance P** | Peptide | Pain perception | Transmission of pain signals |
| **Glycine** | Inhibitory | Inhibitory neurotransmitter in spinal cord | Motor and sensory signal modulation |
| **Nitric Oxide (NO)** | Gasotransmitter | Modulation of synaptic plasticity | Vasodilation, learning, and memory |
