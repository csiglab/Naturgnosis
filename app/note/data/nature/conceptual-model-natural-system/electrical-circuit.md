# Electrical Circuit

> Note: This concept is interesting both for the Science and Praxis.
>

> An electrical circuit is a **hybrid ontological entity**: it is simultaneously **material, relational, functional, and dynamic**. Its “being” is neither purely in the components nor purely in the currents—it emerges from **the organized flow of energy and information through a structured network**.
>

## Index

## Formulation

> What characterizes some network as a circuit?
>

| **Aspect** | **Description** |
| --- | --- |
| **Closed Loop Structure** | The network contains at least one continuous loop, allowing electric charge to flow from the source, through components, and back to the source. |
| **Energy Source** | Presence of a voltage or current source that drives the flow of charge through the network. |
| **Component Connectivity** | All components are properly connected in the loop, ensuring interaction and functional relationships. |
| **Conductive Path** | Paths must be uninterrupted; no breaks exist that would prevent current flow. |
| **Functional Emergence** | The network exhibits emergent electrical behavior, such as voltage drops, current flow, and energy transfer, as a result of the structured arrangement of components. |
| **Graph-Theoretic Property** | In formal terms, the network contains at least one closed cycle (loop) that includes the source and essential components. |

## QA

### When is a close loop needed to move charge particles?

> A **closed loop is needed to move charged particles** whenever you want a **sustained, continuous flow of charge**, i.e., an **electric current**.
>

| Case | Closed Loop Needed? | Reason |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Steady current in a conductor (DC or AC)** | ✅ Yes | Charges need a complete path to flow continuously back to the source. Without a loop, the current stops after local redistribution. |
| **Battery-powered circuit** | ✅ Yes | Electrons flow from the negative terminal to the positive terminal; a complete loop allows continuous current. |
| **Charging a capacitor** | ❌ Not strictly | Charges move locally onto the plates, but the current in the external wire stops once the capacitor is fully charged. |
| **Single charge in an electric field (vacuum or open space)** | ❌ No | The particle moves under the force of the field without needing to return to the source. |
| **Transient displacement currents (changing electric fields)** | ❌ No | Movement is local and temporary; no closed path is needed for the short-term motion of charges. |

### What is the concept of ground?

> A reference point in an electrical circuit against which voltages are measured. Often considered to have **zero electric potential**.
>

> Provides a stable voltage reference and a safe path for excess charge to flow, preventing damage or hazards.
>

Type:

- **Earth Ground:** Physically connected to the earth (e.g., grounding rod).
- **Chassis or Signal Ground:** Circuit reference point, not necessarily connected to earth.

### Why doesn’t current flow through a bird that touches a single wire of an electric fence?

> **Current = Voltage Difference ÷ Resistance**. If the voltage difference across the bird’s body is zero, the current is zero, so the bird is unharmed.
>

> If the bird touches **two wires at different voltages** (or a wire and the ground), it would complete a circuit, and current could flow — then it would get shocked.
>
