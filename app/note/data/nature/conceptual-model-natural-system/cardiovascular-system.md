# Cardiovascular System

> The **cardiovascular system** is a closed-loop system of the **heart** and **blood vessels** that **circulates blood** to deliver essential substances to body tissues and remove waste products.
>

## Index

## 🧩 **Components**

| Component | Description |
| --- | --- |
| **Heart** | Muscular organ that pumps blood through rhythmic contractions. |
| **Arteries** | Carry oxygen-rich blood **away** from the heart to the tissues. |
| **Veins** | Carry oxygen-poor blood **back** to the heart. |
| **Capillaries** | Microscopic vessels where exchange of gases, nutrients, and wastes occurs. |
| **Blood** | The transport fluid containing red and white cells, platelets, and plasma. |
| **Lymphatic System** (connected) | Drains fluid, filters pathogens, and returns it to the blood system. |

## 🔁 **Subdivisions**

| Circuit | Function |
| --- | --- |
| **Systemic Circulation** | Heart → Body → Heart (delivers oxygen, picks up waste) |
| **Pulmonary Circulation** | Heart → Lungs → Heart (gas exchange: CO₂ out, O₂ in) |

## 🌀 **Main Functions**

- 🫁 **Gas Transport** (O₂ and CO₂ exchange between lungs and tissues)
- 🍽️ **Nutrient Delivery** (from digestive system to cells)
- 🧪 **Waste Removal** (to kidneys, liver, lungs for excretion)
- 💬 **Hormone Distribution** (e.g. insulin, adrenaline)
- 🛡️ **Immune System Support** (via white blood cells)
- 🌡️ **Thermoregulation** (body temperature control)

## 🌀 **How It Works**

- The **heart** pumps oxygen-rich blood via **arteries** to body tissues.
- After delivering oxygen and picking up carbon dioxide, **veins** return the oxygen-poor blood to the heart.
- The heart sends it to the **lungs**, where gas exchange occurs.
- **Capillaries** are the tiny vessels where most exchange of materials happens.

## 📦 Blood Is the Carrier

Think of blood as the **transport medium** in a logistic system:

- Oxygen = energy supply
- Nutrients = raw materials
- Hormones = messages
- Wastes = garbage to be removed

## Is blood circulation in direct contact with every cell in the body?

> **Not every cell in the body is directly “touched” or in direct contact with blood circulation.**
>

> Blood **does not flow directly through most cells**.
>

> It circulates within vessels, and cells receive what they need **via diffusion from capillaries through surrounding fluids**.
>

How it works:

- **Blood flows inside blood vessels** — arteries, veins, and especially tiny capillaries.
- **Capillaries** are the smallest blood vessels and are extremely thin-walled (just one cell thick). They are where exchange of gases, nutrients, and wastes happens.
- Most body cells are **very close to capillaries** (within about 10–20 micrometers) so that oxygen and nutrients can **diffuse** from the blood through the interstitial fluid to the cells.
- However, **cells are separated from the blood itself by vessel walls** and the extracellular (interstitial) fluid.

Exceptions & special cases:

- In some tissues like **cartilage** or the **lens of the eye**, there is no direct blood supply; cells get nutrients by diffusion from nearby blood vessels or fluids.
- **Bone marrow** and some immune organs have specialized vasculature.
- **Certain cells, like red blood cells and endothelial cells, are directly in the blood or vessel walls**, but most cells rely on diffusion.
