# Combustion

> **Combustion** is a **chemical reaction** between a **fuel** and an **oxidant**, typically oxygen, that releases energy in the form of heat and light.
>

> What is fuel?  …
>

## How is the combustion reaction triggered?

> The **combustion reaction** is triggered when a **fuel** is exposed to sufficient heat **(activation energy)** in the presence of an **oxidizing agent**, typically oxygen. This **initial heat** breaks **chemical bonds** in the fuel, releasing volatile gases that react with oxygen to form new compounds, such as carbon dioxide and water, while releasing energy in the form of heat and light.
>

## Models

> Here is a table summarizing the different models of the combustion process:
>

| **Model Type** | **Description** | **Examples** |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Chemical Kinetics Models** | Focus on detailed chemical reactions and reaction rates of fuel and oxidizer. | Arrhenius-based models, detailed reaction mechanisms for hydrocarbons. |
| **Thermodynamic Models** | Analyze combustion using energy and mass conservation laws under equilibrium conditions. | Constant volume or constant pressure combustion models. |
| **Fluid Dynamics Models** | Examine gas and flame flow using Navier-Stokes equations. | Laminar flame models, turbulent flame models. |
| **Zero-Dimensional Models** | Simplify combustion to lumped parameters with uniform properties in the system. | Heat release models for engines. |
| **One-Dimensional Models** | Represent combustion along a single spatial dimension, often for flame propagation analysis. | Premixed flame models. |
| **Multi-Dimensional Models** | Simulate combustion phenomena in two or three dimensions. | Computational Fluid Dynamics (CFD) models for engines and burners. |
| **Phenomenological Models** | Use empirical relationships to describe combustion characteristics. | Diesel spray combustion models. |
| **Simplified Combustion Models** | Use a single-step reaction to approximate the combustion process. | Global reaction mechanisms for hydrocarbon fuels. |
