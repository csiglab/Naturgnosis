# Hormone

> **Signaling bio- molecules** that regulate physiological processes and communication between cells.
>

> First study by  https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arnold_Adolph_Berthold.
>

## **Hormone Characterization**

> **Produced by Glands or Cells**:  Hormones are typically secreted by specialized glands (like the thyroid, pituitary, adrenal glands) or cells that are part of endocrine systems. These glands or cells are the sources from which hormones are released into the bloodstream (or other body fluids).
>

> **Hormones** can be proteins, peptides, lipids (e.g., steroid hormones), or even small molecules like amines (e.g., adrenaline). Protein and peptide hormones are made up of amino acids, while steroid hormones are derived from cholesterol.
>

> **Hormones act** by binding to specific receptors on the surface of cells (for protein and peptide hormones) or within the cell (for steroid and thyroid hormones). This interaction triggers a change in the target cell’s activity, such as altering metabolic processes, gene expression, or cell function.
>

|  | Thyroxine | Influences metabolism and growth. |
|  | Cortisol | Regulates the body’s response to stress. |
|  | Adrenaline (Epinephrine) | Prepares the body for fight or flight responses. |
| **Microorganisms** | Quorum-sensing molecules | Coordinate group behavior like biofilm formation in bacteria. |

## List of Human Hormones

> …
>

| **Hormone** | **Gland of Origin** | **Primary Function** |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Insulin** | Pancreas | Regulates blood glucose levels and helps with nutrient storage. |
| **Thyroxine (T4)** | Thyroid | Regulates metabolism, growth, and development. |
| **Cortisol** | Adrenal Glands | Regulates metabolism, stress responses, and inflammation. |
| **Adrenaline (Epinephrine)** | Adrenal Glands | Prepares the body for fight-or-flight response, increases heart rate and blood flow. |
| **Growth Hormone** | Pituitary Gland | Stimulates growth, cell reproduction, and regeneration. |
| **Estrogen** | Ovaries (also adrenal glands) | Regulates female reproductive system, menstrual cycle, and secondary sexual characteristics. |
| **Testosterone** | Testes | Regulates male reproductive functions, muscle mass, and secondary sexual characteristics. |
| **Progesterone** | Ovaries | Prepares the uterus for pregnancy and regulates the menstrual cycle. |
| **Prolactin** | Pituitary Gland | Stimulates milk production in females. |
| **Oxytocin** | Pituitary Gland | Stimulates uterine contractions during childbirth and milk release during breastfeeding. |
| **Thyroid-Stimulating Hormone (TSH)** | Pituitary Gland | Stimulates the thyroid to release thyroid hormones (T3 and T4). |
| **Follicle-Stimulating Hormone (FSH)** | Pituitary Gland | Stimulates the growth of ovarian follicles in females and sperm production in males. |
| **Luteinizing Hormone (LH)** | Pituitary Gland | Triggers ovulation in females and testosterone production in males. |
| **Aldosterone** | Adrenal Glands | Regulates sodium and potassium balance, affects blood pressure. |
| **Melatonin** | Pineal Gland | Regulates sleep-wake cycles and circadian rhythm. |
