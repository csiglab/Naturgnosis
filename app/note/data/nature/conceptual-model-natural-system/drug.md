# Drug

> Abstract Class.
>

> A **drug** is a pharmaceutical product composed of active principles (molecules) that elicit a biological effect in a living organism.
>

### **Action Mechanism (How drugs interact with a biological system)**

> aka. **Pharmacodynamics**
>

Drugs interact with the human body by binding to specific molecular targets (e.g., receptors, enzymes, ion channels, DNA) to produce physiological effects. The key mechanisms include:

- **Receptor Binding**: Agonists (activate receptors) vs. antagonists (block receptors).
- **Enzyme Inhibition/Activation**: Altering biochemical pathways (e.g., statins inhibiting HMG-CoA reductase).
- **Ion Channel Modulation**: Affecting nerve impulses (e.g., local anesthetics blocking sodium channels).
- **DNA/RNA Interference**: Chemotherapy drugs disrupting cancer cell replication.
- **Physical Actions**: Osmotic diuretics (e.g., mannitol) or antacids neutralizing stomach acid.

### **What Drugs Can Do**

- **Alter Physiological Functions**: Pain relief (analgesics), reduce inflammation (NSAIDs), lower blood pressure (antihypertensives).
- **Treat Diseases**: Antibiotics kill bacteria, antivirals inhibit viral replication.
- **Modify Mood/Cognition**: Antidepressants (SSRIs), stimulants (ADHD medications).
- **Replace Deficiencies**: Insulin for diabetes, thyroid hormones for hypothyroidism.
- **Prevent Conditions**: Vaccines, prophylactic antimalarials.

### **What Drugs Can’t Do**

- **Cure All Diseases**: Some conditions (e.g., Alzheimer’s, advanced cancers) lack definitive cures.
- **Work Equally for Everyone**: Genetic differences (pharmacogenomics) affect drug response.
- **Fix Underlying Lifestyle Issues**: Drugs may manage symptoms (e.g., hypertension) but not replace diet/exercise.
- **Act Instantly**: Some drugs require weeks for full effect (e.g., SSRIs).
- **Be Completely Safe**: All drugs have potential side effects or risks (e.g., addiction with opioids).
