# Fundamental Molecular Interaction

> A **fundamental molecular interaction** is the force of attraction or repulsion between molecules that determines their physical and chemical behavior.
>

| **Category** | **Interaction** | **Description** | **Examples** |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Intramolecular** | **Covalent Bonds** | Sharing of electrons between atoms to form molecules. Strong and directional. | H₂ (hydrogen molecule), H₂O (water molecule), CH₄ (methane). |
|  | **Ionic Bonds** | Electrostatic attraction between oppositely charged ions. Strong and non-directional. | NaCl (sodium chloride), MgO (magnesium oxide). |
|  | **Metallic Bonds** | Attraction between metal ions and a "sea" of delocalized electrons. Strong and non-directional. | Fe (iron), Cu (copper), Al (aluminum). |
| **Intermolecular** | **Van der Waals Forces** | Weak forces arising from temporary dipoles in nonpolar molecules. | Noble gases (e.g., Ar), nonpolar molecules (e.g., CH₄, CO₂). |
|  | **London Dispersion Forces** | A type of Van der Waals force caused by temporary dipoles due to electron motion. | All molecules, but significant in nonpolar molecules (e.g., He, N₂, I₂). |
|  | **Dipole-Dipole Forces** | Attraction between permanent dipoles in polar molecules. | Polar molecules (e.g., HCl, H₂O, NH₃). |
|  | **Hydrogen Bonds** | Strong dipole-dipole interaction between H and highly electronegative atoms (N, O, F). | Water (H₂O), DNA base pairs, proteins. |
|  | **Ion-Dipole Forces** | Attraction between an ion and a polar molecule. | Salt dissolved in water (e.g., Na⁺ and Cl⁻ ions interacting with H₂O). |
|  | **Hydrophobic Interactions** | Nonpolar molecules clustering in water to minimize contact with polar water molecules. | Oil droplets in water, protein folding. |
|  | **π-π Stacking** | Attraction between aromatic rings due to overlapping π-electron clouds. | DNA base stacking, graphene layers. |
|  | **Ion-Induced Dipole Forces** | Attraction between an ion and a nonpolar molecule, inducing a temporary dipole. | Ion interacting with a nonpolar molecule (e.g., O₂ in water). |
|  | **Dipole-Induced Dipole Forces** | Attraction between a polar molecule and a nonpolar molecule, inducing a temporary dipole. | Polar molecule (e.g., HCl) interacting with a nonpolar molecule (e.g., Ar). |
