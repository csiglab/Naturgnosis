# Electromagnetic System

> An **electromagnetic system** is a physical system in which **electric** and **magnetic fields** interact with matter and energy, enabling the transmission, conversion, or control of electrical signals, forces, or radiation based on the principles of electromagnetism.

## Electric Field

The **electric field** $\vec{E}$ is a **vector field** that represents the **force per unit charge** experienced by a small **test charge** placed at a point in space.

$\vec{E}(\vec{r}) = \frac{\vec{F}}{q}$

Where:

- $\vec{E}$: electric field at position $\vec{r}$
- $\vec{F}$: force experienced by test charge
- $q$: magnitude of test charge (assumed small so it doesn't disturb the source)

### 🔢 **Units**

- SI Units: **Newtons per Coulomb (N/C)** or **Volts per meter (V/m)**

### ⚙️ **Sources of Electric Field**

- **Point charge**:  $\vec{E} = \frac{1}{4\pi\epsilon_0} \cdot \frac{q}{r^2} \hat{r}$
- **Charge distributions**: use integration over volume, surface, or line charge
- **Time-varying magnetic fields**: from Faraday’s Law (electromagnetic induction)

### 🧲 **Mathematical Properties**

| Property | Description |
| --- | --- |
| **Vector field** | Has both magnitude and direction at each point in space |
| **Linear superposition** | Fields from multiple charges **add vectorially** |
| **Conservative (electrostatics)** | Can be written as $\vec{E} = -\nabla V$, where $V$ is electric potential |
| **Divergence** | $\nabla \cdot \vec{E} = \frac{\rho}{\epsilon_0}$ (Gauss's Law) |
| **Curl (static case)** | $\nabla \times \vec{E} = 0$ for electrostatics |

### 🧪 **Physical Interpretation**

- Tells **how a charge will accelerate** in space
- **Direction** of $\vec{E}$ = direction a **positive** charge would move
- **Strength** of $\vec{E}$ = how strong the force is

### 🔋 **Relation to Potential**

$\vec{E} = -\nabla V$

- The electric field is the **negative gradient** of the electric potential $V$
- Field lines point from **high to low potential**

## Magnetic Field

The **magnetic field** $\vec{B}$ is a **vector field** that represents the **force per unit current and per unit length** or the **force on a moving charge** due to magnetism.

$\vec{F} = q \vec{v} \times \vec{B}$

Where:

- $\vec{B}$: magnetic field at a point
- $q$: charge
- $\vec{v}$: velocity of the charge
- $\vec{F}$: resulting magnetic force

### 🔢 **Units**

- SI Units: **Tesla (T)**

### ⚙️ **Sources of Magnetic Field**

| **Source Type** | **Mathematical Law** | **Typical Use Cases** |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Steady Current (Wire, Loop)** | **Biot–Savart Law** | Coils, solenoids, magnetic dipoles |
| **General Current Density** | **Maxwell–Ampère Law** | Circuits, changing fields, full electromagnetism |
| **Time-Varying Electric Field** | **Displacement Current Term** | Capacitors, EM waves, antenna radiation |
| **Permanent Magnets (microscopically)** | Modeled as current loops (effective $\vec{J}$) | Magnetic materials, dipole fields |

### 🧲 **Mathematical Properties**

| Property | Description |
| --- | --- |
| **Vector field** | Has magnitude and direction at each point |
| **Not conservative** | Cannot be written as a gradient of a scalar |
| **Field lines form loops** | No beginning or end: ∇⋅B⃗=0\nabla \cdot \vec{B} = 0 |
| **Superposition applies** | Fields from multiple sources add vectorially |
| **Generated by currents or changing electric fields** | Described by Ampère’s Law and Faraday’s Law |

### 🧪 **Physical Interpretation**

- Describes **how magnetic forces act** on **moving charges** or **currents**
- **Direction of $\vec{B}$**: defined by the **right-hand rule** around current
- **Strength** of $\vec{B}$: proportional to current and inversely to distance

### 📜 **Maxwell’s Equations (Static Magnetism)**

- **Gauss’s Law for Magnetism**: $\nabla \cdot \vec{B} = 0 \quad \text{(no magnetic monopoles)}$
- **Ampère’s Law (with no displacement current)**:  $\nabla \times \vec{B} = \mu_0 \vec{J}$

### ⚖️ **Relation to Electric Field**

In **electromagnetism**, $\vec{B}$ and $\vec{E}$ are intertwined:

- Changing $\vec{E}$ fields induce $\vec{B}$, and vice versa
- In **relativity**, $\vec{E}$ and $\vec{B}$ are components of the same electromagnetic field tensor

## Case Study

| **Case Study** | **Domain** | **System Type** | **Description** |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Earth’s Magnetosphere** | Physical (Natural) | Open System | Shields Earth from solar wind; driven by Earth’s magnetic field and solar plasma. |
| **Aurora Borealis (Northern Lights)** | Physical (Natural) | Open System | Charged particles from solar wind interact with Earth's magnetic field and atmosphere, emitting light. |
| **Lightning Discharge** | Physical (Natural) | Transient System | Rapid electrostatic discharge caused by charge separation in storm clouds. |
| **Sun’s Electromagnetic Radiation** | Physical (Natural) | Radiative System | Continuous emission of EM radiation across the spectrum due to nuclear fusion. |
| **Synapse in Nervous System** | Physical (Bioelectrical) | Coupled EM-Chemical System | Electric signals propagate through neurons via ion exchange and field potentials. |
| **Electric Motor** | Engineering | Electromechanical System | Converts electrical energy into mechanical motion using magnetic fields. |
| **Transformer** | Engineering | Static EM System | Transfers AC electrical energy between circuits through electromagnetic induction. |
| **Wireless Communication System** | Engineering | Radiative EM System | Transmits information using modulated electromagnetic waves through space. |
| **MRI Machine (Medical Imaging)** | Engineering | Controlled Magnetic System | Uses strong magnetic fields and RF pulses to generate images of internal body structures. |
| **Radar System** | Engineering | Active Sensing System | Emits EM waves and analyzes reflected signals to detect objects and measure distance or speed. |
