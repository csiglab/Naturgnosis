# Interaction

> **Interaction**:  A process by which two or more particles or systems affect each other.
>

> A **fundamental interaction** in the **deepest sense** refers to the most basic and irreducible forces that govern the behavior of all matter and energy in the universe.
>

QA:

- What hold things together?
- How large structure are build by smaller ones?
- How  large systems are build by smaller components?

## Taxonomy of Interactions

> …
>

| **Category** | **Type** | **Description** | **Relative Strength** | **Range** | **Mediator/Mechanism** | **Examples** |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Fundamental Interactions** | **Gravitational Force** | Attraction between masses. | $10^{-39}$ | Infinite | Graviton (hypothetical) | Planets orbiting the Sun, objects falling on Earth. |
|  | **Electromagnetic Force** | Interaction between charged particles. | $10^{-2}$ | Infinite | Photon | Chemical bonds, light, electricity, magnetism. |
|  | **Weak Nuclear Force** | Responsible for radioactive decay and particle transformations. | $10^{-13}$ | \(10^{-18}\) m | W and Z bosons | Beta decay, neutrino interactions. |
|  | **Strong Nuclear Force** | Binds protons and neutrons in atomic nuclei. | 1 | \(10^{-15}\) m | Gluons | Holding nuclei together, nuclear reactions. |
| **Quantum Mechanical Interactions** | **Quantum Tunneling** | Particles passing through energy barriers they classically shouldn’t. | Variable | N/A | Wavefunction overlap | Nuclear fusion in stars, semiconductor devices. |
|  | **Exchange Interactions** | Forces arising from the exchange of virtual particles (e.g., photons, gluons). | Variable | N/A | Virtual particles | Covalent bonds, magnetic interactions. |
|  | **Spin-Spin Coupling** | Interaction between particle spins. | Weak to Moderate | N/A | Magnetic fields | NMR spectroscopy, magnetic materials. |
|  | **Quantum Entanglement** | Correlation between particles regardless of distance. | N/A | Infinite | Quantum state correlation | Quantum computing, EPR paradox. |
| **Intermolecular Forces** | **Van der Waals Forces** | Weak forces arising from temporary dipoles. | Very Weak $1-10 kJ/mol$ | Short-range | Temporary dipoles | Noble gases (e.g., Ar), nonpolar molecules (e.g., CH₄). |
|  | **London Dispersion Forces** | A type of Van der Waals force caused by temporary dipoles due to electron motion. | Very Weak $0.1-5 kJ/mol$ | Short-range | Electron motion | All molecules, but significant in nonpolar molecules (e.g., He, N₂, I₂). |
|  | **Dipole-Dipole Forces** | Attraction between permanent dipoles in polar molecules. | Weak to Moderate (5-20 kJ/mol) | Short-range | Permanent dipoles | Polar molecules (e.g., HCl, H₂O, NH₃). |
|  | **Hydrogen Bonds** | Strong dipole-dipole interaction between H and highly electronegative atoms (N, O, F). | Moderate to Strong (10-40 kJ/mol) | Short-range | H and electronegative atoms | Water (H₂O), DNA base pairs, proteins. |
|  | **Ion-Dipole Forces** | Attraction between an ion and a polar molecule. | Moderate to Strong (40-600 kJ/mol) | Short-range | Ion and dipole | Salt dissolved in water (e.g., Na⁺ and Cl⁻ ions interacting with H₂O). |
| **Intramolecular Forces** | **Covalent Bonds** | Sharing of electrons between atoms. | Very Strong (200-1000 kJ/mol) | Short-range | Electron sharing | Molecules like H₂, O₂, CH₄. |
|  | **Ionic Bonds** | Electrostatic attraction between oppositely charged ions. | Very Strong (600-1000 kJ/mol) | Short-range | Electrostatic attraction | Ionic compounds (e.g., NaCl, MgO). |
|  | **Metallic Bonds** | Attraction between metal ions and delocalized electrons. | Strong (100-500 kJ/mol) | Short-range | Delocalized electrons | Metals (e.g., Fe, Cu, Al). |
| **Emergent and Collective Interactions** | **Superconductivity** | Zero electrical resistance due to electron pairing in certain materials. | Strong (depends on material) | Macroscopic | Electron pairs | Superconductors at low temperatures. |
|  | **Ferromagnetism** | Alignment of magnetic moments in materials, leading to permanent magnetism. | Strong (depends on material) | Macroscopic | Magnetic domains | Iron, cobalt, nickel. |
|  | **Surface Tension** | Cohesive forces between liquid molecules at the surface. | Weak to Moderate | Macroscopic | Cohesive forces | Water droplets, capillary action. |
|  | **Hydrodynamic Forces** | Forces in fluids due to motion and viscosity. | Variable | Macroscopic | Fluid flow | Fluid flow, drag forces, turbulence. |
|  | **Plasma Interactions** | Interactions in ionized gases (plasmas). | Variable | Macroscopic | Charged particles | Stars, fusion reactors, auroras. |
| **Biological Interactions** | **Enzyme-Substrate Binding** | Specific interactions between enzymes and their substrates. | Moderate to Strong | Molecular | Lock-and-key mechanism | Catalysis in biochemical reactions. |
|  | **Antigen-Antibody Binding** | Specific interactions between antigens and antibodies in the immune system. | Strong | Molecular | Molecular recognition | Immune response, vaccine development. |
|  | **DNA-Protein Interactions** | Binding of proteins to DNA for regulation and replication. | Moderate to Strong | Molecular | Molecular recognition | Transcription factors, DNA repair. |
|  | **Cell-Cell Interactions** | Interactions between cells in tissues and organs. | Variable | Cellular | Signaling molecules | Immune response, tissue formation. |
| **Cosmological Interactions** | **Dark Matter Interactions** | Interactions involving dark matter, which does not emit light. | Unknown | Cosmological | Gravitational effects | Galaxy rotation curves, gravitational lensing. |
|  | **Dark Energy Effects** | Repulsive force causing the accelerated expansion of the universe. | Unknown | Cosmological | Unknown | Cosmic acceleration, large-scale structure of the universe. |
|  | **Cosmic Inflation** | Rapid expansion of the universe shortly after the Big Bang. | N/A | Cosmological | Inflaton field (hypothetical) | Early universe, cosmic microwave background. |
| **Specialized and Niche Interactions** | **Casimir Effect** | Attraction between two uncharged, parallel plates due to quantum fluctuations. | Very Weak (depends on distance) | Nanoscale | Quantum fluctuations | Nanoscale systems, quantum field theory. |
|  | **Topological Interactions** | Interactions in topological materials (e.g., topological insulators). | Variable | Nanoscale | Topological states | Quantum computing, exotic materials. |
|  | **Quantum Field Theory Interactions** | Interactions described by quantum field theory (e.g., Feynman diagrams). | Variable | Subatomic | Virtual particles | Particle physics, quantum electrodynamics. |
| **Human-Created Interactions** | **Artificial Intelligence** | Interactions in AI systems (e.g., neural networks, reinforcement learning). | N/A | Macroscopic | Algorithms | Machine learning, robotics. |
|  | **Social Interactions** | Interactions between individuals or groups in societies. | N/A | Macroscopic | Communication | Economics, politics, culture. |

## Interaction’s Outcomes

> …
>

| **Outcome** | **Interaction** | **Description** | **Example** |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Attraction** | **Gravitational** | Objects with mass are drawn toward each other. | Planets orbiting the Sun. |
|  | **Electromagnetic** | Opposite charges attract. | Electrons attracted to protons in an atom. |
| **Repulsion** | **Electromagnetic** | Like charges repel. | Two electrons repelling each other. |
|  | **Strong Nuclear** | Quarks experience repulsion at very short distances. | Quark confinement inside protons and neutrons. |
| **Binding** | **Gravitational** | Objects form stable structures due to gravitational attraction. | Solar systems, galaxies, black holes. |
|  | **Electromagnetic** | Particles form stable structures due to electromagnetic forces. | Electrons bound to nuclei in atoms, atoms forming molecules. |
|  | **Strong Nuclear** | Quarks are bound together to form protons and neutrons. | Protons and neutrons in atomic nuclei. |
|  | **Biological** | Molecules form stable complexes due to specific interactions. | Enzyme-substrate binding, DNA-protein interactions. |
| **Orbital Motion** | **Gravitational** | Objects move in stable orbits around a central mass. | Earth orbiting the Sun. |
| **Scattering** | **Electromagnetic** | Particles change direction or energy after interaction. | Photons scattering off electrons (Compton scattering). |
|  | **Weak Nuclear** | Particles interact and change direction or energy. | Neutrinos scattering off atomic nuclei. |
|  | **Strong Nuclear** | High-energy quarks and gluons scatter in particle collisions. | Quark-gluon plasma in particle accelerators. |
| **Transformation** | **Electromagnetic** | Particles change into other particles or states. | Electron-positron annihilation producing photons. |
|  | **Weak Nuclear** | Particles decay or change into other particles. | Neutron decaying into a proton, electron, and antineutrino (beta decay). |
|  | **Strong Nuclear** | Quarks and gluons transform into hadrons. | High-energy collisions producing protons, neutrons, and mesons. |
|  | **Cosmological** | Cosmic structures evolve over time. | Formation of stars, galaxies, and black holes. |
| **Oscillation** | **Weak Nuclear** | Particles switch between different states. | Neutrino oscillations between flavors (e.g., electron, muon, tau neutrinos). |
| **Turbulence** | **Hydrodynamic** | Chaotic fluid flow due to interactions between fluid layers. | Turbulent airflow around an airplane wing. |
| **Viscous Drag** | **Hydrodynamic** | Resistance to motion due to fluid viscosity. | Objects moving through water or air. |
| **Wave Propagation** | **Hydrodynamic** | Energy transfer through waves in a fluid. | Ocean waves, sound waves in air. |
| **Phase Transitions** | **Thermodynamic** | Systems change from one state of matter to another. | Water boiling into steam, ice melting into water. |
| **Heat Transfer** | **Thermodynamic** | Energy transfer due to temperature differences. | Heat flowing from a hot object to a cold one. |
| **Equilibrium** | **Thermodynamic** | Systems reach a state of balance with no net change. | Gas reaching uniform temperature in a container. |
| **Signaling** | **Biological** | Cells communicate through molecular interactions. | Hormones binding to receptors, nerve impulses. |
| **Self-Organization** | **Biological** | Systems spontaneously organize into ordered structures. | Formation of tissues, organs, and organisms. |
| **Expansion** | **Cosmological** | The universe expands due to dark energy. | Accelerated expansion of the universe. |
