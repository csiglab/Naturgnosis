# Vacuum

> A **vacuum** is a region of spacetime characterized by a **particle number density** $n \approx 0$, corresponding to a **low thermodynamic pressure**  P ≪  $P_{\text{atm}}$, where the mean free path of residual gas particles is significantly longer than the characteristic dimensions of the system.

## Free Fall

> A **free fall in a vacuum model** describes the motion of a body under the influence of gravity alone, without any air resistance or other forces acting on it. It is one of the simplest and most foundational models in classical mechanics.
>

**Key Assumptions**

1. **No air resistance** (i.e., motion occurs in a vacuum).
2. **Only gravity acts on the object.**
3. **Uniform gravitational field** (typically valid near the surface of a planet).
4. **Mass of the object does not affect the motion** (in vacuum, all objects fall at the same rate).

**Mathematical Model (Near Earth's Surface)**

Let:

- $g$ = gravitational acceleration (approximately 9.8  $\text{m/s}^2$ near Earth's surface),
- $t$ = time in seconds,
- $v(t$) = velocity at time $t$,
- $y(t)$ = vertical position at time $t$,
- $y_0$ = initial vertical position,
- $v_0$ = initial velocity.

Then:

**Equations of Motion**

1. **Velocity as a function of time** $v(t) = v_0 - g t$
2. **Position as a function of time**  $y(t) = y_0 + v_0 t - \frac{1}{2} g t^2$

**Special Cases**

- If dropped from rest: $v_0 = 0$

    $v(t) = -g t,\quad y(t) = y_0 - \frac{1}{2} g t^2$

- Time to reach ground (if dropped from height $h$):

    Solve $y(t) = 0 \Rightarrow 0 = h - \frac{1}{2} g t^2$

    $t = \sqrt{\frac{2h}{g}}$

## Galileo’s Conceptual Experiment

> Galileo Galilei's **conceptual experiment** on free-falling objects was a critical turning point in the development of physics, challenging the Aristotelian view that heavier objects fall faster than lighter ones.
>

**The Traditional (Aristotelian) View**

Before Galileo, the dominant belief—based on Aristotle—was:

> "Heavier bodies fall faster than lighter ones in proportion to their weight."
>

This was **not based on systematic experimentation**, but on intuition and everyday experience (which is influenced by air resistance).

**Galileo’s Conceptual Refutation**

Around 1590, Galileo proposed a **thought experiment** (not necessarily performed physically at first) to expose a **logical contradiction** in Aristotle's view:

> Galileo’s Thought Experiment:
>
>
> Imagine two objects, one heavy (A) and one light (B), connected by a string.
>
> - According to Aristotle, A should fall faster than B.
> - But if you tie A and B together:
>     - B should slow A down → the combination should fall **slower** than A alone.
>     - But now A + B is **heavier** than A → the combination should fall **faster** than A.
>
> 💡 **Contradiction!**
>
> This paradox shows that the Aristotelian theory is **internally inconsistent**.
>

**Galileo’s Conclusion**

All objects, regardless of mass, should fall at the same rate in the absence of air resistance.

> Galilean Principle of Free Fall:
>
>
> > “In the absence of air resistance, all objects fall with the same constant acceleration due to gravity.”
> >

**Later Empirical Confirmation**

- **Leaning Tower of Pisa**: Legend says Galileo dropped two spheres of different mass from the tower and observed they hit the ground at the same time. While it is debated whether this experiment was physically performed, it aligns with his writings.
- **Apollo 15**: Astronaut David Scott dropped a hammer and a feather on the Moon. In the vacuum of the lunar surface, both hit the ground simultaneously, visually confirming Galileo’s principle.
