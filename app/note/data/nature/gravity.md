# Gravity

> **Gravity** is the warping of spacetime caused by mass-energy, guiding the motion of objects and the flow of time.
>

> **Objects** in a gravitational field are not being pulled by a force in the traditional sense — instead, they are following natural paths (called geodesics) through the warped spacetime.
>

> **Massive bodies** (like planets, stars, and black holes) create "indentations" or "wells" in the fabric of spacetime. Smaller objects move according to the curves and slopes created by this warping, appearing to us as if they are "falling" toward the massive object.
>

> Even light, which is massless, follows the curved structure of spacetime, causing effects like gravitational lensing.
>

> **Gravity** also affects the passage of time: in stronger gravitational fields, time flows more slowly compared to regions of weaker gravity (gravitational time dilation).
>

> On large scales, gravity organizes the structure of the universe: it drives the formation of stars, galaxies, galaxy clusters, and it governs the expansion dynamics of the cosmos itself.
>

## Formulation

> Gravity is the manifestation of the non-Euclidean (curved) geometry of a four-dimensional Lorentzian manifold (spacetime) whose metric tensor $νg_{\mu\nu}$ satisfies the Einstein field equations, with the energy-momentum tensor $T_{\mu\nu}$ acting as the source of curvature. Objects in free fall locally move along timelike geodesics of the spacetime manifold, minimizing (or extremizing) the spacetime interval $ds^2$ determined by $νg_{\mu\nu}$.
>
