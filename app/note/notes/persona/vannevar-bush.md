# Vannevar Bush


Vannevar Bush was a seminal American engineer, inventor, and science administrator, known for his profound influence on the direction of scientific research in the United States during and after World War II. Here are some of his main works and contributions:

1. **"As We May Think"**: This seminal essay, published in "The Atlantic Monthly" in 1945, speculated on the future of information storage and retrieval, proposing a machine called the "Memex." The Memex was envisioned as a mechanical desk that could store books, records, and communications, and it's notably prescient of modern-day personal computers and the internet.
2. **The Manhattan Project**: Although not a direct creator, Bush was instrumental in the administration and funding of the Manhattan Project through his role as the head of the Office of Scientific Research and Development (OSRD). He coordinated the efforts of military, industrial, and scientific communities during WWII.
3. **OSRD (Office of Scientific Research and Development)**: Bush directed this office, which was responsible for the majority of wartime scientific research efforts, including the development of radar and other critical military technologies. The OSRD effectively connected the scientific community with the military needs of the U.S. during the war.
4. **The National Science Foundation (NSF)**: Following WWII, Bush authored a report titled "Science, The Endless Frontier," which led to the creation of the National Science Foundation. This report laid the groundwork for post-war science policies and emphasized the importance of federal funding for scientific research, advocating for science as a key to improving public health, national security, and economic prosperity.
5. **Early Computers and Digital Networking Concepts**: Beyond the Memex, Bush's vision and technical expertise influenced the development of early computing and digital networking concepts. His ideas foresaw many aspects of information society, including the structure of what would become the internet.

Vannevar Bush's work significantly shaped modern scientific research and development policies, and his vision for technology foresaw the information age decades before it came to fruition.
