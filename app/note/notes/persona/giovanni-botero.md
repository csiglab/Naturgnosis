# Giovanni Botero


> **Giovanni Botero** was an **Italian** philosopher, priest, and diplomat known for his work in political theory and economics during the late 16th and early 17th centuries.
> 

> His most famous work, "Della ragion di Stato" (The Reason of State), laid the groundwork for many statecraft and political economy ideas. Here is a table summarizing some of his key ideas:
> 

| Idea | Description |
| --- | --- |
| **Reason of State** | Botero introduced the concept of the "Reason of State" concept, emphasizing that the state must act pragmatically to ensure its survival and prosperity, sometimes beyond moral or ethical considerations. |
| **Population Growth and State Power** | He argued that a state's power and wealth are directly linked to population size and advocated for policies encouraging population growth. |
| **Economic Policy** | Botero stressed the importance of economic strength for the stability and power of the state. He believed in fostering trade, industry, and agriculture. |
| **Religion and Statecraft** | While recognizing the importance of religion, Botero argued that state interests should occasionally take precedence over religious considerations to maintain order and stability. |
| **Moral Philosophy** | Despite advocating for pragmatism in state affairs, Botero did not wholly abandon moral philosophy and believed rulers should still strive for justice and ethical governance where possible. |
| **Balance of Power** | He suggested that states should maintain a balance of power internally among various social classes and externally with other states to prevent any single entity from becoming too dominant. |
| **Urbanization** | Botero highlighted the significance of cities as centers of economic activity and cultural development, suggesting that their growth was vital for a state's prosperity. |
| **War and Diplomacy** | He emphasized the strategic use of war and diplomacy, advocating for a combination of both to expand and protect state interests. |
| **National Unity** | Botero believed in the importance of national unity and the consolidation of power within a central authority to manage and grow the state effectively. |
| **Role of the Prince** | In his view, the prince or ruler should be wise, prudent, and strategic, embodying the ideals of both a just leader and a wise statesman. |
