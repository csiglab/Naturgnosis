# Sigismund Schlomo Freud


> The issue is not **Freud** himself—his work may be an example of bad science—but his followers should know better and treat his views not as a religion, but as an explanatory framework that must be tested.
> 

## Research

> Which were Freud main research problems?
> 

| **Area** | **Research Questions** |
| --- | --- |
| **Unconscious Processes** | - How much of mental life occurs outside awareness?  
- What mechanisms govern unconscious thought?  
- How do unconscious processes influence conscious behavior and emotions?  
- How can unconscious content be revealed and studied? |
| **Instincts and Drives** | - How do sexual (libidinal) and aggressive drives shape mental life? 
 - How are instinctual desires satisfied, repressed, or redirected? 
 - What is the role of drives in motivating behavior and forming personality? |
| **Mental Functioning Dynamics** | - How do the Pleasure Principle and Reality Principle regulate thought and action?
- How does the mind manage tension between instinctual desires and external constraints?
- What mechanisms underlie repression, resistance, and defense? |
| **Personality Development** | - How do early childhood experiences shape adult personality?
- What is the impact of psychosexual stages (oral, anal, phallic, latency, genital) on behavior?
- How do family dynamics and parental relationships influence mental and emotional development? |
| **Psychopathology** | - What psychic conflicts produce anxiety, hysteria, or neuroses?
- How does repression generate psychological symptoms?
- What are the roles of defense mechanisms in protecting the ego?
- How do early traumas or unresolved conflicts affect later mental health? |
| **Dreams and Symbolism** | - How do dreams reveal unconscious desires?
- What processes (condensation, displacement, symbolization) transform latent thoughts into manifest content?
- Can dreams provide insight into unresolved conflicts or repressed material? |
| **Psychoanalytic Methods** | - How can free association uncover unconscious material?
- What is the significance of slips of the tongue, jokes, and parapraxes?
- How can clinical observation be systematized into a general theory of the mind?
- How can psychoanalytic techniques be validated scientifically? |
| **Mental Energy and Motivation** | - How is psychic energy (libido) distributed and transformed?
- What is the relationship between energy, tension, and behavior?
- How do mental processes conserve or expend energy in different contexts? |
| **Interpersonal and Social Influences** | - How do social interactions and relationships shape personality?
- What is the role of cultural norms in regulating instinctual drives?
- How do authority, attachment, and family structure influence mental development? |

## Model

> Which **models** does Freud used in his works?
> 

| **Target System** | **Model** | **Description** |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Human Mind | **Topographical Model** | Divides the mind into **conscious, preconscious, and unconscious** regions, describing how thoughts and memories exist at different levels of awareness. |
| Human Mind | **Structural Model** | Divides the psyche into **id, ego, and superego**, explaining interactions between instinctual drives, reality mediation, and moral conscience. |
| Mental Functioning | **Principle-Based Model** | Uses **Pleasure Principle** and **Reality Principle** to describe how mental processes seek satisfaction of drives and how they adapt to external constraints. |
| Psychological Development | **Psychosexual Development Model** | Stages of development (**oral, anal, phallic, latency, genital**) that describe how early experiences shape personality and mental structure. |
| Dreams | **Dream-Work Model** | Explains how the **unconscious transforms latent thoughts into manifest dream content**, using processes like condensation, displacement, and symbolization. |

## Terminology

> Which were the terms used - in Freud Works? Which were the terms relied on upon?
> 

| **Term** | **Definition** | **Uses in Freud Theory** | **Tags** |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Psyche (Psyquis) | The totality of the mind | The overarching system studied; the “mind” as an energy-driven structure | Foundational, Conceptual |
| Conscious | Mental content currently in awareness | Ego functioning; rational thought, perception, and voluntary action | Structural, Functional |
| Preconscious | Thoughts/memories not currently conscious but retrievable | Mediates between unconscious impulses and conscious awareness | Structural, Functional |
| Unconscious | Reservoir of desires, impulses, memories outside awareness | Central driver of behavior, dreams, slips, neuroses | Structural, Foundational |
| Instinct (Trieb) | Basic drives motivating behavior (sexual, aggressive) | Explains behavior, neuroses, development, mental energy | Foundational, Motivational |
| Libido | Psychic energy associated with life instincts | Drives behavior, cathexis, creativity, and sexual desire | Motivational, Energy |
| Thanatos / Death Drive | Hypothetical drive toward aggression, destruction, or return to inorganic state | Explains aggression, repetition compulsion, destructive behavior | Motivational, Energy |
| Energy (Psychic Energy) | Conceptual force flowing through mental processes | Movement, displacement, cathexis of desires; foundation for dynamic model of mind | Conceptual, Motivational |
| Tension / Cathexis | Investment of psychic energy into ideas, objects, or actions | Explains focus of drives, attachment, and repression | Functional, Motivational |
| Id | Instinctual, pleasure-seeking part of the psyche | Source of impulses; operates via Pleasure Principle | Structural, Functional |
| Ego | Reality-oriented mediator | Balances id, superego, and reality; governed by Reality Principle | Structural, Functional |
| Superego | Moral and ethical part of psyche | Internalizes social norms; regulates behavior; generates guilt | Structural, Functional |
| Pleasure Principle | Tendency toward immediate gratification | Governs id, primary processes, and early mental life | Functional, Guiding Principle |
| Reality Principle | Tendency to delay gratification according to reality | Governs ego, secondary processes, and adaptive functioning | Functional, Guiding Principle |
| Repression | Mechanism pushing unacceptable impulses out of consciousness | Protects ego from anxiety; central in neuroses | Defense Mechanism, Functional |
| Defense Mechanism | Strategies employed by ego to manage conflict | Includes repression, projection, displacement, etc. | Functional, Adaptive |
| Primary Processes | Irrational, associative, dream-like thought | Id’s mode of functioning; basis of fantasy and dreams | Functional, Cognitive |
| Secondary Processes | Rational, organized thought | Ego’s mode; realistic planning, problem-solving | Functional, Cognitive |
| Free Association | Technique where patient verbalizes thoughts freely | Reveals unconscious content | Methodological, Clinical |
| Dream-Work | Transformation of latent content into manifest content | Tool for analyzing unconscious desires | Methodological, Clinical |
| Condensation | Combining multiple ideas into a single dream image | Part of dream-work mechanism | Dream Analysis, Methodological |
| Displacement | Transfer of emotional significance from important to trivial objects | Part of dream-work; defense mechanism | Dream Analysis, Methodological |
| Symbolization | Representation of repressed ideas in symbolic form | Dreams, slips, parapraxes | Dream Analysis, Methodological |
| Parapraxis (Slip) | Mistakes in speech, memory, or action revealing unconscious thoughts | Evidence of unconscious desires | Clinical, Methodological |
| Transference | Projection of feelings about others onto therapist | Central mechanism in psychoanalytic treatment | Clinical, Methodological |
| Resistance | Opposition to revealing unconscious material | Obstacle in therapy; reveals underlying conflict | Clinical, Methodological |
| Catharsis | Release of repressed emotions | Therapeutic goal in psychoanalysis | Clinical, Methodological |
| Neurosis | Mental disorder arising from unresolved unconscious conflict | Manifestation of repressed drives; target for therapy | Psychopathology, Clinical |
| Psychosexual Stages | Developmental stages: oral, anal, phallic, latency, genital | Explain personality formation and influence of early experience | Developmental, Functional |
