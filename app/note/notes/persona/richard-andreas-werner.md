# Richard Andreas Werner


> Richard Werner is a prominent economist known for his contributions to the field of banking and finance.
> 

Here are some main ideas associated with Richard Andreas Werner:

1. **Origination of the Term "Quantitative Easing"**: Werner is credited with coining the term "quantitative easing" in the early 1990s, describing the unconventional monetary policy used by the Bank of Japan to combat deflation.
2. **Banking and Financial Systems**: He has extensively researched and written about the role of banks in the economy, emphasizing their ability to create money through the process of credit creation.
3. **Monetary Policy**: Werner's work challenges conventional views on monetary policy, advocating for policies that prioritize credit creation by banks to stimulate economic activity over interest rate adjustments.
4. **Princes of the Yen**: He authored the book "Princes of the Yen," which delves into the workings of the Japanese financial system and the influence of central banks on economic policy.
5. **Research on Economic Development**: Werner's research extends to economic development, particularly focusing on the role of banking systems in fostering sustainable growth and development.
6. **Critique of Neoliberal Economics**: He is critical of mainstream neoliberal economic theories and policies, advocating for a more interventionist approach by central banks and governments to address economic challenges.
7. **Application of Economic Theory in Policy**: Werner's work often emphasizes the importance of applying economic theories in real-world policy contexts, particularly in addressing issues related to financial stability and economic growth.
8. **Scholarly Contributions**: Through his publications, lectures, and research, Richard Werner has significantly contributed to the understanding of banking, monetary policy, and economic development, challenging conventional wisdom and offering alternative perspectives.
