> An Ideology is a **structured set of mental representations** that shapes perception, evaluation, and behavior in a social or political context.
> 

> A socially and cognitively structured system of mental representations—including beliefs, values, norms, and heuristics—that shapes perception, evaluation, and behavior in political, social, and cultural contexts. While ideologies organize understanding and coordinate action, they are **not guaranteed to be epistemically accurate**; their normative, prescriptive, and socially stabilizing functions often **supersede truth-tracking**. Originating from Destutt de Tracy’s *idéologie* (“science of ideas”), the term has evolved historically to signify prescriptive frameworks rather than purely descriptive study.
> 

> Note: Ideology is an abuse of the suffix logy - which mean study of - this was introduced by the modern connotation.

## **Critical Notes**

1. **Etymological abuse:**
    - The suffix **“-logy”** properly denotes a **study or systematic inquiry**.
    - In “ideology,” it no longer refers to the study of ideas but rather **a prescriptive or normative framework**, which can be misleading.
2. **Epistemic caution:**
    - Treating an ideology as a valid cognitive framework can represent a **major epistemic failure mode**: it often focuses on **instruments, doctrines, or dogmas**, rather than **goals and reality**.
    - For example, in domains like morality or decision-making, one should prefer **precise normative constructs** (e.g., moral principles, ethical norms) rather than using the **broad, vague, and charged term “ideology.”**

**Implication:**

- Ideologies are **cognitively and socially potent**, but they can **obscure accurate reasoning and goal-oriented action**.
- Rigorous analysis favors **explicit principles and evaluative criteria** over abstract ideological frameworks.

### What does the term **Ideology denotes**?

**Ideology** denotes a **system of ideas, beliefs, and values** that:

- Provides a **framework for interpreting reality**.
- Guides **political, social, or cultural action**.
- Can **legitimize social structures, norms, and institutions**.
- Often functions as a **shared cognitive lens** within a group or society.

In short, an ideology is a **structured set of mental representations** that shapes perception, evaluation, and behavior in a social or political context.

## What is the origin of the term?

- The term **“ideology”** comes from French **idéologie**, coined in the **late 18th century** by **Antoine Destutt de Tracy**.
- It is formed from the Greek roots:
    - **ἰδέα (idéa)** → “idea”
    - **λογία (-logia)** → “study of” or “science”
- Originally, it meant **“the science of ideas”**—a systematic study of how ideas are formed and interrelated.
- Over time, especially in the 19th and 20th centuries, the meaning **shifted from a neutral study of ideas to denote a prescriptive or political set of beliefs**.
