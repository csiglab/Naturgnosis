> A **principle** is a fundamental truth, law, or assumption that serves as a foundation for a **system of beliefs, behaviors, or reasoning**.
> 

> Note: The word first principles is idiotic; because principles already means first.
> 

Key Question:

- What evidence support this `principle`?
- What is the nature of  `'principles'`? Principles are **fundamental statements about how reality works**. The aspects they capture may concern **structure, state, dynamics, constraints, or regularities** within a domain. They illuminate the **space of possible configurations** of reality, clarifying both its **scope and its limitations**.
    - Note: The term **principle** can also be used in **agentic domains**—such as problem solving—to denote **a normative guideline that directs action or decision-making**.

### Key aspects of a principle:

- **Fundamental:** It underlies more complex ideas or systems.
- **General:** Applies broadly across situations or fields.
- **Guiding:** Helps explain, predict, or regulate phenomena or actions.
- **Often foundational:** Used to build theories, methods, or practices.

## Origin of *Principle*

- Comes from **Latin** *principium*, meaning **“beginning,” “origin,” or “first part.”**
- *Principium* derives from *princeps* (genitive *principis*), meaning **“first,” “chief,” or “leader.”**
    - *Princeps* itself comes from *prim-* (meaning “first”) + *capere* (meaning “to take” or “to seize”).
- Entered **Old French** as *principe*, retaining the meaning of a fundamental rule or starting point.
- Adopted into **Middle English** as *principle*, carrying the meaning of a fundamental truth or rule.
