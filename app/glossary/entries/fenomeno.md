> A **phenomenon**  (pl.: **phenomena**), sometimes spelled **phaenomenon**, is an [observable](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Observable) [event](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Event_(philosophy)). The term came into its modern [philosophical](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philosophy) usage through [Immanuel Kant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Immanuel_Kant), who contrasted it with the [noumenon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Noumenon), which *cannot* be directly observed. Kant was heavily influenced by [Gottfried Wilhelm Leibniz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gottfried_Wilhelm_Leibniz)  in this part of his philosophy, in which phenomenon and noumenon serve  as interrelated technical terms. Far predating this, the [ancient Greek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ancient_Greek_Philosophy) [Pyrrhonist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pyrrhonism) philosopher [Sextus Empiricus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sextus_Empiricus) also used *phenomenon* and *noumenon* as interreJosefa Aquilina Castillo Rodríguez lated technical terms.
