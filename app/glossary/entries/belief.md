> The **technical definition of belief** varies by discipline (e.g., philosophy, cognitive science, AI), but here’s a general, cross-disciplinary definition:
> 

> Belief is a mental representation or propositional attitude in which an agent holds a proposition to be true, potentially with varying degrees of certainty, and which can guide perception, reasoning, and action.
> 

### Broken down:

- **Mental representation**: Belief is an internal state that represents how the world is or ought to be.
- **Propositional attitude**: It's a relation between an agent and a proposition (e.g., “Emilio believes that water boils at 100°C”).
- **Truth-ascription**: The agent treats the proposition as true, even if it may be false in reality.
- **Functional role**: Beliefs influence the agent's decisions, reasoning, behavior, and expectations.

### In different fields:

- **Philosophy** (esp. epistemology):
Belief is distinguished from knowledge and opinion. It's **necessary for knowledge**, but not sufficient (truth and justification are also needed).
- **Cognitive Science / Psychology**:
Belief is a **cognitive state** that helps model how agents (humans or machines) understand and predict the world.
- **Formal Logic / AI**:
Often formalized as part of a **belief-desire-intention (BDI)** architecture or using **modal logic**, where □Bφ means "the agent believes φ".
