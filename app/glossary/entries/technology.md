> **Technology**: The World That We **Have** → The World That We **Want**.
> 

> "'Tecnología' comes from the Greek word τέχνη (pronounced 'téchnē') and means art, craft, or skill, which we **commonly refer to as tools that enable us to perform specific tasks**.”
> 

> Technology is best understood as the *process* of **crystallizing human imagination** into **new realities**, rather than *just an outcome**.***
> 

$$

\begin{split}
&T: I \rightarrow O ;\\
&T(\text{technology process}): \text{Use human imagination to create a new reality;}  \\&I(\text{inputs}): \text{A problem to be solved; a set of tools;}  \\
&O(\text{outputs}): \text{Tangible or intangible solutions to a certain problem.}
\end{split}

$$

> Technology: Materials (Inputs) → Tools (New Objects).

## Space

> **"Phenomena"** generally refers to observable events, occurrences, or facts that can be perceived through the senses or detected through instruments.
> 

> **Nature encompasses** the entirety of the physical world, including living organisms, ecosystems, natural phenomena, and the underlying principles governing the universe.
> 

> **"Foundational technology"** typically refers to fundamental or core technologies that serve as a basis for the development of more advanced systems, applications, or innovations. These technologies often provide essential capabilities, infrastructure, or frameworks upon which other technologies and industries are built.
> 

> **Physical Technological Space**: The "Physical Technological Space" refers to the tangible and spatial domain where physical objects, devices, and technologies exist and interact, encompassing the physical environment shaped by technological artifacts and their real-world applications.
> 

> **Digital Technological Space**: The "Digital Technological Space" refers to the interconnected realm created by the digital infrastructure, encompassing networks, devices, and platforms, where information, communication, and computational processes occur within the digital domain.
>
