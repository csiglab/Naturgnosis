## Etymology

> Which is the etymology of ‘Category’ in Spanish?
> 

> The term derives from Ancient Greek **κατηγορία (katēgoría)**, itself from **κατηγορεῖν (katēgoreîn)**—“to accuse,” “to assert publicly,” or “to predicate”—a verb composed of **κατά (katá)** (“down,” “against,” “concerning”) and **ἀγορά (agorá)** (“public assembly,” “marketplace”), thus originally evoking the act of speaking or making a charge in the public forum.
> 

> [**Predication Type Hierarchy**] [**Epistemic Tool**] In philosophy—especially in Aristotle’s treatise Categories—the term evolved to mean a **fundamental mode of predication**, i.e., a way in which something can be said of a subject (substance, quantity, quality, relation, etc.).
> 

> In Spanish, **categoría** encapsulates a significant conceptual shift: from the original sense of **public accusation or assertion**, to **logical predication**—what may be affirmed of a subject—and ultimately to the modern meaning of a **class or division** under which entities are systematically grouped.
> 

## Connotation Space

> Which are  ideas that the term ‘Category’ evokes?
> 

| Connotation | Context | Definition (Operational Sense) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Classification | General / Epistemic | Systematic grouping of entities according to shared properties. |
| Conceptual Delimitation | Logic / Epistemology | Boundary-forming operator that determines inclusion and exclusion criteria. |
| Predication | Classical Philosophy (cf. Categories by Aristotle) | Fundamental mode under which something can be asserted of a subject. |
| Ontological Typing | Metaphysics | Basic kind of being or mode of existence (e.g., substance, quality, relation). |
| Hierarchy / Rank | Ordinary Language | Level, tier, or status within a stratified system. |
| Cognitive Schema | Cognitive Science | Mental structure used to compress and organize perceptual and conceptual information. |
| Structural Abstraction | Mathematics (Category theory) | Formal system defined by objects and morphisms, emphasizing relational structure over internal composition. |
