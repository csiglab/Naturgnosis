> Precision refers to the use of clear, specific, and unambiguous terms or measurements to convey information accurately. In language, it involves minimizing vagueness by choosing well-defined words and concepts. In models, precision ensures consistency of interpretation across contexts and reduces the risk of miscommunication or misapplication.
> 

### Example: Varying Levels of Precision and Understanding

Different contexts require different levels or types of precision. Consider the term **technology**:

- **Technology as end products**: Refers to tangible outputs like machines, software, or tools—**technical objects**.
- **Technology as technical knowledge**: Refers to the underlying know-how, processes, and principles—**technical knowledge**.

In a **user-centered setting**, the first definition (technology as products) may be more appropriate. However, in a **policy context**—such as when crafting **industrial policy**—the second, more nuanced understanding (technology as knowledge) becomes crucial.
