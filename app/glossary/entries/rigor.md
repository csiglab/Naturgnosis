> La palabra rigor viene del latín rigor, rigoris (rigidez, inflexibilidad, severidad, rigor y dirección recta de las cosas).
> 

> Comparte raiz con la palabra governar (regere).
> 

> **"Rigor"** means something slightly different depending on the context, but it always refers to **precision, thoroughness, and strict adherence to rules or principles**. Here's how it applies in different domains:
> 

## Index

### 🔢 **In Mathematics**

**Rigor** refers to:

- **Precise definitions** of all concepts.
- **Logically sound arguments**, where each step follows from definitions, axioms, or previously proven theorems.
- **Clarity about assumptions** and **no gaps** in reasoning.

### 📚 **In Philosophy**

Rigor means:

- Logical consistency in argumentation.
- Careful examination of assumptions.
- Clear definitions of terms.
- Systematic exploration of implications.

### 🧪 **In Science**

Scientific rigor means:

- Carefully controlled experiments.
- Reproducible methods.
- Transparent data analysis.
- Avoiding bias and unfounded conclusions.

### 📊 **In Social Sciences**

Rigor can be both:

- **Quantitative** (e.g., sound statistical design, valid measurements),
- and **Qualitative** (e.g., clear research protocols, careful coding of interviews, triangulation of data).

## Antonyms

> Here are antonyms (opposites) of **rigor**, tailored to different contexts:
> 

### 🔢 **In Mathematics**

- **Sloppiness** – careless logic or undefined concepts.
- **Hand-waving** – vague, informal explanations without proof.
- **Heuristic reasoning** – useful but not formally justified steps.

### 📚 **In Philosophy**

- **Vagueness** – unclear terms or ideas.
- **Incoherence** – contradictions or illogical reasoning.
- **Superficiality** – shallow analysis without depth or precision.

### 🧪 **In Science**

- **Anecdotal** – relying on personal stories instead of data.
- **Uncontrolled** – lacking experimental control or replication.
- **Speculative** – drawing conclusions without solid evidence.

### 📊 **In Social Sciences**

- **Methodological looseness** – unclear data collection or coding.
- **Cherry-picking** – selectively using data to support a claim.
- **Ambiguity** – unclear research questions or concepts.

### 🧠 **In General Use**

- **Carelessness**
- **Laxness**
- **Indiscipline**
- **Negligence**
- **Leniency** (in rules or enforcement)
