> Del griego  antiguo λόγος (lógos, "discurso, oración, cita, historia, estudio,  palabra, cálculo, razón"), de la raíz de λέγω (légō, "yo digo, yo pongo  en orden").
> 

> Logo: decir en griego.
> 

## **Etymology**

> **"La ciencia de la razón"** o **"el estudio del pensamiento estructurado"**.
> 

> In ancient philosophy, particularly with **Aristotle**, *logos* was used to describe the principle of rational thought and reasoning. Aristotle's work on syllogistic logic laid the foundation for the development of formal logic.
> 

> The word *λόγος* (logos) in Greek means "word," "speech," "discourse," or "reason." It can also be interpreted as "principle," "account," or "reasoning."
> 

> The term *logos* was adopted into Latin as *logica* to mean "the study of reasoning" or "science of reasoning," which led to the modern term *logic*.
> 

## Logos

> The word **λόγος** (*lógos*) in Ancient Greek is very rich and multi-layered. It comes from the verb **λέγω** (*légō*, “to gather, to pick, to say, to recount”), and its meanings range widely depending on context.
> 

> From *gathering* → *choosing* → *putting in order* → *recounting in words* → *reasoned account* → *principle.*
> 

### **Core Meanings**

1. **Word / Speech / Discourse**
    - Any spoken utterance, statement, conversation, or narrative.
    - Example: *ὁ τοῦ φιλοσόφου λόγος* – “the philosopher’s discourse.”
2. **Reason / Rationality**
    - The faculty of reasoning, the structure of thought.
    - In philosophy, it often means the capacity for rational thought distinguishing humans from animals.
3. **Account / Explanation**
    - A reasoned argument, justification, or explanation.
    - Example: *δίδωμι λόγον* – “to give an account.”
4. **Principle / Ratio / Measure**
    - In mathematics: proportion, relation, or ratio.
    - In metaphysics: a structuring principle or formula.
5. **Law-like Structure / Order**
    - Especially in the Stoics and later Neoplatonists, **λόγος** meant the rational principle that orders the cosmos.
    - Hence, it could mean "the universal reason."

### **Philosophical Uses**

- **Heraclitus (6th–5th c. BCE):** *λόγος* is the underlying order of the cosmos, a rational principle that unifies opposites.
- **Sophists & Rhetoricians:** speech, persuasive argument, rhetoric.
- **Plato & Aristotle:** both as "speech/argument" and as "rational principle" (e.g., Aristotle uses *λόγος* as “reason” in humans).
- **Stoics:** the *λόγος* is the divine rationality immanent in the universe (sometimes *λόγος σπερματικός*, the generative principle of the cosmos).
- **Christianity (later, in Greek):** the Gospel of John begins *Ἐν ἀρχῇ ἦν ὁ Λόγος* — “In the beginning was the Word (Logos).” Here *λόγος* means divine Word, reason, and ordering principle, identified with Christ.
